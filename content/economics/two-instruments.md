*Note, 29 August 2026 — Drew Griffiths.*

Four papers now sit alongside each other, forming two instruments. A constitutional model for a recurring charge on the holding of concentrated assets, with its stress-testing companion. A constitutional architecture for a UK sovereign endowment, with its evidential companion. They address adjacent constraints, and they are offered together for that reason and no other.

This note exists to say what each establishes, what none of them establishes, and what external work would settle the questions they leave open. It makes no new claim. It is short because its purpose is to prevent an inference, not to add one.

## What each paper answers

**[The Persistent Asset Charge](/economics/persistent-asset-charge/)** asks whether additional fiscal capacity can be raised from a particular tax base without waiting for a realisation event. Its answer is that a charge on the holding of concentrated assets can be imposed on every liable person above a threshold, by reference to a continuously maintained standing value, removing realisation-based avoidance at its root and closing the escape into legal form wherever beneficial ownership is attributable. Offshore opacity is a disclosed residual limit that no domestic design closes. The [companion pass](/economics/persistent-asset-charge-stress-test/) tests that model adversarially and reports that it produces substantial recurring revenue, that it does not clear the full-replacement ceiling the pass set itself, and that its revenue and its human-rights durability are coupled, so that pushing either the rate or the interaction rule toward more revenue increases the model's most exposed legal risk.

**[The Arithmetic of Patience](/economics/the-arithmetic-of-patience/)** asks whether sustained fiscal capacity can be converted into a stronger long-term public balance sheet. Its answer is that the income objective fails at any plausible contribution path and the balance sheet objective survives on the evidence tested, that the source of finance determines which of the two is available, and that historical survival is not a forecast probability.

**[The Long Asset](/economics/the-long-asset/)** asks what institution could hold such an asset across political and fiscal cycles long enough for the arithmetic to matter. Its answer is a design, offered conditionally: if the balance sheet proposition fails in the companion, the institution described should not be created.

## What none of them establishes

**That the two combine into one mechanism.** Nothing in any of them shows that revenue raised by the charge should fund the endowment, or that doing so would produce the effects each paper reports separately. That interaction has not been modelled and is not claimed. The papers are adjacent, not assembled.

**That any of them should be adopted.** Each is a design study. The charge paper says plainly that it does not argue wealth ought to be taxed and that its contribution is the mechanism rather than the politics. The endowment pair says the economic proposition deserves examination, not that the institution should be built.

**That the evidence behind them is settled.** The historical findings rest on a single published academic compilation, reproducible from the specification given but not independently corroborated. The charge model's base rests on survey data that under-captures wealth at the top, and the circularity in that estimate is disclosed rather than resolved.

## What would settle the rest

Three pieces of external work, each answerable by a different body, none requiring anyone to endorse anything.

**Fiscal classification.** Whether an endowment of this kind would receive the classification described in section 5A of *The Long Asset*. The paper answers the framework question from ONS material and from the treatment now applied to comparable institutions; what remains is confirmation for this specific object. A question for the Office for Budget Responsibility or the Treasury.

**Counsel's opinion.** Whether the reciprocal-purchase election survives Article 1 of Protocol 1 and Article 6 as the architecture now specifies it, and whether the entry threshold's discontinuity survives the excessive-burden test. A case-law reading can frame these; it cannot settle them.

**A government costing.** What the charge would raise on taxpayer-level data, including the entity layer, which the public data cannot size. Only HMRC can produce this, and the stress-testing pass says so rather than estimating around it.

## A note on how these papers are written

Each separates what follows from its own principles, what is a legislative choice, and what only evidence can settle, and labels which is which. Where a finding has been corrected after publication, the correction is stated in the paper rather than made silently, and the superseded position is retained in the project's record. Where a claim could not be sourced, it has been narrowed or removed rather than softened.

That discipline is the reason these papers can be offered together without the offering itself becoming a claim. They address related constraints. Whether they belong in one architecture is a further question, and it is not one any of them has answered.
