> **What this is.** A DM-facing companion to module I6, covering everything below the castle: the Larders of Ill Omen, the Dungeon and Catacomb, the forty crypts, the four tombs, the climax and the optional ending. **It is designed to be opened cold.** Each encounter carries the statistics, the mechanics that fire and the consequences.
>
> **What to keep to hand.** **These guides provide the rules needed for I6's encounters, but they don't replace the AD&D First Edition Players Handbook, Dungeon Masters Guide or Monster Manual.** **Keep them available for player spells, class abilities, equipment and actions the adventure doesn't anticipate.** **If you use Unearthed Arcana, consult it separately; the guides use the original core rules and don't incorporate its changes.**
>
> **The companion Playability Guide.** **The multi-part Ravenloft I6 Playability Guide explores how to prepare and run the adventure as a coherent story, developing its setting, characters, motivations, choices and consequences.** **These three Encounter Guides organise the same adventure by location, putting encounter statistics, rules, triggers and immediate consequences where the DM needs them during play.** **Both are designed to be usable independently, but they serve different purposes and complement one another.**

> **Labels and suggestions, used throughout.** **(printed)** is a fact on a page of I6 or an AD&D First Edition rulebook, cited. **(ruling)** is this guide's decision where those books leave a gap. **(interpretation)** is this guide's reading of printed text that can be read more than one way. **Optional material the DM may use or ignore is marked as a guide suggestion.**
>
> **Combat.** **To-hit and saving throw numbers are given at each encounter where a fight is likely.** **For anything else, section 9k carries the attack matrices, saving throws, item saving throws and the rules for zero hit points.**

> **Where this guide joins the others.** **Guide I** covers the approach, the village and Barovia. **Guide II** covers Tser Pool, the road and the castle above ground, K1 to K60. **Four structures cross the boundary** and are filed at their own keys in Guide II, cross-referenced here where the route passes.

## 1. Before you start

### 1a. What this guide covers

**Larders of Ill Omen** K61 to K72. **Dungeon and Catacomb** K73 to K83a. **The Catacombs** K84 and the forty crypts. **The four tombs** K85 to K88. **The confrontation with Strahd and its possible outcomes**, and **the optional ending**. **Treasures and Tomes**, the four placed items.

### 1b. Three of Strahd's eight places are here, and this is where he dies

**Printed, I6 p.11 and the Fortunes of Ravenloft, p.4.** The Fortunes reading decides where Strahd can always be found, and where the Tome, the Holy Symbol and the Sun Sword lie. **The DM draws it before play, and it can be replaced during play by the reading at Madam Eva's tent, G1, in Guide II, section 2c.** **A party that accepted a reading there is running on those results and not on the prep results**, so **establish which reading is in force before using anything in this guide that depends on it.**

**Three of the eight places are in this guide**: **K85 Sergei's tomb**, **K86 his own tomb** and **K88 the tomb of Barov and Ravenovia**. **Five are in Guide II.** **Four keyed areas here print the instruction to check the Fortunes results**: K85, K86, K88 and the crypts (printed, I6 pp.30, 28).

> **Read "can always be found" with the conditions that govern it, which are at 1c.** The phrase is the Fortunes' own wording and it is true of the list of eight. **It is not an instruction that he is in any of these rooms now.**
- **The reading names one location of the eight.** At the other two here his presence is not established by the Fortunes. **Consult the record rather than rolling again, and do not assume presence because a room is a possible location.**
- **Below ground daylight does not help the party, which is the reverse of Guide II** (1c).
- **His designated location, his actual whereabouts and his recovery state are three separate things** and are recorded separately (9f).

**But the coffin is always here.** **K86 is where his body lies if he has been reduced to 0 hit points within the previous 8 game hours** (printed, I6 p.30).

> **That does not mean the confrontation must end here.** **Five of his eight Fortunes locations are above ground, in Guide II**, and **he can be destroyed anywhere by any of the printed methods**: sunlight, running water, or stake and decapitation while he is held. **K86 matters because it is where his forced recovery happens**, which gives a party that drove him off a place to finish the work. **A party that destroys him outright in the Study never comes down here at all.**

### 1c. Conditions that hold below the castle

**Water.** **K73 to K76 are flooded.** The water in K73 and K74 is **5 feet deep**; K76's is **3 feet deep**. Movement, missile fire and anything dropped are all affected, and **I6 prints no swimming rule**, so the DM applies the ordinary encumbrance and movement judgements (ruling).

**Fog.** **It clings at knee height in the catacombs and obscures the floor** (printed, I6 p.27, in K84's own description). **At K62 it obscures everything less than 3 feet above the floor.** **The stone door of Crypt 7 lies flat on the floor hidden by it.**

**Light.** **Almost nothing down here is lit.** The exceptions are printed: **K62's lantern on the floor, K65's blazing fire, and K78's brazier.** Everything else is the party's own light. A torch lights a 40-foot radius (printed, I6 p.2).

**Pursuit, and the three rooms that stop it (printed, I6 p.27).** **"Once out of their crypts, the creatures in K84 will pursue the PCs anywhere, except into rooms K85, K86, and K87."** **That is the printed sanctuary rule and it is the most useful tactical fact in the guide.** A party being chased by what it woke in the catacombs can break pursuit by reaching a tomb.

**Daylight does not help a party down here, and that is the reversal to grasp before the crypts.** **Above ground the established ruling keeps him from acting by day, which is why Guide II's five locations find him absent.** **Below ground the Monster Manual's exception applies: far beneath the surface he need not rest in a coffin at all, and by day he is far beneath the surface (ruling).** **So daylight neither removes him from an underground room nor confines him to his coffin, and descending in daylight escapes his above-ground activity and may bring the party into his refuge instead.**
- **Which reading governs placement was settled at G1** (Guide II, 2c), **and a party that arrived here without passing the tent is running on the prep results.**

**The way up.** **K18 and K18a** rise from K84 (Guide II, section 4g): **the staircase is blocked by a masonry wall just below K17, with only a chink that passes gas.** **K21** runs from K73 up through K61, K9, K30 and K35 to K47. **K64** runs from K68 up past K13 to K46. **K83** runs from K78 up to K83a and on to K37. **K79** rises to K72.

## 2. Larders of Ill Omen, K61 to K72

**Map 11.**

### 2a. K61, the elevator trap

**This trap delivers its victims into Guide II, at K47.** **The machinery that works it is K31, filed in Guide II.**

**Printed, I6 p.22.** **The trap is triggered by weight: 400 gp or more, with a 30% chance per character.** **Two portcullises drop**, and a Dexterity check at +5 avoids 1-8 points of damage from them. **Stone blocks 10 feet by 2 feet close in a single full round**, weighing **40 tons**. **Sleeping gas fills the compartment: a saving throw versus poison at -4 or the character falls unconscious.** **The compartment then carries the trapped and sleeping characters up to K47.**

> **This is not a killing trap, it is a delivery system.** **A party that triggers it loses people upward, asleep, into the room with the Guardian Portrait**, whose printed purpose is to hold characters until Strahd commands their release. **The trap, the portrait and Strahd are one mechanism, and the party experiences it as three unrelated disasters.**

> **If someone outside tries to get them out, which the printed timing invites.** **The sequence is the thing to hold on to.** **The portcullises drop with lightning speed**, closing the middle ten-foot section, with a Dexterity check at +5 to avoid 1-8 from them. **Only then do the stone blocks begin their one full round down, and they close behind the portcullises**, so the barrier nearest a rescuer is the portcullis and the stone is sealing the space beyond it while he works.
- **The attempt is available.** Nothing printed forbids it, and the module gives the DM a round of visible descent to adjudicate.
- **Use the Strength procedure already in these guides** for raising it, as this guide does for the coffin lid at crypt 14 and Guide I does for forcing a door. **No new roll is introduced here.**
- **Success at the portcullis does not by itself free anyone.** **The stone is coming down behind it.** What a lift can achieve is reaching someone in the moment before the stone is fully down, and **whether it reaches them is decided by where they are standing, how far the stone has descended and how long the attempt takes**, not by the roll alone.
- **What the module does print about the outcome.** Those outside **hear grinding for one melee round and then watch the stones and portcullises rise on a clear corridor.** **The trapped are gone upward, not dead.** **A failed rescue costs the attempt, not the rescuer.**

> **The rescue route is K31, in Guide II.** The compartment has **a secret door in its top**, and K31b opens onto the shaft from K39. **A party that understands the machine can go up after its people.**

### 2b. K62, Servants' Hall, and Cyrus Belview

**Printed, I6 p.23.** **The hall stands in deadly silence.** The low ceiling sags from heavy beams. **A fog clings to the floor in thick patches, obscuring everything less than 3 feet above the floor.** **A giant shadow is cast across the ceiling as a dark figure shuffles purposefully down the corridor toward you.**

**What is there (printed).** A rusted but sound portcullis east to K63. Heavy plank double doors west, banded with steel. Stairs east on the north wall up to K23. **"The figure approaching is Cyrus Belview who will not attack first."** **The light in the room comes from a lantern on the floor behind Cyrus.**

**Cyrus Belview, human, F-2:** **AC 8; MV 12"; hp 8; #AT 1; Dmg 1-4; AL CN.** **He uses the 2nd-level fighter attack and saving throw tables.**

**To hit, 2nd-level fighter:** AC 10 on 10, AC 8 on 12, AC 6 on 14, AC 4 on 16, AC 2 on 18, AC 0 on 20.

**Saves, as a fighter of 1 to 2:** paralysis, poison or death magic 14; petrification or polymorph 15; rod, staff or wand 16; breath weapon 17; spell 17.

**Who he is (printed).** **"Poor old Cyrus is obviously crazy."** **He has served the master for uncounted years and is totally devoted to him.** **He tries to get the PCs to retire "to your room in the tower" (K49).** **If the PCs do not go to K49, Cyrus returns to his work of preparing his dinner in K65.** **He giggles to himself from time to time for no apparent reason.** **He also likes to tell poor jokes at the most inopportune moments.**

> **The giant shadow and the shuffling figure are the module setting up a monster and delivering a butler.** **He will not attack first** and he is **the only creature below the castle that can be talked to.**

> **What he is actually doing is steering.** **"Your room in the tower" is K49, the Lounge, next to K50, the Guestroom the witches raid at night.** **He is not threatening anyone. He is showing them to a bed.**

### 2c. K63 to K68, the service rooms

**K63, Wine Cellar (printed, I6 p.23).** Arched frames of stone form a low wet ceiling. **Great casks of wine line the walls, their bands rusting and their contents long since spilled onto the ground.** **At the far south end of the west wall, a crack half an inch wide by 5 inches long leads to K18.**

> **Half an inch by five inches.** **Nothing walks through that.** It is a route for sound, for light, and for anything in gaseous form.

**K64, Guards' Stair (printed).** A spiral staircase of grey, dusty stones. **It starts at K68 and goes up past K13 to K46.**

**K65, Kitchen (printed, I6 p.23).** **A horrible odour of decay assaults your senses as you enter this steaming room.** **A huge pot bubbles over a blazing fire in the centre of the floor, its green, muddy contents rolling over and over.**

**The rule, printed.** **Any characters that look into the pot encounter the zombies that are in the pot.** **If Cyrus Belview is present when the zombies attack, he grabs a large club near the door and tries to beat them back into the pot.** **Cyrus explains that he just is not the cook he used to be and his meals tend to get out of hand these days.**

**3 normal zombies** (printed, I6 p.23): **AC 8; MV 6"; HD 2; hp 5, 4, 2; #AT 1; Dmg 1-8; AL N.** **They always strike last.**
- **To hit, 2 Hit Dice:** AC 10 on 6, AC 8 on 8, AC 6 on 10, AC 4 on 12, AC 2 on 14, AC 0 on 16.
- **Saves, as a fighter of 1 to 2:** paralysis, poison or death magic 14; petrification or polymorph 15; rod, staff or wand 16; breath weapon 17; spell 17.

> **These are ordinary zombies, not Strahd zombies.** **HD 2, turned on the zombie row, no dismemberment rule.** The Strahd zombies at K76 are a different creature with a different row.

> **Cyrus beating his own dinner back into the pot with a club is the one moment of comedy I6 allows itself below the castle**, and it is printed as a straight instruction to the DM.

**K66, Butler's Quarters (printed, I6 p.23).** A 20-foot-square room cramped by items filling it from wall to wall. A small sagging bed under **a huge faded tapestry of Ravenloft castle.** Dusty lanterns, huge rich curtains draped haphazardly. **Thousands of pieces of junk cover the floor. Broken swords, crumpled shields and helmets lie in piles all about.**

**What is here (printed).** **"There is nothing of value in this room."** **If Cyrus Belview is with the party, the PCs should notice that he is fingering their equipment and chuckling to himself.** **Cyrus has been picking up equipment from dead adventurers for years.** **"He looks forward to adding to his collection after Strahd gets through with the PCs."**

> **The piles of broken swords are the previous parties.** The module shows the players what happened to everyone who came before, **and has the pleasant old man appraise their gear in front of them.**

#### K65 and K66: playing Cyrus

**The three in the pot are anonymous.** **I6 says nothing about who they were or how they got there, and looking into the pot shows nothing to identify them** (I6 p.23). **They are ordinary zombies, so the Strahd zombies' printed origin (I6 p.32) does not reach them.** **Cyrus's line about his cooking is a remark about his cooking, not an account of them.** **K66's collection from "dead adventurers" is one room away, and I6 draws no line between the two; neither does this guide.**

**Play him as one man in both rooms.** **At K65 he is a devoted old servant with a club, beating his dinner back into the pot and apologising for his cooking.** **At K66 he fingers the party's equipment, chuckles, and looks forward to adding it to his collection once Strahd has finished with them.** **Pleasant, harmless in a fight, genuinely helpful about a bed for the night, and already estimating what the party will be worth to him dead.** **All of it is printed, and the DM needs nothing more.**

**K67, Guards' Hall (printed, I6 p.23).** **Darkness, cold as a winter sweat, wraps around you.** Large oak tables, scarred and beaten, **lay scattered like toys about this room, their wood crushed and splintered.** **Dark stains cover the floor and the wall.** 30 feet east to west, 60 feet north to south.

**K68, Guards' Run (printed).** A 10-foot-wide arched corridor starting at a heavy wooden door on the north. **Cool, moist air blows out from an archway in the west wall.** The archway leads to K69, the north door to K67, and the hallway ends at K64 to the south.

### 2d. K69, Guards' Quarters, and the ten skeletons

**Printed, I6 p.23.** **Sickly yellow lichen covers the ceiling of this 10-foot-wide passage running east and west.** **Opening off to both sides of this passage are 10-foot-square alcoves.** **Rotting cots and rags are in the shadows of the alcoves.** **A deathly silence fills the hall.**

**The rule, printed.** **"When the characters enter the hall half way, 10 skeletons leap from the alcoves and attack."**

**10 skeletons** (printed, I6 p.23): **AC 7; MV 12"; HD 1; hp 7, 7, 7, 6, 6, 5, 3, 3, 1, 1; #AT 1; Dmg 1-6; AL N.**

**To hit, 1 Hit Die:** AC 10 on 9, AC 8 on 11, AC 6 on 13, AC 4 on 15, AC 2 on 17, AC 0 on 19.

**Saves, as a fighter of 1 to 2:** paralysis, poison or death magic 14; petrification or polymorph 15; rod, staff or wand 16; breath weapon 17; spell 17.

**What the Monster Manual adds (MM pp.87, 88).** **"Skeletons suffer only one-half damage from sharp and/or edged weapons such as spears, daggers, swords. Blunt weapons such as clubs, maces, flails, etc. score normal damage."** **Fire scores normal damage.** ***Sleep*, *charm*, *hold* and cold-based spells do not affect skeletons.** **Holy water causes 2-8 hit points of damage for each vial which strikes.** **"Skeletons attack until destroyed."**

> **2-8 here, and 2-7 against a vampire, and both are correct.** **MM p.88 prints 2-8 for skeletons; MM p.99 prints 2-7 per vial-full against vampires.** **The figures are creature-specific and are not to be standardised to one number.** Each entry in these guides carries the figure printed for that creature.

> **These are the real skeletons, and the party has been trained to ignore them.** **K23 and K26 in Guide II both print skeletons that are explicitly harmless scenery.** This corridor prints ten that leap from alcoves at the halfway point. **A party that has learned to walk past hanging bones walks into the middle of the passage first.**

> **Half damage from edged weapons is the detail that decides the fight.** A party of sword users does half damage to ten attackers. **A cleric with a mace, or a flask of holy water at 2-8, is worth more here than a fighter.**

> **They are turned on the skeleton row, the easiest on the matrix: a 1st level cleric turns them on a 10 and a 3rd level cleric on a 4.** Section 9a.

#### What the party sees, and what the trigger does not say

**The boxed description is given on entering, so the room's former purpose is visible before anyone searches; the skeletons are not in it.**

**The trigger is a position, not a perception** (printed). **It tells the DM when the ambush fires. It does not say the skeletons cannot be found before it.**

> **I6 prints no concealment rule here, no surprise roll and no detection difficulty.** **A party that advances with light and looks into an alcove before the halfway point is doing something the module does not adjudicate**, and the DM decides what they find. **The guide supplies no compulsory roll and no automatic discovery** (ruling). **The one printed cue is that the cots and rags are "in the shadows of the alcoves", which is a reason to look rather than a bar to looking.**

> **The failure to avoid is turning a positional trigger into an undetectability rule.** **A cautious party that illuminates the alcoves, probes ahead or refuses to walk down the centre has earned whatever advantage the DM judges**, and the halfway line remains the point at which the ambush fires if nothing has changed before it.

#### Optional characterisation: the room, not the occupants (guide suggestion)

**What is printed about who they were: nothing.** **I6 names the passage Guards' Quarters, and K64 and K68 are Guards' Stair and Guards' Run, so the printed names establish what the passage was for.** **They do not identify these ten.** **I6 does not say the skeletons were the guards who slept here, how they died, or who animated them.**

> **The former-guards reading is available and stays optional** (guide interpretation). **The room's name makes it plausible. It does not make it printed.** **No insignia, no names, no possessions, no reconstructed deaths.**

**What the room supplies without any of that.** **Alcoves with rotting cots and rags in them: places where people once slept.** **Ten things now rise out of those places to kill anyone who walks between them.** **The DM can stage the encounter on that alone**, and the uncertainty about who they were survives it intact.

**Two printed details that help the staging.**

**The alcove count is not printed.** **I6 prints alcoves opening off both sides and ten skeletons leaping from them. It does not say how many alcoves there are**, so the DM distributes them freely and nothing is contradicted. **Positions are the DM's staging, never a printed assignment.**

**The hit points are not ten individual totals.** **Printed: 7, 7, 7, 6, 6, 5, 3, 3, 1, 1.** **Ten entries, five distinct values, and a spread from seven down to one.** **Two of them fall to almost anything.** **That gives the DM a shape for the fight, with the first to drop meaning very little and three of them standing well past the rest**, and it does so without assigning anyone a history.

> **One connection to refuse.** **K70, the next room, prints broken bones amid crumpled and crushed plate armour with shields and swords driven into the walls.** **It is adjacent, it is armoured dead, and I6 prints no link between it and these ten.** **The guide draws none.** **A DM who wants one is making it**, which is the same decision as a name on a rack at K76 and carries the same cost.

### 2e. K70 to K72, and the shadow demon

**K70, Kingsman Hall (printed, I6 p.23).** A 30-foot-square room lying in chaos. **Shattered furniture lies in heaps near the walls. Broken bones lie scattered amid crumpled and crushed plate armour.** **Shields and swords jut from the walls as if driven there by some tremendous force.**

**K71, Kingsmen Quarters (printed).** A dark, low passage leads to an ascending staircase. **Sickly yellow lichen covers the ceiling.** A small room on each side of the passage.

> **That ascending staircase is K20a, and it leads up into the Guardian of Sorrow**, filed in Guide II at section 4i. **The tower is alive and the stair is part of it.** A party going up from here wakes it.

**K72, Office of Vengeance (printed, I6 p.24).** **"This shadowy room is in perfect order."** An old cot stands to one side, **its heavy blanket made taut and straight.** A great table with its chair, inkwell and quill **lying carefully in place.** **Lances and swords are carefully hung on the walls.**

**The rule, printed.** **"A shadow demon lives in this room. After the party enters the room, the demon will leap out and attack."** **A secret door in the north end of the west wall leads to K79.**

**1 shadow demon** (printed, I6 p.24): **AC 1; MV 12"; HD 7+3; hp 35; #AT 3; Dmg 1-8 / 1-6 / 1-6; AL CE; SA +2 to hit, can cast *darkness 10' radius* and *fear* once per day; SD takes half damage in darkness or torchlight, immune to fire, cold and lightning, turn as "special" undead, 50% chance of going undetected.**

**Light changes it, and the figures are printed.** **"If illuminated by a torch, the demon becomes AC 5, only +1 to hit, and takes normal damage."** **"If a *light* spell is cast upon it, the demon takes 1-6 points of damage per level of the caster, as if struck by a fireball."** **It appears as a shadowy humanoid form with dim red eye slits.**
- **To hit, 7+3 Hit Dice, at its printed +2 in darkness:** AC 10 on 1, AC 8 on 3, AC 6 on 5, AC 4 on 7, AC 2 on 9, AC 0 on 11.
- **To hit under a torch, at +1:** AC 10 on 2, AC 8 on 4, AC 6 on 6, AC 4 on 8, AC 2 on 10, AC 0 on 12.
- ***Darkness 10' radius***, once a day: the magic-user's *darkness, 15' radius* (PHB p.69) at the printed 10-foot radius, worked at its 7 Hit Dice (ruling). **Range 7"; casting 2 segments; lasts 1 turn and 7 rounds; no saving throw.** **Total, impenetrable darkness: infravision and ultravision are useless, and neither normal nor magical light works inside it except *light* and *continual light*.** **A *light* spell and the darkness cancel each other.** **Inside it the demon has its darkness figures: AC 1, +2 to hit, half damage.**
- ***Fear***, once a day (PHB p.76), worked at its 7 Hit Dice as the caster's level (ruling). **Range 0; casting 4 segments; a cone 6" long, 3" across at the far end and half a foot at the demon; a save negates.** **Each creature in the cone that fails drops whatever it holds at once on a d100 roll of 60% at 1st level or 1 Hit Die, less 5% for each level or Hit Die above that (35% at 6th, 15% at 10th, none from 13th), and from the next round flees at its fastest rate for 7 rounds.**
- **Saves, 7+3 counts as 8, as a fighter of 7 to 8:** paralysis, poison or death magic 10; petrification or polymorph 11; rod, staff or wand 12; breath weapon 12; spell 13.
- ***Dispel evil*** **from the K78 scroll can banish it only if it counts as a summoned creature or one enchanted into evil service, which I6 does not establish: decide before play** (3d). **If it does, the reader must touch it, and it saves to negate.**

> **This is the clearest light puzzle in I6 and the numbers are stark.** **In darkness: AC 1, +2 to hit, half damage from everything.** **Under a torch: AC 5, +1 to hit, full damage.** **Under a *light* spell: 1-6 per caster level, as a fireball, which from a 5th level magic-user averages 17 or 18 against its 35 hit points.**

> **Two *light* spells kill it outright on average.** A party that reaches for *light* rather than a sword has solved the room. **A party fighting in the dark is attacking AC 1 for half damage while being hit at +2.**

> **It is turned on the "special" row**, which no cleric below 8th level can attempt (section 9a). **In practice turning is not available.**

## 3. Dungeon and Catacomb, K73 to K83a

**Map 12.**

### 3a. K73, Dungeon Hall, and the teleport floor

**Printed, I6 p.24.** **The stairs descend into black, still water that fills an arched hallway before you.** **The water's surface is like dark mirrored glass, disturbed only occasionally by the "thwick" of a drop falling from the ceiling.** Twenty feet down the hallway, arched doorways lead downward 2 feet from each side. **In each arched doorway, an iron door stands closed and partially submerged.** **All is still except for a weak cry for help coming through the south door.**

**The floor, printed and exact.** **"The floor beneath the water is not as solid as it may seem."** **There is a safe path, shown in I6's Diagram A, but the rest of the floor is covered with special weight-sensitive trap doors.** **There is a 5% chance per 100 gp (10 lbs) weight that a person standing on the false floor will cause the trap to open.** **The pit under each trap door is a *teleport* activated by the opening door.**

**What the others see (printed).** **An explosion of air and water flies up around the trapped character**, air that was trapped in the pit released suddenly. **The trapped character suddenly falls from sight.** **The trap doors automatically reset themselves, leaving only a slowly dissipating swirl in the water.**

**Where they go (printed).** **Trapped characters are teleported into dungeon cells, closed with iron bars, the floor 5 feet under water**, in K74 and K75. **"Strahd attacks lone characters here if he can."**

> **The weak cry for help is the werewolf at K75, and it is bait.** **A party that hurries toward it crosses the trapped floor at speed.**

> **The trap separates the party silently and resets.** Nobody can follow, nobody can see where they went, **and the printed line says Strahd takes lone characters.** **This is the module's kidnapping machine and it is dressed as a flooded corridor.**

> **(ruling)** The safe path exists and is printed on the diagram, so **a party that probes ahead with a pole or sends its lightest member first is playing the room as designed.** **5% per 10 pounds** means a fully equipped fighter is far more likely to fall through than an unarmoured scout.

### 3b. K74 and K75, the dungeons

**K74, North Dungeon (printed, I6 p.24).** **The fungus-laden ceiling hangs 3 feet above still, black water that fills this dungeon corridor.** **The water is 5 feet deep.** **Small cells, their entrances blocked by iron bars, are on both sides of this arched central hall.** **Liquid drips from the hanging growth on the ceiling.**

**The corridor is 40 feet long.** **There is a secret door 5 feet up the north wall of cell "e" that leads to a polished marble slide, K82.** **The secret door can only be opened from K82.**

**What is in the cells (printed).** **"In each cell listed below, many previous adventurers gave up their gold. All the items are lying under water."**

| Cell | Contents, printed |
|---|---|
| **a** | 6,100 ep |
| **b** | 5,600 ep |
| **c** | 600 pp |
| **g** | 6,900 ep |
| **h** | **magical (glowing) +2 sword, LG; I 11; EGO 9** |

> **The +2 sword in cell h is intelligent, lawful good, Intelligence 11 and Ego 9.** **I6 prints no further powers and this guide invents none.** **An Ego 9 weapon in the hands of a non-lawful-good wielder is a personality contest the DMG governs**, and the DM who wants that subplot has the printed numbers to run it.

> **The ceiling is 3 feet above the water and the water is 5 feet deep.** **Anyone standing has their head in the fungus.** A character who falls, or who is dropped here by the K73 teleport, **arrives in five feet of water inside a barred cell** and **can stand up in it**, with 3 feet of air between the surface and the fungus. **The danger is the cell and not the water**, and it becomes the water only for someone who cannot stand: unconscious, held down, or in armour heavy enough to keep them off their feet (ruling). **I6 prints no drowning rule here and the guide imposes none on a character who is merely wet.**

**K75, South Dungeon (printed, I6 p.24).** **The same description as K74.** **"A werewolf lurks in the water in cell a, waiting."** **There are 650 pp in cell b.**

**1 werewolf** (printed, I6 p.24): **AC 5; MV 15"; HD 4+3; hp 17; #AT 1; Dmg 2-8 (2d4); AL CE; SD can only be hit by silver or +1 or better weapons.**

**To hit, 4+3 Hit Dice:** AC 10 on 5, AC 8 on 7, AC 6 on 9, AC 4 on 11, AC 2 on 13, AC 0 on 15.

**Saves, 4+3 counts as 5, as a fighter of 5 to 6:** paralysis, poison or death magic 11; petrification or polymorph 12; rod, staff or wand 13; breath weapon 13; spell 14.

**His disposition, printed.** **"The werewolf appears as a villager. He begs the PCs to rescue him."** **He was locked in here by Strahd because he disobeyed Strahd.** **"The werewolf is anxious to prove his worth to Strahd, so he will try to join the party and attack the PCs at the first good chance."**

**What the Monster Manual adds (MM p.63).** **Surprise on a 1 to 3.** **The infection rule: a character bitten for damage equal to half or more of their total hit points, but not killed, contracts lycanthropy.** **Belladonna eaten within an hour gives a 25% chance of cure**, and ***cure disease* cast by a patriarch of 12th level or higher within three days removes it.**

The werewolf cries for help from a flooded cell. Cyrus offers them a room.** **I6 builds the same trap three times and only the second one is honest about being a trap.**

> **The cry for help is audible from K73**, which is what draws a party across the trapped floor.

### 3c. K76 and K77, the torture room and the balcony

**K76, Torture Room (printed, I6 p.24).** **"Dark, low shapes thrust up out of the still, brackish water that fills this room."** 50 feet square. **The ceiling is 17 feet above the water, which is 3 feet deep.** An arched exit in the centre of the east wall. **To the north, a large balcony, K77, stands 7 feet above the level of the water.**

**What the shapes are (printed).** **Close examination reveals racks, iron maidens, stocks and other torture apparatus.** **"The skeletons of their last victims seem frozen in silent screams. The lower portions of them are underwater."** **"They are Strahd Zombies who attack after the party is in the room."** **The zombies rise slowly out of the water, their slime-grey arms clawing upward through the water as they attack.**

**Check the record before placing them.** **Strike 2 draws its shortfall from the reserve first and then from here, before K28** (9e-bis). **If it has been spent, this room holds what is left of its six, and may hold none.** **Use the surviving garrison, not the printed one.**

**6 Strahd zombies** (printed, I6 p.24): **AC 8; MV 9"; HD 4; hp 18, 16, 15, 13, 12, 10; #AT 1; Dmg 1-8; AL N; single hit of 5 or more points dismembers, dismembered limbs attack; turn as mummies.**

**To hit, 4 Hit Dice:** AC 10 on 5, AC 8 on 7, AC 6 on 9, AC 4 on 11, AC 2 on 13, AC 0 on 15.

**Saves:** they are non-intelligent (I6 p.32), so they save as a fighter of 1 to 2 except against poison or death magic, where they save as a fighter of 3 to 4: paralysis 14, poison or death magic 13; petrification or polymorph 15; rod, staff or wand 16; breath weapon 17; spell 17.

> **These match the Table 6 Strahd zombies and differ from the two at K28 in Guide II**, which print MV 6", Dmg 1-6 and AL NE. **Both are carried as printed.**

> **The dismemberment rule and three feet of water together are the encounter.** **A hit of 5 or more severs a limb and the limb attacks.** **The party is standing in waist-deep water fighting things that multiply when struck hard**, and severed limbs in black water cannot be seen.

> **"After the party is in the room" is a printed instruction to let them commit.** The zombies do not rise at the doorway.

#### Who the six were: attribution, then characterisation

**The connection is printed in terms, which makes this the opposite case to E7.** **"The skeletons of their last victims"** names these six as the victims of the apparatus they are still standing in. **No suggestion is needed to make the link and the guide invents nothing to supply it.**

**The route to it is printed too.** **"Close examination reveals"** governs the whole identification: the boxed description gives the players only "dark, low shapes" in brackish water. **The apparatus, the skeletons and the silent screams are what close examination reveals**, so this is a discovery the players make rather than a fact the DM announces, in the same way the dust governs the crypt doors at 4a-bis. **A party that keeps its distance fights six shapes in the water and never learns what the room was for.**

**I6 individuates them before the guide does.** **Six distinct hit point totals are printed: 18, 16, 15, 13, 12 and 10.** **The DM already has six distinguishable individuals and needs no invention to tell them apart.**

**A printed tension, recorded and not resolved.** **I6 p.32 prints that Strahd zombies "were created from the long-dead guards of Castle Ravenloft". I6 p.24 prints that these six were the torture apparatus's last victims.** **Both are printed. The guide does not reconcile them.**

> **An interpretation the two printed facts permit (guide interpretation, not printed).** **Read together they describe the castle's own guards being tortured to death in its dungeon and raised afterwards to serve the man who had it done.** **The two statements permit that reading. They do not establish it**, and I6 asserts no causal link between the guards' origin and the apparatus's last victims. **A DM is free to hold the two apart instead.** **It is offered because it costs nothing to hold and changes how the room reads**, not because the module requires it.

**Optional characterisation (guide suggestion).** **The six are still in the apparatus.** **The DM might let that do the work**: which of them is on the rack and which in the stocks, which one the party sees first, which one rises slowest. **The printed hit point spread gives that a spine already, and the weakest at 10 points need not be the one furthest gone.** **None of this changes a statistic or a behaviour.**

> **What stays out.** **A surviving possession, a recognisable face, a name scratched into a rack or anything else that would let the party identify an individual gives the characters something to discover that I6 does not provide.** **A DM may add one deliberately, and should know that is what they are doing.** **The guide places none.**

> **What is lost by placing one.** **I6 gives these six a collective identity and no individual one.** **They are the last people this room was used on, and they are still here, and nobody knows which of them was which.** **A name on a rack answers a question the room is more effective for leaving open.**

**K77, Observation Balcony (printed, I6 p.24).** **Two large thrones on this balcony.** **Behind the throne is a large red velvet curtain running 30 feet from the east wall to the west wall.** The ceiling is 10 feet high. **The room continues behind the curtain an additional 10 feet to a wall with a door in the centre of it.** **The door leads to K78.** **A bag containing 600 pp is hidden beneath the western-most throne.**

> **Two thrones overlooking a torture chamber.** I6 does not comment and does not need to.

### 3d. K78, the Brazier Room

**Printed, I6 p.26.** 30 feet square, rising to a 20-foot-tall flat ceiling. **Gargoyle carvings smile, revealing their teeth, from high upon the walls, well lit by a brazier that burns fiercely in the centre of the room.** From alcoves in the centre of the east and west walls, **two iron statues stand saluting with their weapons bared.** **Each statue has four arms, one with a shield, one with a sword, and the remaining two with their palms toward the ceiling over their heads.** **Their eyes seem to watch you as you walk about the room.** **High overhead, an hourglass with writing on its base hangs suspended above the brazier.** **All of its sand is in the upper portion, somehow refusing to run down into the bottom.** **In the centre of the room next to the brazier is a golden chest.** A single door in the south wall, three doors in the north wall.

**The statues are iron golems. Each golem holds a stone in each hand** (printed).

**2 iron golems** (printed, I6 p.26): **AC 3; MV 6"; HD 10; hp 80, 80; #AT 1; Dmg 4d10; AL N; SD immune to all spells except electrical attacks, which slow them to 50% movement; magical fire-based attacks repair instead of damage.**
- **To hit, 10 Hit Dice:** AC 10 on 0, AC 8 on 2, AC 6 on 4, AC 4 on 6, AC 2 on 8, AC 0 on 10.
- **They make no saving throws here: no spell affects them except electrical attacks, which slow them without a save.**

**I6 restricts them further, and the restriction is printed.** **"The golems remain inanimate until the sands of the hourglass run out. They will not use their natural poisonous gas ability. They can only be struck by +3 weapons or better."**

> **I6 disables the Monster Manual's poison gas explicitly.** **MM p.48 gives an iron golem a cloud of poisonous gas once every 7 melee rounds; I6 prints that these will not use it.** **The module governs its own encounter and the gas does not happen.**

> **The +3 requirement is printed in I6 itself, not only in the Monster Manual.** **80 hit points each, 4d10 a hit, and nothing under +3 touches them.** **The three +3 maces in the Treasury at K41, in Guide II, are the only weapons at +3 or better that I6 itself supplies.** **The *sunsword* is not among them as printed: it is +3 against undead only, and an iron golem is not undead, so it strikes at +2** (printed, I6 p.31). **The Fortunes reading places only the Holy Symbol, the Sunsword hilt and the Tome** (I6 p.4), **so it adds no weapon either.**

> **What the module supplies is not the same as what the party has.** **Characters arriving in Barovia may already carry weapons of +3 or better, and those damage the golems exactly as the maces do.** **The maces are a provision for a party that has none, not a gate the module expects every party to pass through.**

**The verse on the hourglass base (printed):**

> *In the palm of my hand*
> *Is the time left to thee*
> *When it's consumed*
> *Will the flame set you free!*

**The puzzle, printed and exact.** **All the doors in this room are spring-loaded and shut on their own if not prevented from doing so.** **When all the doors are shut, the sands in the hourglass start falling and run for 5 rounds.** **All of the doors lock shut.** **After the sands run out, the golems activate and attack for 5 rounds.** **They then return to their original positions, at which time the door the party entered the room by unlocks.** **The opening of any door resets the hourglass sands.** **Only a single door can be opened at any one time.**

**The solution, printed.** **To open a door other than the one they entered by, the characters must get one of the stones from the statues' hands and place it into the flaming brazier.** **If they do, the fire seems to consume the stone, but the stone magically appears again in the palm of the statue's hand.**

| Door | Stone | Golem | Hand |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Southern door** | Red Stone | West | Right |
| **West door on north wall** | Blue Stone | West | Left |
| **Centre door on north wall** | Black Stone | East | Right |
| **Eastern door on north wall** | Opal Stone | East | Left |

> **The verse is the instruction and the threat in four lines.** *In the palm of my hand* names where the stones are. *Is the time left to thee* names the hourglass. *When it's consumed* is the fire taking the stone. *Will the flame set you free* is the door opening.

> **Five rounds is the clock, and it is generous if the party reads the verse and brutal if they do not.** **Holding a door open stops the sands entirely.** A party that wedges the door it came in by has all the time in the world. **A party that lets every door swing shut has five rounds before two 80-hit-point golems hit for 4d10.**

> **The golems hold the stones while inanimate.** **Taking a stone from the hand of a thing that is going to animate in five rounds is the room's real tension**, and I6 prints no roll for it, so it is simply done (ruling).

**The chest (printed).** **It appears locked.** **Opening the chest from the front releases a cloud of *sleeping gas* from the false compartment in the top.** **Once the false top has been opened, an *illusion* shows an empty trunk.** **The trunk actually opens from the back** and contains **a *deck of many things*; a scroll of *protection from magic*; a scroll containing the two clerical spells *dispel evil* and *flamestrike*; and two *potions of healing*.**

> **Three deceptions stacked in one chest: a gas trap, an illusion of emptiness, and a hinge on the wrong side.** **A party that survives the gas sees an empty trunk and walks away from the best treasure below the castle.**

**The scroll of *protection from magic*** (DMG pp.127, 128). **Any character can read it, whatever their class; the clerical scroll beside it is for a cleric alone.** **Reading takes 8 segments.** **It raises an invisible globe of anti-magic 5 feet in radius around the reader, which moves with them and lasts 5 to 30 rounds (5d6).** **No magic passes into or out of it; normal things are unaffected.** **A magic item that touches the globe saves on 11 or better on a d20 or is drained of all its magic.** **If several items meet it at once, the leading one is checked first, then the rest in order of decreasing power, until one fails: that item is drained and the globe ends.**

> ***Dispel evil* and *flamestrike* on one scroll are climax equipment**, and **a *deck of many things* is the most dangerous object in AD&D.** **I6 prints no warning and this guide adds none beyond saying so.** **(ruling)** The deck is used under the DMG's own rules; **the DM who does not want it in play removes it before the game rather than fudging it afterwards.**

**The scroll's two spells** (PHB p.49). **A cleric reads it** (9c), **and each spell is erased as it is read.** **Its spells work at 10th level: a scroll spell is written one level above the lowest at which it can be cast, never below 6th** (DMG p.128), **and a cleric first casts fifth level spells at 9th** (PHB p.20). **A cleric below 9th level can still try** (DMG p.128): **the chance of failure is 5% for each level short of 9th; on a failure a second d100 decides what goes wrong: 1 to 3 levels short, 01 to 95 nothing happens and 96 to 00 a reverse or harmful effect; 4 to 6 levels short, 01 to 85 and 86 to 00; 7 to 9 levels short, 01 to 75 and 76 to 00.** **The spell is gone either way.**
- ***Flamestrike*** (*flame strike*). **Range 6"; a column of fire 1" across and 3" high; casting 8 segments.** **Each creature in the column saves: failure takes 6 to 48 (6d8), success 3 to 24 (3d8).** **It heals the iron golems here instead of harming them (magical fire, above), and the shadow demon at K72 is immune to fire.**
- ***Dispel evil***. **Touch; casting 8 segments; lasts 1 round per level, so 10 rounds.** **It sends summoned creatures of evil nature, and monsters enchanted and caused to perform evil deeds, back to their own plane or place**: aerial servants, demons, devils, djinn, efreet, elementals, invisible stalkers. **The creature touched saves to negate.** **While it lasts, every creature it could affect attacks the caster at -7.** **The shadow demon at K72 (2e) is a demon, one of the spell's examples, but I6 says only that it lives there: decide before play whether it was summoned or enchanted, and so whether the spell can touch it.** **Strahd and the undead in the crypts are neither summoned nor enchanted, and it does nothing to them (ruling).**

> **The *sleeping gas* prints no saving throw here, and the encounter still has to be run (guide ruling).** **I6 p.26 gives this chest a cloud of sleeping gas and no save, no duration and no effect.**
>
> **The adjudication: a saving throw versus poison at -4, and unconsciousness for 1 to 4 hours on a failure.** **That is not imported from outside the module.** **I6 prints exactly that figure twice for the same effect, at the K38 chest (I6 p.19) and at the K61 elevator trap (I6 p.22)**, both within this dungeon. **Where the module states a convention twice and omits it a third time, the guide follows the module's own convention rather than inventing one.**
>
> **What is not carried across.** **K38 prints that a party all of whom fall asleep wake in K50 four hours later.** **No such consequence is printed for K78 and none is supplied here.** A party that falls asleep in the Brazier Room is simply unconscious on the floor, **in a room whose doors have shut and whose golems animate when the sands run out.**

The wind within howls mournfully.** The stairs rise at 45 degrees for 20 horizontal feet to a 10-foot-square landing, **where the masonry walls abruptly end, opening into a roughly hewn tunnel heading east, K81.**

## 4. K84, the Catacombs, and the forty crypts

**Map 12.** **I6 heads this section: "PLEASE READ THIS SECTION WITH CARE. MUCH ACTIVITY CAN TAKE PLACE HERE."**

### 4a. The catacombs themselves

**Printed, I6 p.27.** **Buried deep beneath the keep of Ravenloft, this arched ceiling sags over squat crypts, forming these catacombs.** **A thick fog clings to the floor at knee height.** **Cobwebs hang limp in the musty air.** **Thick dust has settled over everything, filling the crypts' engravings, making it difficult to make out the names of those buried here.** **"The black ceiling is moving."**

**The shape of it (printed).** **Roughly 110 feet east to west by 180 feet north to south.** **10-foot-wide arched walkways running between 10-foot-square crypts.** **The arched walkways and the crypts support the ceiling.**

**Five exits (printed).**
1. **The crypt 1 door to the tunnel, K81.**
2. **The barred doorway north to room K85.**
3. **The barred doorway east to room K87.**
4. **The teleport in crypt 32 to room K86.**
5. **Up the high tower stair K18 or shaft K18a to the west.**

**Opening a crypt (printed).** **"The percent chance of opening a crypt door is equal to twice the total number of strength points applied."** **Super-strength characters add their percentage bonuses to the total chance.** **Unless noted otherwise, each crypt contains a 3-by-5-foot rectangular slab of marble, 3 feet high, with a skeleton draped in rags lying atop it.**

> **Twice the strength points applied, so two characters of 16 and 15 give 62%.** **Opening crypts is a group activity and the party will be doing it forty times.** **(ruling)** A failed attempt can be repeated; I6 prints no limit.

**3,000 bats** (printed, I6 p.27): **AC 6; MV 4"/18"; HD 1d4; #AT 0; Dmg 0; AL N.**

**Use the colony's survivors, not the printed three thousand.** **This is one population and the record carries it** (9f). **The bats that hunt the valley at night come from here and return to it, and the bats Strahd brings to the nightly harassment are drawn from the same survivors** (**both guide rulings; I6 states the colony and the evening flight, and not the relation**). **A party that killed bats here has reduced what the valley meets afterwards**, and a party that fought them in the valley meets fewer here. **The relation is not asserted for every bat anywhere**: it covers this colony, the valley's nightly bats and the harassment, which is what the two rulings name.

**The bats, printed in full.** **They hang here during daytime hours and fly out in the evening through K18a to hunt at night.** **"They will not attack humans unless specifically commanded to by Strahd or unless provoked."** **These bats do no damage but do get in the way of spell casting.** **"While these bats are attacking, any character attempting to cast a spell with a material component must make a dexterity check for the spell to work."** **"Make it obvious to the players that killing the bats is more trouble than it is worth."**

> **The moving black ceiling is three thousand bats and they are harmless until provoked.** **The danger is that a party fires into them.** **Then every spell with a material component needs a Dexterity check, in the room where the module expects its climax.**

> **Strahd can command them.** That is printed, and it is the one way the bats become a weapon.

### 4a-bis. Reading the crypt doors, which is how the dead are identified

**The rule I6 prints (I6 p.27).** **"Each crypt houses the remains of the person (or persons) listed on the crypt's stone door with an epitaph."** **"Each crypt is listed below. The epitaph on each stone door is written in quotes."**

> **That is a test the DM can apply without judgement.** **Text in quotes in the crypt list is the door, and the players can read it.** **Everything else in a crypt entry is the DM's and nobody in the adventure says it.**

**The dust (printed, I6 p.27).** **The read-aloud at 4a has it filling the engravings and making the names hard to read.**

> **The names are there and the dust is in the way.** **Identifying the dead is something the players do, not something the DM hands over as they walk past.** **What I6 prints is difficulty, not a procedure**, so the guide sets no required method and no roll: clearing a door is the obvious way and the DM may allow any other that fits (ruling). **A party that touches nothing learns nothing, and that is a choice they made rather than a secret the module kept.**

**What this does not decide.** **A connection that is not on the doors is not thereby unreachable.** **It is not discoverable from the printed inscriptions**, which is a statement about the stones. **Ordinary play, investigation, magic or an inhabitant may still produce information**, and the guide neither invents a predetermined revelation nor rules one out.

### 4b. The transpositional teleports, the best trap in I6

**Printed, I6 p.27.** **"There are transpositional teleports between crypts 37 and 38, between crypt 37 and the wall south of it, and between crypt 38 and the wall south of it."** **"These teleports form a protective ring around the obvious entrance to Strahd's coffin."**

**What they do, printed and exact.** **"These teleports exchange a living body passing through them for the undead body of a wight from crypt 14."** **Since the transfer is practically instantaneous, and since only living and undead matter is teleported, the teleported character's armour, clothing and so on becomes suddenly occupied by a wight.** **"The teleport puts the wight into the same pose the character it is replacing had."** **"The teleport exchange appears to others as though the character who was walking through the crypt passage suddenly turned into a wight."** **"The wight turns and attacks the party with the original character's weapons."** **"A wight appearing in a PC's clothing and armour is not damaged by any holy symbols that PC had (because the symbols are not forcefully presented)."**

**Where the character goes (printed).** **"The teleported character finds himself lying in a dark, confined space (the interior of the wight's coffin) wearing ragged, rotting cloth."** **"The coffin lid requires a lift gates throw to be made with a penalty of -5% to open it because of the confined space."** **"If Strahd attacks at this time, he may want to help open the coffin lid and attack the character in it."**

> **Nothing else in I6 does what this does.** A party member walks down a passage and **turns into a wight in front of everyone, wearing their armour and swinging their sword.** **The party must decide, in one round, whether to kill their friend.**

> **Meanwhile the real character is sealed in a stone coffin in crypt 14, in rags, in the dark, among fifteen wights**, needing a bend bars and lift gates roll at -5% to get out. **And crypt 14's printed note says the remaining wights assist anyone inside a coffin trying to remove its lid, which makes the feat relatively easy.** **The wights want them out.**

> **The holy symbol detail is a deliberate piece of cruelty.** The cleric's symbol is in the wight's hands now, and it does nothing, **because it is not being forcefully presented.**

> **The ring guards the obvious entrance to K86.** **The teleport in crypt 32 is the unobvious entrance and it is safe.** A party that finds crypt 32 bypasses the ring entirely.

> **What the ring does not catch, and why that is the point of it (ruling).** The reading has three parts.
- **Printed.** **The exchange takes a living body, and only living and undead matter is teleported** (above).
- **Interpretation.** **A creature transformed into gas has no corporeal body for this particular mechanism to exchange.** The module takes a living body and moves living and undead matter; **it does not say whether gaseous living matter is a qualifying body**, and that silence is where the interpretation sits.
- **Consequence.** **Strahd in forced gaseous form passes without triggering the exchange.** That is why the module can seal his own door with the ring without sealing his own route: **he comes home through a trap that cannot see him.**
- **The same consequence reaches a living character in gaseous form, and for the same reason.** One under a *potion of gaseous form*, taking the chink route past the masonry wall, **has no corporeal body for the floor to exchange either**, so the ring does not act on them. **The operative point is the absence of a body, not the difference between living and undead**, so being alive does not restore the trigger. **Treat this as a second safe way in**, beside crypt 32. **The DM applies it when a player proposes it.** It is not a route the characters can learn about in advance, because nothing in the crypts states the trigger's wording.
- **This is a reading of this trigger's wording and nothing more.** **It is not a general exemption from teleportation for gaseous creatures**, and nothing elsewhere in these guides is built on it. The crypt 32 teleport, for instance, is a different mechanism with different wording and moves whoever enters it.

### 4d. The creatures in the crypts

**Which procedure each of these needs, so none of it has to be found mid-encounter.** **Turning is at 9a for every undead here.** **Energy drain, which the spectre, the wights and the crypt 20 vampire all inflict, is at 9b.** **The crypt 20 vampire is governed throughout by 9d, by the gaze and charm procedure at 9j, and at 0 hit points by the forced gaseous form at 9h.** **The ghost's possession and its rescue are carried in this section**, because nothing else in the series carries them at the point of use. **Death and what can be undone is at 9c, and the ghost is the printed exception to it.**

**Crypt 4, 1 ghost** (printed, I6 p.27): **AC 0/8; MV 9"; HD 10; hp 40; #AT 1; Dmg age 10-40 years; AL LE.**

**Its rules, printed.** **"Characters that see this ghost age 10-40 years and flee in panic for 2-12 (2d6) turns unless they make a saving throw vs. spells."** **Clerics above 6th level are immune to this attack, and other classes above 8th level gain a +2 bonus on their rolls.** **The ghost attacks with a *magic jar* spell while ethereal, or by touch if materialised as noted.** **"Spells do not work against a ghost unless they are cast on the ethereal plane."** **"Any human, dwarf, elf, gnome or halfling that is killed by a ghost is dead forever."**

> **Dead forever is printed and absolute.** **No *raise dead*, no *resurrection*, no wish.** It is the only death in I6 that cannot be undone, and it happens on a touch. **A DM who opens crypt 4 without having read this line will undo a campaign by accident.**

> **Ariel was a terrible man, who sacrificed more than himself in his quest for wings** (printed, and DM text rather than the door: the stone carries only the name and title). **I6 prints the name as "Aerial" on the door and "Ariel" in this sentence; both are kept as printed.** **The two armour classes, 0 and 8, are the ethereal and material states** (ruling, from the printed ghost convention).

**What can touch it, where I6 is silent** (MM p.43). **Until it semi-materialises it can be fought only by a creature on the ethereal plane, where it is AC 8, or by telepathic means.** **Semi-materialised, at AC 0, it can be struck only by silver, which does half damage, or by magical weapons.** **That is what the printed line about spells means in practice: the party cannot reach it until it chooses to be reachable.**

> **The crypt's own wording governs the sight, and the Monster Manual is used only for what the crypt leaves out.** **I6 ages a character 10-40 years for seeing the ghost here. The Monster Manual's general entry ages on sight by 10 years and reserves 10-40 for the touch.** **Crypt 4 uses its own numbers**, on the same principle applied to the banshee's saving throw below: the module governs its own encounters.

**Turning (DMG p.75, and section 9a).** **No cleric below 7th level can affect a ghost at all.** **A 7th-level cleric needs 20, an 8th needs 16**, and a paladin turns as a cleric two levels lower. **At this series' levels turning is effectively not available**, which is the same answer the party will get from their spells.

**The possession, which the printed line names and does not explain** (MM p.43, with the *magic jar* procedure).
- **Range and rate.** **Any one creature within 60 feet indoors may be attacked, and each attempt takes one round.** **That a failed attempt may be repeated on the following round is a guide ruling**, drawn from the spell's one round per attempt; no frequency is printed for the ghost.
- **The saving throw is the whole of the resistance.** It is adjusted by comparing the **combined intelligence and wisdom** of ghost and victim, intelligence alone for a non-humanoid victim.

| Difference | Adjustment to the victim's die |
|---|---|
| Negative 9 or more | +4 |
| Negative 8 to 6 | +3 |
| Negative 5 to 3 | +2 |
| Negative 2 to 0 | +1 |
| Positive 1 to 4 | 0 |
| Positive 5 to 8 | -1 |
| Positive 9 to 12 | -2 |
| Positive 13 or more | -3 |

**A negative difference means the ghost's combined score is the lower and the victim gains the bonus.**

- **Fix the ghost's own scores before play (ruling).** **The Monster Manual gives it high intelligence and no wisdom at all.** **Choose a wisdom, write it on the record, and read the adjustment off the table** rather than arguing it at the moment a character's body is being taken.
- **On a failed save the ghost has the body.** **It can call on the victim's rudimentary knowledge only: not their language and not their spells.**
- **The victim keeps trying to throw it out**, at a frequency set by the same difference: **every round** where the difference is negative or positive by less than 5; **every turn** at positive 5 to 8; **every day** at positive 9 to 12; **every week** at positive 13 or more.
- **On a successful save the attempt fails**, and the ghost may semi-materialise and attack by touch instead, **ageing the victim 10 to 40 years on a hit**.
- **Where the victim is while it lasts (ruling).** **Displaced into an inactive state bound to their own body: aware, unable to act, unable to communicate.** **There is no gem, jar or receptacle anywhere in this adventure**, so a party must not be sent hunting for one. **Ending the possession restores them while the body lives.**
- **If the possessed body is killed (ruling, provisional).** **The possession ends, the ghost returns to its ethereal existence unharmed, and the displaced victim dies with the body.**

**The rescue is *exorcise*, and it is an encounter in itself** (PHB, 4th-level clerical). **Range 10 feet. Casting time 1 to 100 or more turns. Duration permanent. Components verbal, somatic, the cleric's holy object and holy water. No saving throw.** **The base chance is 1% to 100%, which the DM may instead set from the circumstances.** **The dice are rolled each turn of exorcism and the spell succeeds when that number or less comes up.** **Modify by -1% for each level the cleric is below the possessor, reading the ghost's 10 Hit Dice as its level, so -3% for a 7th-level cleric, and by +1% cumulative where the cleric is the higher.** **A religious artefact or relic adds 1% to 50%.** **Once begun the casting cannot be interrupted, or the spell is spoiled and useless.**

> **What that costs, and why it will usually not be available.** **The casting runs in turns rather than rounds**, the victim must stay within 10 feet for all of them, and a single interruption wastes the spell, in a room the module expects to be full of things that interrupt. **A 4th-level clerical spell is beyond a party at this series' levels, and the scroll census finds no *exorcise* anywhere in I6.** **So the honest answer at the table is that a successful possession is not cured below the castle.** **The ghost is a creature to get away from, and the guide says so rather than implying a remedy the adventure does not contain.**

**Crypt 7, 1 spectre** (printed, I6 p.27): **AC 2; MV 15"/30"; HD 7+3; hp 40; #AT 1; Dmg 1d8 plus drain 2 energy levels; AL LE; SD +1 or better weapon to hit.**

**Printed here:** **not affected by *sleep*, *charm*, *hold* or cold based spells.** **"Anyone totally drained of levels by the spectre becomes a half-strength spectre in the control of the monster that drained him."**

> **The door lies flat on the floor under the fog and the crypt already gapes open.** **Nobody has to roll strength to open this one.** It is waiting.

**Who he was, and the tragedy that reaches three locations (all printed, I6 pp.27 and 28).** **The epitaph reads: "Endorovich (Endorovich the Terrible): What the blood of an thousand slaves did not do, the spurn of a woman accomplished."**

**I6 prints the whole story under this crypt.** **"This man loved Marya (see crypt 6) and found that she loved someone else in his court. As Marya and her lover were dining, Endorovich put poison into the man's wine glass. The glasses were mixed up and the girl drank it instead. The lover was hanged for the deed and buried in the cemetery behind the church in Barovia township. Endorovich never did get over his guilt and, in his madness, killed many in his lifetime."**

> **This is the one piece of backstory I6 tells in full, and it is easy to run past.** **It joins three places the party can reach**: this crypt, **crypt 6 two doors away**, and **the churchyard at E7 in the village**, which the party may have walked through on their first evening in Barovia.

> **What each location gives the DM.**
> **Here**, a spectre that drains two levels a hit, whose epitaph says a woman's refusal did what a thousand deaths could not. **What is printed is the guilt**: "Endorovich never did get over his guilt and, in his madness, killed many in his lifetime." **A DM who wants to play the rage as grief has the material for it, and that is a characterisation rather than a stated motive.** **I6 gives the spectre no motive for anything it does in the crypt**, and it attacks as the printed statistics say regardless.
> **At crypt 6**, a stone with the name clawed off **"as if by some mad tormented beast"** (printed). **The epitaph it once carried, "Marya Markovia: Great was her beauty, undone by a jealous hand", is printed and readable to the DM.** **Who clawed it off is not printed.** The guide does not say it was Endorovich; the module places a mad killer two doors away and leaves the inference to whoever wants it.
> **At E7, in Guide I**, the grave of a man hanged for a poisoning he did not commit. **I6 places him in the churchyard behind the Barovia church by name of location, not by name of person**, and prints no marker, no name and no one who remembers him. **There is no printed route by which the characters learn he is there.**

> **What the characters can actually learn, which is far less than the DM now knows.** **The whole account above is DM-facing text in the module. None of it is said to anyone in the adventure.**
> **Crypt 7's epitaph names no one**: it says only that the spurn of a woman did what the blood of a thousand slaves could not. **Crypt 6's epitaph cannot be read at all, because it has been clawed off.** **So a party that opens both has a cryptic boast and a defaced stone**, and no way to join them from the stones alone.
> **The DM knowing it is the point: it governs how these two crypts are described, and what a question about them is answered with.**

> **Two means of inquiry exist, and neither is a route to this fact.** **The ten statue spirits at K45, in Guide II, each answer one question, short and vague, with a 20% chance of being wrong** (printed, I6 p.19). **The helpful spirit on Table 7 answers one question honestly in 25 words or less** (printed, I6 p.12).
> **Neither is said to know anything about Endorovich, Marya or the man in the churchyard.** **They are mechanisms by which a question may be asked, not provisions that it will be answered.** **What any of them knows is the DM's adjudication**, and the statue spirits are wrong one time in five whatever they say.
> **Nothing in I6 makes this history discoverable.** **A DM who wants the party to have it is building a route, which is characterisation rather than attribution.** **A DM who leaves it buried has run the module as written.**

**Crypt 14, 15 wights** (printed, I6 p.28): **AC 5; MV 12"; HD 4+3; #AT 1; Dmg 1d4 plus drain 1 energy level; AL LE.** **"The wights can only be hit by magical weapons."**

**Printed here:** **the vault appears to be three times the size indicated by its walls.** **15 stone coffins with heavy lids, a wight in each.** **"Characters are teleported into these coffins (as the wights are teleported out) from the traps around the entrance to K86."** **"The remaining wights assist anyone inside a coffin trying to remove its lid, thus making the feat relatively easy."**

> **Fifteen wights, each draining a level, hit only by magical weapons.** **This is not a fight the module expects to be won by force.**

**What a silver weapon does here, since the two books differ.** **I6 prints "The wights can only be hit by magical weapons" (I6 p.28).** **MM p.100 gives wights generally as affected by "silver or magical weapons".** **I6's is the narrower rule and it governs this encounter**, on the same principle applied to the banshee's saving throw below: the module governs its own. **A silver weapon that is not also magical does nothing to these fifteen**, and a party that has been relying on silver since the werewolf at K75 needs telling.

**The one thing in the module that destroys a wight outright (MM p.100).** **"A *raise dead* spell destroys a wight."** **Crypt 30 is in these catacombs and holds the only scroll of *raise dead* in I6.** **A party that has taken it is carrying a weapon against this vault.**
- **It is a clerical scroll, so only a cleric can read it** (PHB p.27; DMG p.127), **and a cleric below 9th level risks failure, as at K78** (3d).
- **One scroll, one casting, one wight.** **It destroys the wight it is cast on and leaves fourteen**, so it is an answer to a single coffin or a single pursuer rather than to the vault.
- **Spending it here spends the module's only raising.** **That is the decision the room poses**, and the guide does not make it for the party: see 9c for what is lost.

**Crypt 20, 1 vampire** (printed, I6 p.28): **AC 2; MV 12"/18"; HD 8+3; hp 47; #AT 2; Dmg 1d6+4 plus drain 2 energy levels; AL CE.** **"This vampire is an old wife of Strahd's, a townsperson now under his control."**

> **This vampire is not the Table 7 vampire and the differences are printed.** **Table 7 prints AC 1 and #AT 1 (I6 p.12). Crypt 20 prints AC 2 and #AT 2.** **Two attacks of 1d6+4, each draining two levels, is four levels a round.** **Both are carried as printed.**

**Crypt 21, 1 banshee** (printed, I6 p.28): **AC 0; MV 15"; HD 7; hp 47; #AT 2; Dmg 1d6+2 with 2 levels energy drain; AL CE; SA keening, save vs. spells or die.**

**Printed here.** **Dwarves notice that the engraving on this stone door is newer than most of those found so far.** **"The Banshee inside attacks as soon as the crypt is opened."** **Her treasure is behind her in the crypt.** **"She may only cry her deadly keening once per day, and then only in total darkness."** **"The very sight of her acts as a *fear* spell unless a saving throw is made."**

**Her sight, worked as *fear*** (PHB p.76).
- **Each character who sees her saves on the spell column, once in the encounter (ruling); a save means no effect.**
- **A failure drops whatever is held at once on a d100 roll of 60% at 1st level, less 5% for each level above (35% at 6th, 15% at 10th, none from 13th).**
- **From the next round the character flees at full speed for 7 rounds, worked at her 7 Hit Dice as the caster's level (ruling).**

**Who she was (printed).** **Patrina was a Rhenari elf maiden** (I6 p.28, where she is called a gypsy elf maiden) **who, having learned in early life a great deal of the black arts, was nearly a match for Strahd's powers.** **She felt a great bond with Strahd and desired to become one of his wives.** **Strahd, ever willing, agreed, but before the final draining of spirit from her soul could take place, her own people stoned her to death in mercy.** **Strahd demanded, and got, the body.** **She then became the banshee spirit found here.**

> **Three sources give three different saving throws against the keen and they must not be merged.** **Crypt 21 prints "save vs. spells or die" (I6 p.28).** **Table 7 prints "save vs. deathray or die" (I6 p.12).** **MM p.50 prints "save versus magic or die on the spot."** **(ruling)** **Each encounter uses its own printed throw: crypt 21 uses spells, a Table 7 result uses deathray.** The module governs its own encounters.

> **The statistics differ too.** **Crypt 21: #AT 2, Dmg 1d6+2, and two levels of energy drain.** **Table 7: #AT 1, Dmg 1-8, and no drain at all.** **Patrina is substantially the more dangerous of the two.**

> **What the Monster Manual adds, where I6 is silent (MM p.50).** **SD +1 or better weapon to hit. Magic resistance 50%.** **Impervious to cold and electricity; unaffected by *charm*, *sleep* or *hold*.** **"Exorcism kills them."**

> **"Her own people stoned her to death in mercy" is the saddest line in I6** and it is printed in a bestiary entry. **Her treasure is behind her because she is guarding her own grave.**

**Crypt 27, 3 huge spiders** (printed, I6 p.28): **AC 6; MV 18"; HD 2+2; hp 16, 16, 11; #AT 1; Dmg 1-6 plus poison; AL N.**

> **The Monster Manual settles the poison and adds something worse (MM p.90).** **"All saving throws versus the poison of huge spiders are at +1 on the dice."** **The +1 is in the party's favour, and it is the only good news in the entry.**
>
> **A failed save is death, as with the giant spider's poison at K40 (ruling).**

> **"Note that these creatures surprise prey on a roll of 1-5 (out of 6)."** **That is a five in six chance of surprise**, against the ordinary one or two in six. **Three spiders in a sealed crypt that the party has just levered open will almost always act first**, and I6 gives them 1-6 plus poison on that free attack.

> **They are roving hunters rather than web builders** (MM p.90), **which is why this crypt has no webs in its description.**

**Crypt 31, 1 trapper** (printed, I6 p.29): **AC 3; MV 3"; HD 12; hp 62; #AT 4+; Dmg 4 plus victim's AC; AL N.** **"The floor is a trapper who attacks anyone that steps up to the apparent chest."**

> **The chest is bait and the floor is the monster.** **62 hit points and damage equal to 4 plus the victim's armour class.** **The higher the numerical armour class, the more damage the trapper inflicts.** A fighter in plate at AC 2 takes 6 points; a magic-user at AC 9 takes 13. **It punishes the least protected characters.**

**Crypt 38, 3 hellhounds** (printed, I6 p.29): **AC 4; MV 12"; HD 5; hp 29, 23, 23; #AT 1; Dmg 1-10 plus breath weapon; AL LE.**

**Printed here.** **"When this crypt is opened, three pairs of red glowing eyes stare out."** **These hellhounds do 5 points of damage at up to 10 feet from their scorching firebreath, or only 4 points if the character saves vs. dragonbreath.** **"They can use their breath once per round for as many rounds as they wish."**

> **Five points a round each, every round, with no limit.** **Fifteen points a round from breath alone before anyone closes.** The saving throw reduces it to twelve. **This is the most relentless damage output below the castle and it never runs out.**

**Crypt 39, 1 nightmare** (printed, I6 pp.29, 30): **AC -4; MV 15"/36"; HD 6+6; hp 29; #AT 3; Dmg 2d4 (fangs) / 2d4+2 / 2d4+2 (burning hooves); AL NE; SA breath weapon of smoke.**

**Printed here.** **"A nightmare inside rears up when the door is opened."** **"The nightmare's smoking breath obscures vision, blinds opponents, and chokes them temporarily, causing them to attack with a -2 penalty to hit and damage unless a saving throw is made."** **"This is Strahd's steed."** **"The steed usually leaves by flying up the shaft in the spiral staircase, K18a."**
- **The smoke is its breath weapon: the victim saves on the breath weapon column, and a failure means -2 to hit and damage for 1 round (ruling).**

> **AC -4 is the lowest armour class in the module, lower than Strahd's.** **A party of the module's level hits it on a 19 or 20.**

> **It usually leaves rather than fights, and it leaves by K18a.** **A party that opens crypt 39 has most likely just told Strahd where they are**, because his horse has gone up the shaft.

## 5. The four tombs, K85 to K88

### 5a. K85, Tomb of Sergei Von Zarovich

**Possible Fortunes location** (1b). **If the applicable reading names this room, Strahd is ordinarily found here, subject to his actual movements and his recovery state.** **Daylight does not remove him from it or confine him to a coffin: it is far beneath the surface** (1c).

**Printed, I6 p.30.** **"A peaceful stillness, a calm amid the storm, is felt here."** **In the centre of the tomb, a white marble slab holds the coffin of Sergei Von Zarovich.** **This quiet room is 50 feet long north to south and 30 feet across.** **To the north, behind the intricately inlaid coffin, there are three alcoves.** **"Beautifully carved statues stand in each alcove, just as the day they were placed there."**

**What is here (printed).** **"The coffin opens easily to the touch of any lawful good character."** **The body is clothed in shining *+2 plate mail* armour.** **Check the Fortunes of Ravenloft results for possible treasure and encounters here.**

> **A calm amid the storm, and the creatures of K84 cannot follow the party in here** (printed, I6 p.27). **This is a sanctuary and it is also the brother's grave.**

> **Only a lawful good character opens it.** That is the printed condition, and it is the same test as the curtain at K87. **The module keeps asking the party the same question.**

### 5b. K86, Tomb of Strahd Von Zarovich

**Possible Fortunes location, and the room his forced recovery depends on whatever the reading says** (1b). **If the applicable reading names K86, he is ordinarily found here, subject to his actual movements and his recovery state; if it names another location, the coffin is here regardless, and the eight-hour rule below is separate from the placement.** **Daylight does not remove him from it or confine him to a coffin: it is far beneath the surface** (1c).

**Printed, I6 p.30.** **"A darkness clouds this room and the essence of evil permeates the very air."** **The smell of freshly turned earth is here.** **This room appears to be 50 feet long from east to west and 30 feet across.** **There are three empty alcoves in the south wall.** **"Settled into the dirt on the floor, lies a shining black coffin of finely waxed wood. The coffin's fittings are of brilliant brass. The lid is closed."**

**The rule that matters (printed).** **"If Strahd has been reduced to 0 hit points within the previous 8 game hours, his body is in this coffin."** **Check the Fortunes of Ravenloft results for possible treasure and encounters here.**

**The alcoves (printed).** **Two of the three alcoves on the south wall are teleports. The centre alcove is non-magical.** **The east alcove is the entry point for a teleport from crypt 32; entering the east alcove does nothing.** **The west alcove teleports whoever enters it to the west alcove in crypt 32.**

> **Eight game hours is the clock the whole adventure runs on, and this room is where those hours are spent** (6b). **Drive him to 0 hit points anywhere, reach this room within 8 hours, and he is in the coffin and helpless.** **It is an opportunity, not an outcome: the party still has to survive the journey here, find the room, and act inside the window.**

> **The smell of freshly turned earth is because the coffin sits in soil.** **A vampire rests in its native earth** and the dirt on the floor is the detail that tells the party they have found the right room.

### 5c. K87, Guardians

**Printed, I6 p.30.** **Wide steps descend to a landing flanked by two alcoves.** **Within each alcove, standing the full 30-foot height of the ceiling, is a bronze statue holding a spear.** **"A soft blue curtain of light flows between the two alcoves."** **Dimly visible on the other side of the light curtain are more descending stairs.**

**The rule, printed.** **"Anyone of lawful good alignment can pass through the curtain without difficulty."** **"All other alignments are teleported back to the top of the stairs behind them."**

> **This is a hard gate and it is not a puzzle.** **Lawful good passes. Everything else is sent back.** **Not harmed, not trapped, simply refused.** **A party with no lawful good member cannot reach K88 this way at all**, and I6 prints no alternative route to it.

> **The statues never act.** They are described at length and do nothing. **The curtain does the work.**

### 5d. K88, Tomb of Barov and Ravenovia

**Possible Fortunes location** (1b). **If the applicable reading names this room, Strahd is ordinarily found here, subject to his actual movements and his recovery state.** **Daylight does not remove him from it or confine him to a coffin: it is far beneath the surface** (1c).

**Printed, I6 p.30.** **"This tomb rests in hushed silence."** **Great stained windows filling the eastern walls filter dim light into this room.** **A closed coffin stands on each side of this roughly 40-foot-square room.** **Check the Fortunes of Ravenloft results for possible treasure and encounters here.**

> **His parents.** **Barov and Ravenovia are named on Crypt 30's epitaph as "King Barov and Queen Raven"**, which the Prefect was beloved of. **The catacombs' creatures cannot follow the party here** (printed, I6 p.27).

> **Stained glass filtering light, 400 feet underground.** **I6 does not explain it and this guide does not either.**

## 6. The confrontation with Strahd, and how it can end

### 6a. Strahd's statistics, carried here in full

**Printed, I6 p.3 and Table 7, p.12.** **Count Strahd Von Zarovich, styled "The First Vampyr."** **AC -1; MV 12"/18"; HD 10; hp 55; #AT 1; Dmg 5-10 (1d6+4) plus drain 2 life energy levels; strength 18/76; AL CE.** **He is a 10th level magic-user.**

**To hit, 10 Hit Dice, +2 for Strength 18/76:** AC 10 on -2, AC 8 on 0, AC 6 on 2, AC 4 on 4, AC 2 on 6, AC 0 on 8, AC -1 on 9. **(Guide ruling: I6 p.3 prints the Strength and applies its damage bonus in his 1d6+4, so the same row's attack bonus is applied here; the interaction of a monster's matrix with an ability bonus is not printed anywhere.)** **The damage already includes the bonus. Do not add it twice.**

**Saves, the better of fighter 9 to 10 and magic-user 6 to 10, since he has a magic-user's abilities:** paralysis, poison or death magic 8; petrification or polymorph 9; rod, staff or wand 9; breath weapon 9; spell 10. ***Sleep*, *charm* and *hold* do not affect him, nor do poison or paralysis** (MM p.99).

**Printed abilities (I6 p.3).** **Charm by gaze: he charms at will by gazing into a character's eyes, causing a saving throw versus spells at a penalty of -2.** **Regeneration: 3 hit points per round.** **Gaseous form at will.** **Shape change into a large bat.**

> **The full treatment of his spells, his goals and his conduct outside the castle is in Guide I, section 1b, and Guide II, section 1b.** **What is carried here is what is needed to finish him.**

### 6b. Reducing him to 0 hit points is not killing him

**Printed, MM p.99.** **A vampire brought to 0 hit points by combat is not killed.** **It is forced into gaseous form and must reach its coffin within 12 turns, rest there 8 hours and reform.** **It keeps regenerating 3 hit points a round throughout, and regaining hit points does not release it** (section 9h).

**I6's own rule (printed, I6 p.30).** **"If Strahd has been reduced to 0 hit points within the previous 8 game hours, his body is in this coffin"**, K86.

> **The two rules fit together into the party's best opportunity, which is not the same as a guaranteed outcome.** **Drive him to 0 anywhere. He goes gaseous and makes for this room. If he reaches it, his body lies in the black coffin for 8 hours.** **A party that reaches it inside that window finds him helpless.** **A party that does not has driven him off and nothing more.**

> **Twelve turns is two hours of game time**, and that is how long he takes to arrive. **A party that drops him in the Study at K37 and takes the K83 stair down can plausibly beat him to the coffin or arrive while he is still helpless.**

### 6c. The printed methods of destruction

**From the Monster Manual (MM p.99).** **"Exposure to direct sunlight kills the creature in 1 turn, and it becomes powerless immediately."** **"A vampire immersed in running water for 3 melee rounds is killed. The vampire loses one-third of its hit points per round of immersion."** **"If a wooden stake is driven through a vampire's heart it is killed, but only for so long as the stake remains; to finish this task the vampire's head must also be cut off and its mouth filled with holy wafers."**

**From I6 itself (p.30).** **"Remember that vampires are destroyed by exposure to direct sunlight for 10 rounds."**

> **The two agree, and the apparent difference is only in units.** **A turn is ten rounds.** **MM p.99's "1 turn" and I6 p.30's "10 rounds" are the same duration**, so no ruling is required. **What the Monster Manual adds is that the vampire is powerless immediately**, from the first round of exposure, **not only at the end of the ten.**

> **Running water is three rounds, not one**, and it works by taking a third of the vampire's hit points each round. **Against Strahd's 55 that is roughly 18 a round.**

**Repelled, not damaged (printed, MM p.99).** **Strong garlic makes a vampire hesitate 1 to 4 rounds.** **A mirror, or a lawful good holy symbol presented boldly, makes it position itself so the object is not between it and its intended victim, or have one of its creatures remove the device.** **None of these harms it or drives it off.**

> **Only garlic buys the party rounds, and the difference decides what holding up a mirror is worth.** **A mirror or a symbol moves him; it does not stop him.** A party that presents one has changed where he is standing and has not bought itself time.

**The invitation (printed, I6 p.6).** **A vampire cannot enter a building until someone invites him in.** **The rule is I6's, not the Monster Manual's**, and the full scope of it, including that any person may issue the invitation and that it holds for that building afterwards, is carried in Guide I. **Below the castle it protects nobody: there are no homes down here.**

**Holy water (printed, MM p.99).** **"Holy water splashed upon a vampire causes 2-7 hit points of damage per vial-full which strikes the monster."** **It applies to a direct hit and not to a vampire in gaseous form.**

**Throwing it (printed, DMG pp.63, 64).** **A vial is a grenade-like missile: roll to hit as for any missile, against the target's armour class, at range 3", -2 beyond 1" and -5 beyond 2".** **A hit must then break the vial: roll its item saving throw (9k), and only a vial that breaks does damage.** **Every creature within 3 feet of the break saves against poison or is splashed; a splashed vampire takes 2.** **A miss lands 1d6 feet off target (1d4 at short range, optionally), in the direction given by 1d8: 1 long right, 2 right, 3 short right, 4 short, 5 short left, 6 left, 7 long left, 8 long.** **Thrown at a wall, read long as high and short as low.**

### 6d. The two items built for this fight

**The *Holy Symbol of Ravenkind* (printed, I6 p.30).** **An ancient platinum medallion once worn by the High Priest of Ravenloft, a powerful lawful good symbol.** **Shaped like the sun, with a large crystal embedded in its centre, and around the crystal many holy symbols of light and truth.** **"When presented forcefully toward any undead creature, the medallion adds +2 to the cleric's turn undead roll."** **"When presented against vampires, however, it flares with the light of the sun for 1-10 rounds."** **"Vampires cannot move or attack while the medallion flares."** **"Other creatures are not affected by the medallion."** **"The medallion can only be used once per week."**

> **Put the two printed numbers side by side.** **The medallion flares for 1-10 rounds. Vampires are destroyed by direct sunlight for 10 rounds.** **A roll of 10 destroys Strahd outright, and he cannot move or attack while it burns.** **That is a one in ten chance of ending the adventure with a single action.**

> **Any other roll still buys the party that many rounds in which he can neither move nor attack**, which is the most valuable thing in the fight even when it does not kill him. **(ruling)** The flare is sunlight for its duration, and a partial flare contributes those rounds toward the 10 if the party can produce more sunlight; **nothing printed says the count resets, and nothing printed says it carries, so the DM decides before play.**

> **Once per week, and it is +2 to turning, not +4.** **The *Icon of Ravenloft* at K15, in Guide II, is the +4 item and it does not flare.** **They are two different artefacts and a party that confuses them brings the wrong one to the coffin.**

**Printed properties (I6 p.31).** **The *sunsword* is a magical longsword.** **Its blade is a form of crystal glassteel, held by a platinum hilt and guard.** **It glows with dim blue light most of the time.** **"The *sunsword* is a +2 sword against all creatures except undead."** **"When within 30 feet of any undead, the blade suddenly glows brilliant blue."** **"The blade is +3 vs. undead (to hit and damage)."** **"When used against vampires, the blade flares and sparks when it hits, inflicting 10 additional points of damage per hit."**

**How the party comes to have it (printed, I6 p.31).** Strahd once employed a magician to destroy the sword, a process requiring **the hilt and blade to be separated, then destroyed simultaneously.** **The magician's assistant stole the blade and fled; his body was found in the woods without it.** **"When it was separated from the hilt, the blade took on the appearance of a normal longsword."** **The hilt remained in Castle Ravenloft and the blade was never found.** **"Strahd has always lived in fear that the blade may be reunited with the hilt."** **"It so happens that one of the PCs has been using the blade with a normal hilt (since without the hilt it cannot be discerned from a normal blade)."** **"If the PCs find the hilt, it will magically leap onto the blade, replacing the PC's hilt, becoming the *sunsword*."**

**What the Fortunes reading does and does not decide.** **The reading fixes where the hilt is found** (printed, I6 p.4). **It does not limit where the weapon can be used.** **Once the hilt and blade are joined, the *sunsword* is an adventure-wide resource and matters wherever undead are met.**

**The two bonuses are separate, and the distinction decides more than it appears to.** **Against undead it is +3 to hit and damage, with 10 extra points per hit against vampires.** **Against anything that is not undead it is +2.** **The two iron golems at K78 are not undead**, so the *sunsword* strikes them at +2 as printed and does no damage, **since nothing under +3 harms them** (printed, I6 p.26; MM p.48).

**Optional accommodation for an established character (guide ruling).**

> **This is a deliberate change to I6 rather than a reading of it**, recorded so a DM can adopt it knowingly. **The printed properties above are what the module says.** **It is not applied without the DM's agreement and it is not presented to players as an I6 rule.**
>
> **The problem.** I6 assumes a player character has carried the blade all along as an ordinary longsword. **For a character whose sword is already better than +2, taking up the adventure's signature weapon is a downgrade against everything that is not undead.**
>
> **The accommodation.** **Where the character who takes up the *sunsword* gives up a magical sword of a higher bonus, the DM may allow the *sunsword* to match that higher bonus against ordinary opponents, while keeping its own properties against undead.** **A character surrendering a +3 longsword wields it as +3 against ordinary opponents instead of the printed +2, and the printed one-point differential carries, making it +4 against undead.** **The 10 extra points per hit against vampires are unchanged.**
>
> **Three limits.**
> 1. **Swords only, and only the sword actually given up.** The accommodation exists because accepting the *sunsword* costs that character their current weapon. **A character who keeps a better weapon of another kind has surrendered nothing and gains nothing**, and a better blade kept in the pack does not qualify.
> 2. **It belongs to that character's use of the weapon, not to the weapon.** **The *sunsword* is not permanently improved, and another character taking it up wields it at the printed +2.**
> 3. **The adjusted bonus is the real bonus, including for overcoming special defences.** **A *sunsword* adjusted to +3 does damage the iron golems at K78.** **That is accepted rather than worked around.** **The three +3 maces at K41 are a provision for a party without a qualifying weapon, not a restriction needing protection**: a party arriving with its own +3 weapons could always damage the golems, and I6 never said otherwise.

> **One of the player characters has been carrying the blade the whole time and nobody knows, including the player.** **This is the best reveal in the module and it requires the DM to have decided in advance whose longsword it is.**

**Where it decides the climax.** **Ten extra points a hit against vampires, on top of +3 to hit and damage, against Strahd's 55 hit points is roughly four hits.** **It is the difference between a winnable fight and an unwinnable one**, and it applies to Helga, to the vampire in crypt 20 and to every Table 7 vampire as well. **The catacombs are almost entirely undead**, so the +3 line governs crypt 4's ghost, crypt 7's spectre, crypt 14's fifteen wights and crypt 21's banshee. **The two exceptions below the castle are the iron golems at K78 and the trapper in crypt 31**, neither of them undead, where the weapon is +2 and the golems take nothing from it as printed.

### 6e. The Tome of Strahd

**Printed, I6 p.31.** **An ancient work penned by Strahd himself, a tragic tale of how he came to his fallen state.** **Bound in a thick black leather cover with brass hinges and fastenings.** **The pages are of parchment and very brittle.** **"Most of the book is written in the curious shorthand that only Strahd himself employs."** **"Stains and age have made most of the work illegible."** **"There is a base 30% chance to read any paragraph in the book."** **The paragraphs are read in order.**

> **Thirty per cent per paragraph, read in order.** **A party will get a broken, partial account**, which is the point: the Tome is a voice, not a briefing.

> **The Tome of Strahd is never treated as authoritative rules evidence.** **It is Strahd's own account of himself.** **His statements may be true, false, incomplete or self-serving, and nothing in it overrides the Monster Manual or I6's own rules.** **Where the Tome appears to describe a mechanic, the mechanic is read from the rules and the Tome is read as character.**

> **What it does establish, in his own words, is the shape of the tragedy**: that he was a warrior and just, that the killing years wore down his soul, that he brought his family to Ravenloft, that he hated his younger brother Sergei for his youth and his looks, that Tatyana spurned him and was betrothed to Sergei, and that when she called him "brother" he saw his own death reflected in her eyes. **The optional ending at section 8 is the other half of that story.**

### 6f. Five ways this can end, and only one of them is victory

**I6 provides no guaranteed outcome.** **The adventure can end with the characters victorious, with Strahd victorious, or with the survivors withdrawing without resolving anything.** **He can also be beaten more than once without being destroyed.** **The five states below are distinct and the guide keeps them distinct.**

**1. Temporary defeat.** **He is reduced to 0 hit points, forced to gaseous form, reaches his coffin within 12 turns and rests 8 hours** (printed, MM p.99). **He is not harmed in any lasting way.** **A party that drove him off and did not follow has bought itself a day.**

**2. Forced recovery interrupted.** **He is reduced to 0 and the party reaches K86 inside the 8 hours** (printed, I6 p.30). **His body is in the coffin and the destruction methods at section 6c apply to a vampire that cannot resist them.**

**3. Permanent destruction.** **By the printed methods**: ten rounds of direct sunlight, three rounds of immersion in running water, or stake, decapitation and holy wafers (MM p.99; I6 p.30). **Or by failing to reach the coffin within 12 turns** (ruling, section 6b), **which a cleric can cause by turning the gas and keeping it away until the deadline passes** (9a).

**4. His victory.** **He achieves his object without necessarily killing anyone.** **His printed goals are the Fortunes reading's business, and taking Ireena is one of them.** **Crypt 18 stands empty with her name newly cut into it** (printed, I6 p.28).

**5. Unresolved.** **The survivors withdraw, or never reach him, or cannot find him.** **The valley is still sealed, because the gates open only when Strahd is destroyed** (printed, I6 pp.5, 6, 7). **There is no printed provision for a party that simply stops.**

> **Withdrawing from down here is not the same as leaving.** **Check the closure field before describing the way out** (9f): **the castle's own printed line is that entering the keep once closes the way out**, and a party that came in that way finds the drawbridge up and the portcullis down whatever they decide below (Guide II, 3a). **The valley gates are a second, separate seal.**

> **A total party kill is not the same as Strahd's victory.** **The party can be destroyed by the Guardian of Sorrow, by a fall down K18a, by the hellhounds in crypt 38, by the trapper, or by their own decisions, with Strahd nowhere near.** **Conversely he can take what he came for and leave them alive.**

> **What the guide does not supply.** **I6 leaves open what Strahd does with prisoners, bodies or survivors, and no epilogue is invented here.** **The DM decides, and the only printed constraint is that the gates stay shut.**

### 6g. He can be destroyed without the party ever knowing

**This follows from two printed rules and one of this guide's own.**

**The mechanism.** **At 0 hit points he is forced to gaseous form and must reach his coffin within 12 turns** (printed, MM p.99). **This guide rules that missing that deadline destroys him**, as does a coffin destroyed before he arrives (ruling, section 6b). **Nothing printed requires the party to witness any of it.**

**Why they may not be able to tell.** **I6 p.30 puts his body in the K86 coffin only if he was reduced to 0 within the previous 8 game hours.** **So an empty coffin is consistent with two opposite situations: he is elsewhere and active, or he is gone.** **The party cannot distinguish them by looking.**

**What they will actually observe.** **The attacks stop.** **The nightly harassment in Barovia ends, the strikes end, and nothing comes for them.** **They reach the location the Fortunes reading named, and any other of the eight they can get to, and he is in none of them.** **A party that has been hunted for days reads a sudden silence as patience, not as absence.**

> **Played this way it is more ominous than a body would be.** **They may conclude he is avoiding them, that he is waiting, that he has gone to do something worse.** **They may leave the valley believing they failed**, which they would discover is wrong only when the gates open, **because the gates are controlled by his will and open when he is destroyed** (printed, I6 pp.5, 6, 7).

> **That printed gate rule is the one honest signal available**, and the DM may let the party find it or not. **It is also the reason the uncertainty cannot last forever.**

**And the gas itself can be destroyed, which makes the silent ending likelier than it looks.** **Magical fire does normal damage to a vampire in gaseous form, lightning half, a whirlwind double** (section 9h). **A party that drops him and then drops a *fireball* on the gas may destroy him outright in the same round and have no way of telling.** **The gas leaves no body, and a destroyed vampire does not arrive at K86 to be found.**

### 6h. What ending the adventure requires

**The printed conditions, gathered.** **Strahd reduced to 0 hit points, which forces him gaseous.** **His body then lies in the coffin at K86 for 8 game hours** (I6 p.30). **Destruction by stake, decapitation and holy wafers, by running water, or by 10 rounds of direct sunlight** (MM p.99; I6 p.30).

> **The guide makes no ruling on what the party must do at the coffin beyond those printed methods**, because I6 prints none. **A staked, beheaded vampire whose mouth is filled with holy wafers is destroyed by the Monster Manual's own words**, and that is the procedure the module assumes.

> **The optional ending requires Ireena to be with the party** (printed, I6 p.30), **and it is set at dawn on the overlook, K6, in Guide II.**

## 8. The optional ending

**Printed, I6 p.30.** **"After Strahd is destroyed forever, you may want to provide an ending for the players to wrap things up."** **The scenario is set at dawn on the overlook, K6**, which is in Guide II, section 3c. **"Ireena Kolyana must be with the PCs."**

**What happens, printed.** Thick clouds overhead, the lands of Barovia visible far below through chilly morning mists. **"There is a peacefulness here. Rest has come to the valley for the first time anyone can remember. There is sleep without fear."** **A light flashes brilliantly behind the party.** **A shining stately man in white armour and cape**, his rugged features showing great strength of will, **"yet the forcefulness of his presence is tempered by his calm, sad eyes."** **"His features are those of Strahd, yet subtly different."**

**"My name is Sergei von Zarovich."** He turns to Ireena: **"Tatyana, the time is at hand to rest. Come, my love and wife."** He stretches forth his hand. **Ireena's questioning eyes suddenly open with recognition and knowledge.** **Forgotten memories rush upon her.** **"Sergei!" she cries, springing to him with the grace of a doe.** They embrace.

**Ireena turns to the party.** **"I am Ireena Kolyana, but in my past I was Sergei's beloved Tatyana. Through these many centuries we have played out the tragedy of our lives. Now, with our deepest gratitude to you, that tragedy is over. It is time for joy to begin again."**

**Shimmering light surrounds them.** **Hand in hand they walk east toward the edge of the overlook.** **Their feet do not touch the ground; they tread a path beyond this mortal world.** **Their invisible road takes them beyond the eastern precipice, their glow illuminating and thinning the clouds above Barovia.** **"The clouds suddenly break open, letting glorious shafts of sunlight flood through. In the valley below, the strange fog dissolves, loosing its power. Barovia is free once more."**

> **This is where the Tome of Strahd pays off, and the two texts should be read together before running it.** **The Tome names Tatyana, names Sergei, and says she was betrothed to him.** **A party that read the Tome at 30% a paragraph has heard half of this already, in Strahd's own bitter voice.** **Here they hear the other half.**

> **It requires Ireena alive and present**, which makes every decision about her from Guide I onward a decision about whether this ending is available. **A party that left her in the village, or let her die, has finished the adventure but not this scene.**

> **"The strange fog dissolves, loosing its power."** **The fog at the border, which has sat around the lungs of everyone in the valley since Guide I, is gone.** **The gates that would not open until Strahd was destroyed** (printed, I6 pp.5, 6, 7) **no longer matter. Barovia is open.**

## 9. Procedures

### 9a. Turning undead

**The procedure (printed, DMG p.76).** **"A d20 is rolled, and if the number shown is matched or exceeded on the die roll the undead are turned."** **Each cleric present may try.** **From 1 to 12 undead are affected, or 7 to 12 where the matrix shows an asterisk.** **A good cleric drives them directly away, to stay as far away as possible, for not less than 3 nor more than 12 rounds.**

**After a failed attempt, no further attempt by that cleric against that particular undead** (DMG p.76). **This guide reads that bar as lasting for the rest of the encounter** (ruling).

#### Mixed groups and multiple forms

**The two printed provisions (DMG p.65).**

**Multiple forms.** **"If the cleric attempting the turning is successful against any or all types within the group of multiple forms of undead, that type or multiple types, to the maximum number indicated by the dice roll or otherwise indicated by the rules, are turned, and on the next round the cleric so successful may attempt to turn other undead of the group. This process may continue as long as each successive attempt is successful and the cleric lives."** **Those turned run "lowest hit dice types to highest hit dice types", and "Any failure to turn undead disallows a further attempt by the same cleric."**

**The disallowance.** **"If the undead are in a mixed group ... you may opt to disallow any turning or other effect if the most powerful member ... is not affected by the cleric."** **It applies "only to groups of mixed undead where the lesser are following or serving the greater", and "Mindless undead, skeletons and zombies, cannot be considered."** **The word is "may opt": the disallowance is the DM's option, not an automatic consequence.**

**Strahd zombies under both provisions (ruling).** **I6 changes how Strahd zombies are turned, not what they are.** **I6 p.32 prints INTELLIGENCE non- and HIT DICE 4, and prints only that they are "turned as mummies".** **The mummy row is therefore a turning number and nothing else.**

| Undead | Turning row used | Mixed-group treatment | Hit Dice |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strahd zombie | Mummy | Mindless zombie, excluded from the disallowance | 4 |

- **Turning numbers.** **They are turned on the mummy row, as I6 specifies.**
- **The disallowance.** **They remain mindless undead and cannot serve as the most powerful member on which a disallowance rests.** **Using the mummy row does not make them intelligent undead.**
- **The sequence.** **The mummy row does not change their actual Hit Dice.** **The chain proceeds by actual Hit Dice, lower to higher, while attempts succeed.** **DMG p.65 treats table order and Hit Dice order as the same thing; for Strahd zombies they are not, and this guide follows the Hit Dice** (ruling).

> **What this means when Strahd arrives with his zombies.** **A cleric who cannot turn Strahd may still turn the Strahd zombies with him.** **Strahd's presence does not protect his mindless servants from turning**, because the disallowance needs a most powerful member whom the lesser are following or serving, and mindless undead cannot be considered for it. **The DM keeps the option where Strahd is accompanied by undead that are not mindless.**

**Presentation.** **Strahd zombies are never relabelled as mummies.** **Only their turning numbers come from that row.** **Their encounter descriptions and statistics stay as the printed Strahd zombie entries.**

**Paladins turn as a cleric two levels below their own level** (printed, DMG p.75).

> **"Turn as mummies" sets the row and nothing else.** **Strahd zombies do not gain a mummy's fear, paralysis, disease or weapon resistance.**

> **Below the castle the turning spread is extreme.** **The ten skeletons at K69 fall to a 1st level cleric on a 10.** **The fifteen wights in crypt 14 need 20, 19 or 16 at the first three levels.** **The spectres in crypt 7 need 5th level to attempt at all, the ghost in crypt 4 needs 7th, and the shadow demon needs 8th.**

**Turning a vampire that is already in gaseous retreat (ruling).** **A good cleric can turn the retreating gas.** **It must move directly away from the cleric and stay as far away as possible, at full speed, for the turning's duration.**
- **The twelve turns run on throughout.** Turning does not pause the deadline; it spends it.
- **The cleric's position fixes the direction.** The gas chooses its path within that, round obstacles and through the openings available, and where the direct way is blocked it keeps as far from the cleric as the openings allow.
- **When it comes back, the cleric may turn it again**, "only once by each cleric" being read as once for each approach.
- **Once it has reached its coffin, turning no longer moves it.**
- **If turning keeps it from its coffin until the deadline passes, it is destroyed.**

> **This is a way to destroy Strahd, and it is the only one that needs no weapon.** **A cleric who can turn a vampire, standing between the gas and the route home, is not driving him off: he is running down the clock.** **The turning numbers are in the matrix above**, and nobody below 6th level can attempt it.

### 9b. Energy drain

**What a drain takes (DMG p.119).** The level, the Hit Dice, **the hit points that went with them including any Constitution bonus for the lost level**, and the attack and saving throw capability of that level.

**What drains what, in this guide.** **The fifteen wights of crypt 14 drain one level.** **The spectre of crypt 7, the vampire of crypt 20, the banshee of crypt 21 and Strahd drain two.**

**A character drained to 0 level is alive** (DMG p.119) **and is "never capable of gaining experience again"** unless restored. **A further drain kills them.**

**What creates a new undead.** **Hit point damage alone never does** (ruling). **A human totally drained by a wight becomes a half-strength wight under its slayer's control; by a spectre, a half-strength spectre under the control of the spectre that drained him** (MM pp.100, 89, and printed again at I6 p.27). **A victim drained of all life energy by a vampire becomes "an appropriately strengthed vampire under control of its slayer"**, and **"this transformation takes place 1 day after the creature is buried, but if and only if the creature is buried"** (printed, MM p.99). **An unburied victim does not rise.** **The rule covers any human or humanoid, not humans alone.**

> **And destroying Strahd frees them.** **"If the vampire which slew the creature is itself killed, the vampires created by it become free-willed monsters"** (printed, MM p.99). **Helga at K32, Sasha Ivliskova in crypt 20 and the Table 7 vampires are all his.** **Killing him does not destroy them; it releases them from his control.** **(ruling)** They remain vampires and remain dangerous, and **a DM who wants the valley clean at the end deals with them before the coffin, not after.**

**Half-strength, which the Monster Manual does not define (ruling).** Half the standard creature's Hit Dice, any fraction or bonus rounded up, **hit points rolled on the reduced dice**, other abilities unchanged.

**Restoring a lost level (PHB p.53).** ***Restoration* raises the life energy level by one**, so four levels need four castings. **Only effective if cast within one day per level of the caster of the loss.** **A 7th level clerical spell.** **Casting it ages the caster 2 years** (DMG p.13).

### 9b-bis. Resting, and regaining spells

**What the rules require (DMG p.40).** **A caster needs complete rest, usually sleep, before regaining spells.** **Its length is set by the highest spell level being regained: four hours for 1st and 2nd level, six hours for 3rd and 4th.** **After the rest, a quarter of an hour of study or prayer per spell level, for each spell.** **Recovery is not tied to night** (interpretation): a party that rests by day regains spells by day.

**The rest must be uninterrupted (ruling).** **An interrupted rest does not part-count**: the caster begins again.

**An encounter during rest is not the same thing as an interruption, and the distinction decides whether spellcasters ever recover (ruling).**
- **A passing encounter does not interrupt rest.** Something that goes by, or is heard and does not come, costs nothing.
- **An encounter that reaches and disturbs the occupants does interrupt it.**
- **Hostile creatures attempting entry interrupt it whether or not they get in.**
- **A report is not an automatic interruption.** What Strahd learns is not by itself an event in the room.

> **Why the distinction matters below the castle.** **The creatures of K84 pursue anywhere except K85, K86 and K87** (1c), **so a party that has woken something cannot rest in the catacombs at all until it is dealt with or a tomb is reached.** **A tomb that breaks pursuit is therefore also the only place down here a rest can reliably finish.** **There is no wandering-encounter check below the castle**: what interrupts a rest down here is what the party has already woken, and the fixed encounters they have left behind them.

### 9c. Death, raising and the ghost exception

**What I6 provides below the castle.** **The scroll of *raise dead* in Crypt 30, and nothing else.** **Madam Eva has the spell and will not use it** (printed, I6 p.11). **Donavich is 2nd level.**

***Raise dead* (PHB p.50).** The subject **must not be an elf.** **A body dead no longer than one day per level of the caster.** **The raised character returns at 1 hit point** (ruling: PHB p.50 states no hit point figure) **and weak**, needing one full day of rest per day dead before being able to act. **The body must be whole enough.** **Casting it ages the cleric 2 years** (DMG p.13).

**Before the victim rises** (ruling; MM p.44). **A pending victim whom the spell successfully restores before the rise returns alive and does not rise.** **If it fails, or cannot affect the victim, the rise stands.** **A ghoul's victim must first be blessed**, and that requirement applies only before the rise: **a risen ghoul falls under the provision below.** **A failed *raise dead* on an exhumed body does not make it a vampire.** (Ruling.)

**And it reaches the newly risen** (printed, PHB p.50). **“Newly made *undead*, excluding skeletons, which fall within the days of being dead limit are affected by *raise dead* spells cast upon them. The effect of the spell is to cause them to become resurrected dead, providing the constitution permits survival; otherwise, they are simply dead.”** **So a victim who has already risen is not beyond restoration.** If the rise falls inside the caster's days-dead limit, the spell ends the undead state and **the resurrection survival roll decides whether the person returns alive or stays dead**. **Skeletons are excluded.** **Read alongside the Monster Manual, the spell also destroys a wight and slays a wraith** (MM pp.100, 102); that reading is a guide ruling, the provision above is printed. (Ruling.)

**What level the restored character returns at** (ruling; MM p.102, PHB p.50). **A drained victim restored, before or after the rise, returns at the level held immediately before the fatal draining attack; earlier losses stay lost.** **The remains must be accessible to the caster, and a buried body is exhumed first.** **An elf risen as a wraith is slain by the spell but cannot be restored**: the creature is destroyed and the elf stays dead. (Ruling.)

***Raise dead* has a second use and the catacombs put it in reach.** **"A *raise dead* spell destroys a wight"** (MM p.100). **Crypt 14 holds fifteen of them**, and the ruling on what that scroll will and will not do there is at 4d. **The scroll holds one casting, and one casting reaches one target.** **It cannot both destroy a wight here and raise a dead companion elsewhere**, which is the resource question this section turns on. **That is not a rule that a single casting cannot produce both outcomes for one target.** **Where the target is a newly made undead creature within the caster's days-dead limit, the same casting that ends the undead state is what restores the person**, under the provision at the *raise dead* entry: one casting, one target, two faces, and no second casting is granted by it. **The wights standing in Crypt 14 are not this party's newly made dead**, so there the choice is the resource choice stated here.

***Resurrection* (PHB p.52).** Restores the subject fully and **requires a resurrection survival roll on Constitution.** **Each resurrection permanently costs one point of Constitution.** **Casting it ages the cleric 3 years** (DMG p.13).

**Using a scroll found in play.** **A cleric scroll can be used by a cleric.** **A magic-user cannot use a clerical scroll.** **Thieves and scrolls.** **At 10th level or above, a thief may use scrolls of all kinds except clerical scrolls, including magic-user, illusionist and druidic scrolls** (PHB p.27; DMG p.127). **There is a 25% chance of misunderstanding the writing, with a further risk of mispronouncing a spell.** **Protection scrolls may be read by characters of any class** (DMG pp.127, 128). **A cleric below the level at which the spell can be cast may still try, at a risk of failure** (3d). **Scroll or device use causes no ageing** (printed, DMG p.13).

> **The exception that overrides all of this.** **"Any human, dwarf, elf, gnome or halfling that is killed by a ghost is dead forever"** (printed, I6 p.27, crypt 4). **No spell, scroll or item in the module reaches that death.**

**Bodies that cannot be recovered below the castle.** **A character teleported into a coffin in crypt 14 who is not got out.** **A character slid down K82 into a barred cell under five feet of water.** **Anyone who falls down K18a, 390 feet, from K59.** **A DM tracking a death tracks the body as well.**

**These apply to Strahd, to the vampire in crypt 20 and to any Table 7 vampire.**

**To hit.** **+1 or better weapon required** (MM p.99).

**Charm by gaze (printed, I6 p.3).** **Strahd charms at will by gazing into a character's eyes, saving throw versus spells at -2.**

**Regeneration (printed, I6 p.3).** **Strahd regenerates 3 hit points per round.**

**Forms (printed, I6 p.3).** **Gaseous form at will; shape change into a large bat.**

**At 0 hit points by combat (MM p.99).** **Not killed. Forced into gaseous form.** **The full procedure, what harms the gas and when damage destroys it are at section 9h.**

**Destruction, repulsion and holy water.** See section 6c.

### 9e. Falling and the drops below the castle

| Where | Printed distance | Damage |
|---|---|---|
| **K18a**, from K59 to K84 | 390 feet | **20d6** |
| **K82**, the marble slide | into cell e of K74 | **none printed; a capture, not an injury** (ruling) |
| **K81**, through the trap door | onto the K82 chute | **as K82** |
| **K31a**, riding the compartment roof | crushed within one round | **3-30 (3d10)** |

**Voluntary gaseous form, which is not the forced retreat (ruling).** **He takes gaseous form at will, and that is a different state from the one he is forced into at 0 hit points.**
- **He is not bound to any retreat and can resume another form at will.**
- **He makes no blow, and so drains no levels. He uses no gaze. He casts no spell with a verbal component.**
- ***Polymorph self* is a spell and is distinct from the innate gaseous and bat forms.**
- **He suffers the same damage in voluntary gas as in forced gas**: magical fire at normal damage, lightning at half, a whirlwind at double, and nothing else.

> **So gaseous form is movement, concealment and escape, and it is never an attack.** **A DM who lets him drain or gaze as a cloud has given him a round he does not have.**

**What a round costs him when he casts (ruling).**
- **Whether he can cast at all (DMG p.65).** Spells **"cannot be cast while violently moving ... such as running, dodging a blow, or even walking normally"**; casting **"requires that a figure be relatively motionless and concentrating"**.
- **What casting costs him (ruling).** **He makes no physical attack, and does not use his gaze, in a round in which he casts a spell.** **A spell spoiled early in the round still takes the round: he gains no gaze later in it.**

> **This is his action economy and it decides the climax, and it is narrower than it first looks.** **A blow and a gaze in the same round are permitted**: he turns his gaze on one character a round **in addition to his blow** (ruling). **What casting excludes is both of them.** **A round in which he casts is a round with no blow and no gaze**, and **a spell spoiled early in it is still a round he has spent.**

**What he summons, and when it arrives (printed, MM p.99).** **In the wilds, typically 3 to 18 wolves.** **Below ground, usually 10 to 100 rats or bats.** **They arrive within 2 to 12 melee rounds.** **The bats confuse and obscure opponents' vision rather than physically attacking.**

> **The delay belongs to the summoning and to nothing else.** **Creatures already present act at once**: the wolves that came with him, a garrison, anything the party has already met. **Only what he calls after the fight starts takes its 2 to 12 rounds to arrive.**

> **The bats do not attack, and a DM should not give them a bite.** What they do is obscure, which is worth more to him than damage: see the bats at the catacombs for what obscuring does to spellcasting.

### 9e-bis. Strahd's reports and his three strikes, which do not reset below ground

**These continue from Guides I and II without resetting.** **A party that reaches the catacombs may have already seen one or two of his three strikes spent**, and the report clock has been running since they entered the valley. **Arriving here gives him nothing back.**

**Printed, I6 p.3.** **His spies and servants report four times a day: dawn, noon, dusk and midnight.** **There is a 60% chance at each that he knows where the party is**, and **if he does he attacks wherever they are within two hours**, choosing the time and method.

**How this guide runs it (rulings).**
- **A report is an opportunity, not an obligation**, since I6's own principle is that he **"is allowed to make an attack how and where he wants"**. He may change the circumstances, decline, and need not replace an abandoned attack.
- **The reports reach him underground at their printed times.** While he rests, his servants hold routine reports until he is available, **may wake him for urgent ones, such as the party entering the castle or gaining a dangerous artefact**, and raise the rest when he wakes.
- **The two-hour window runs from the scheduled report time, even when the report is held.** Once it expires, the opportunity lapses.
- **Above ground he does not act by day** (MM p.99). **His servants carry out his orders by day; a personal attack above ground cannot come by day.**
- **Characters concealed where his servants cannot find them are not revealed by a report.** Characters who conceal themselves after one can be sought at their last known place but not found there while concealed.

**His three strikes, each usable once in the whole adventure (printed, I6 p.3).**
1. **He attacks a single character for 5 melee rounds, then leaves.**
2. **He calls 10 to 15 Strahd zombies**, deploying no more than survive in his reserve and any garrisons he withdraws: **the reserve first, then the shortfall from K76 and then K28.**
3. **He calls 3 to 12 worgs**, drawn from the valley's survivors.

**The Strahd zombie reserve (ruling).** **Roll 1d10 once before play: that is the reserve.** **The garrisons are two at K28 and six at K76, which stay in place unless moved in play.** **Strike 2 deploys no more than survive in the reserve and any garrisons withdrawn: the reserve first, then the shortfall from K76 and then K28.** **Withdrawn garrison zombies are recorded against their garrison while deployed; after a strike, surviving reserve zombies return to the reserve and surviving garrison zombies to their rooms, if able.** **The dead are never replaced, because no more can be made** (interpretation).

**The zombies, or the worgs, attack together in one place.** **A strike needs him to know where the party is**, from a report or from what he or his servants have learned in play. **A strike against a building that fails to get in is spent.** **Outside the first strike, no fixed duration is imposed on his combat**: he presses or withdraws as his goals and the circumstances dictate.

> **What is different down here.** **The daylight restriction does not help the party** (1c), so **a dawn or noon report can bring him in person** below ground where it could not above it. **And he is close**: his coffin is at K86 and by day he is far beneath the surface (ruling), so a report that locates the party in the catacombs locates them in the neighbourhood he is already in.

> **A strike spent below the castle is still spent.** **If the party withdraws and returns, he does not get it back.**

A time does.**

### 9f-bis. Making holy water, and whether the party can

> **Read this as a conditional, not as a service the adventure provides.** **The Dungeon Masters Guide establishes what a font can do. I6 does not establish that either church in the adventure has one.** **Everything below is available only if the DM decides a font exists**, and the section ends with what follows from each answer.

**Who can do it (printed, DMG p.114).** **"Only clerics, excluding druids, are able to prepare holy water."** **"As a third level spell is involved, it requires a cleric of not less than 5th level of experience to manage to create such fluid."**

> **Donavich is 2nd level and cannot, whatever he is given.** **A player character cleric of 5th level or above can.** The third level spell is *prayer*.

**The ritual (printed, DMG p.114).** **A specially blessed basin of fine workmanship and precious metal must be fashioned for the cleric**, engraved with the holy symbols of the deity, **and it must sit within a repository of finely crafted and carefully worked rare wood, with a base, pedestal, chest-like holder and lid, the whole being known as a font.** **The cleric, robed in formal vestments, casts the following spells in succession:** ***create water*; *purify food & drink* (the water); *bless*; *chant*, one full turn; *prayer*.**

**How much, by the metal of the basin (printed, DMG p.115):**

| Metal | Capacity | Basin cost | Font cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| copper | **6 vials** | 130 to 180 gp | 200 gp |
| silver | **10 vials** | 1,900 to 2,400 gp | 500 gp |
| electrum | **18 vials** | 8,000 to 12,000 gp | 1,000 gp |
| gold | **32 vials** | 19,000 to 22,000 gp | 1,500 gp |
| platinum | **50 vials** | 110,000 to 200,000 gp | 2,000 gp |

**Combination metal vessels are allowed, with capacity by interpolation** (printed). **Fonts and basins are designed and constructed on special order, the process taking 4-10 (2d4+2) weeks.**

**Vials (printed, DMG p.114).** **Once created, the water cannot leave the font for more than a turn without losing its efficacy unless placed in specially blessed vials of crystal, either rock or special leaded glass.** **Each empty vial is worth 2-5 gp.**

**How often (printed, DMG p.115).** **"Holy water or unholy water may be made but once per week."** **The ritual takes a full day of prayers and meditation, followed by the actual ritual, followed by a need for not less than 8 hours of rest and repose.** **"Only one holy/unholy water font per religious edifice is possible."**

**Defilement (printed, DMG p.115).** **If a non-believer blesses or curses the font, or uses less refined means such as excreting wastes into the font or basin, the whole is desecrated and unfit.** **It must be smelted down and remade in its entirety, at 20% to 50% of the costs above, taking 4 to 6 weeks.** **Either method requires actual contact: blessing or cursing from a distance is absolutely ineffectual.**

**The rule that matters most in this adventure (printed, DMG p.115).** **"Ingesting or bathing with holy water will have the beneficial effect of slowing the onset of lycanthropy or of becoming undead. For each vial so used, the process is slowed by from 1-4 turns."** **"This time delay is to be secretly determined and noted."**

> **Read that against what this valley does to people.** **A character drained to death by a vampire, or bitten by the werewolf in Guide III at K75, has a delay that the party can buy with vials**, and the DM rolls it in secret. **It does not cure anything.** It buys turns, 1 to 4 per vial, in which a cure might be found.

**Whether the party can make any in Barovia (ruling).**

> **I6 never mentions holy water or a font anywhere.** **Neither word appears in the module**, which was checked rather than assumed. **So whether the church in the village has a font is not printed and is the DM's to decide**, and the answer governs everything above.
>
> **If the church has a font, a player character cleric of 5th level or above can use it**, with Donavich's cooperation, **once per week, for the capacity of its basin.** **A village church in a valley this poor is a copper or silver basin, which is 6 or 10 vials a week**, and that is the realistic scale.
>
> **If it has no font, none can be made during the adventure.** **A basin and font are built on special order over 4 to 10 weeks**, in a valley nobody can leave, with no trade in precious metals. **The party cannot buy their way out of this one.**
>
> **The castle chapel at K15, in Guide II, is a second religious edifice**, and the one-font-per-edifice rule means it could have its own. **I6 prints nothing about that either.** It is a possibility the DM may use rather than a provision the module makes.

### 9g. Strahd zombies

**The general entry, printed at I6 p.32.** **FREQUENCY very rare; NUMBER APPEARING 1-10; ARMOR CLASS 8; MOVE 9"; HIT DICE 4; % IN LAIR 80%; TREASURE nil; NUMBER OF ATTACKS see below; DAMAGE/ATTACK 1-8; SPECIAL ATTACKS severed members also attack; SPECIAL DEFENSES turn as mummies; MAGIC RESISTANCE normal; INTELLIGENCE non-; ALIGNMENT N; SIZE M.**

**What they are, printed in full (I6 p.32).** **"Strahd zombies are a form of undead that serve the vampire Strahd von Zarovich. They were called into being through a dark magic, now forgotten even by Strahd himself. Strahd zombies were created from the long-dead guards of Castle Ravenloft."**

**"Strahd zombies appear fragile. Their gray green flesh looks soft and their bones seem brittle. Any single hit of 5 points or more severs parts of their bodies."**

**"Despite their appearance, however, the life forces of Strahd zombies are very great. Severed limbs continue to attack."**

**"Each Strahd zombie's life force lies in its total being. So, damage to any part damages the whole creature. Damage to any one part in excess of a Strahd zombie's total hit points destroys all of that zombie's parts."**

**"Strahd zombies are turned as mummies."**

**How that runs at the table.** **One hit point pool per zombie, however many parts it is in.** **A severed arm is not a new creature with its own hit points; it is another attack by the same creature.** **Damage dealt to any part comes off the one pool, so a party that keeps hitting the body is not wasting effort while limbs crawl.** **When damage to any single part exceeds the zombie's total hit points, every part of that zombie is destroyed at once.** **Number of attacks is therefore counted by the DM from the parts in play, which is what the printed "see below" means.**

**What the entry establishes about who they were.** **They are the former guards of Castle Ravenloft** (printed). **That is an identity supplied by I6, not by this guide**, and it applies to every Strahd zombie in the adventure.

**What the entry does not establish.** **It describes their creation and says nothing about preservation.** **The dark magic is "now forgotten even by Strahd himself", which is a fact about the method, not about the bodies.** **I6 prints that they were created from the long-dead and that they now have soft grey green flesh, and offers no mechanism reconciling the two.** **This guide supplies none**, and the DM who wants an explanation has a printed mystery rather than a printed answer.

**Keyed appearances vary from this entry and are carried as printed where they occur.** **Table 6 and K76 match it at MV 9", Dmg 1-8, AL N.** **K28 prints MV 6", Dmg 1-6, AL NE.** **The guides carry the figures printed at each location rather than this general line.**

**Where they appear in this guide.** **K76, the six in the torture apparatus**, at the figures printed there. **I6 names them as a class: they are "the skeletons of their last victims"** (I6 p.24), **which sits beside the general entry's statement that Strahd zombies were made from the castle's long-dead guards.** **Both are printed and the guide does not reconcile them.**

### 9h. The forced gaseous form

**Reaching 0 hit points.** **Any combat damage, a blow or a spell, that takes a vampire in its body to 0 hit points or below brings it to 0.** **It "is not killed, but it is forced into gaseous form"** (printed, MM p.99). **It must reach its coffin within 12 turns, rest 8 hours and reform.** **It keeps regenerating 3 hit points a round, but regaining hit points does not release it, and it takes no other form before reaching the coffin.**

**What harms the gas (ruling).**

- ***Fireball* or other magical fire: normal damage, with its normal saving throw.**
- **A lightning spell: half damage.** **Other magical lightning the same**, after the attack's normal saving throw.
- **Halved damage rounds down, with a minimum of 1 whenever an attack inflicts damage** (ruling).
- **A whirlwind: its source's damage, doubled.**
- **Nothing else harms the gas.** **A weapon, magical or not, does not. Holy water does not.**

**When damage destroys it (ruling).** **Damage to the gas within the hit points it has regenerated since reaching 0 only reduces them**, and its retreat and its 12-turn deadline continue. **Damage beyond them takes it below 0, and it is destroyed.**

> **A gas at 0 that has regenerated 6 hit points and takes 36 from a *fireball* stands at -30 and is destroyed.**

**Whatever brought it to 0, a blow or a spell, magical fire, lightning or a whirlwind that then reaches the gas, in the same round or a later one, can destroy it.** **The order within a round follows ordinary combat sequencing.**

**The other ways the retreat fails (ruling).** **Missing the 12-turn deadline destroys it.** **So does a coffin destroyed before it arrives.** **Passage is not invitation: gaseous form gives no way into a building the vampire has not been invited into** (printed, I6 p.6). **An ordinary closed door, shutter, window or coffin lid is not an airtight seal: the gas passes through its gaps. It does not pass through solid stone or intact glass without an opening.**

**Why it matters here.** **His coffin is at K86 and the retreat ends here.** **The 8-hour rule at I6 p.30 and the five outcomes at section 6f both turn on what happens to the gas.**

### 9h-bis. Strahd's conduct when encountered

**When Strahd is actually encountered he is the full version** (ruling). **He uses everything and anything at his disposal.** **There are exactly two restraints and no others:**

1. **Anything that would directly interfere with the goal the Fortunes reading generated for him.**
2. **Anything that would harm Ireena.**

**He husbands nothing.** **There is no reserved spell, no held-back power and no resource he is saving for later.** **He casts as a 10th-level magic-user, he drains two levels a blow, he charms by gaze at will, he summons, he changes form, and in a real confrontation he will use whichever of these serves him.**

> **Why the reservation argument does not apply to him.** **He relearns everything with a day's rest in his coffin, so a spell spent is not a spell lost.** **Treating his list as a dwindling stock imports an adventuring party's logic into a creature who has none of their constraints.** **The only costs of spending are the two restraints above.**

**The printed harassment is a different thing and is not an encounter.** **I6 p.6: each night the party stays anywhere but the castle he attacks with wolves and bats, "His attacks are intended more to frighten the PCs than to damage them. Strahd just toys with them", and after 5 melee rounds he and his creatures withdraw.** **That restraint is printed, it is specific to the nightly harassment, and it does not describe his conduct when the party actually meets him.**

> **The distinction the DM needs.** **Harassment is theatre with a printed five-round limit. An encounter has no such limit and no such restraint.** **A DM who plays every meeting as toying has made Strahd harmless; a DM who plays the harassment as a real attempt has killed the party in week one.**

**The Ireena restraint, printed and already absolute** (I6 p.6). **He never attacks her and neither do his wolves.** **He does not turn his gaze or his spells on her to win an invitation, because that is an attack** (ruling). ***Fireball* is the clearest case: one that would catch her is one he does not cast.** **Nothing else in the valley is protected by this.**

**The goal restraint, which varies by reading.** **The Fortunes reading sets what he wants.** **Where his goal needs a character alive, charmed, isolated or unharmed, he will not do the thing that destroys it**, and that is a restraint on method rather than on effort. **A Strahd pursuing a new identity wants a charmed character intact; a Strahd who simply wants the party dead has no such problem.**

**Not reserving is not casting indiscriminately** (ruling). **He is intelligent and uses a spell when it serves his purpose.** **Deception, charm, a withdrawal or simply watching are all available to him and none of them is conservation.** **A Strahd who opens every meeting with *fireball* has been played as badly as one who never casts at all.** **The question at each moment is what serves the goal, not what is left in the list.**

### 9i. Strahd's spells, worked at 10th level

***Comprehend languages*** (1st, Alteration, reversible; PHB p.65). **V, S, M** (a pinch of soot and a few grains of salt). **Range touch. Casting time 1 round. Duration 50 rounds. No saving throw.** One written object or speaking creature. He can read an otherwise incomprehensible written message, or understand a speaking creature; **he must touch the object or creature**, and the spell does not let him write or speak the language. It does not read magical writing beyond knowing that it is magic. **Reverse, *confuse languages*, prevents comprehension or cancels the spell.** *Encounter use:* reading what the party carries, or following a tongue he does not share.

***Hold portal*** (1st, Alteration; PHB p.66). **V only**, so **it needs no hands and no components**. **Range 20". Casting time 1 segment. Duration 10 rounds. No saving throw. Area 800 square feet.** Bars a door, gate or valve of wood, metal or stone as if securely stopped and locked. **A *knock* or *dispel magic* negates it; a magic-user of 14th level or higher opens it at will; any extra-dimensional creature shatters it; and it can be broken or battered down.** *Encounter use:* separating the party, or sealing his own line of retreat. **Ten rounds is ten minutes, which is one turn: it buys time, not safety.**

***Protection from good*** (1st, Abjuration, the reverse of *protection from evil*; PHB p.67). **V, S, M** (powdered iron and silver, to trace the circle). **Range touch. Casting time 1 segment. Duration 20 rounds. No saving throw.** Creature touched. **The magic-user version runs 2 rounds per level where the clerical version runs 3**, so his is 20 rounds and Donavich's is 6. Its effects are the clerical spell's, reversed, as carried in the series' *protection from evil* material (PHB p.44). *Encounter use:* against a lawful good party, and in particular against a paladin.

***Sleep*** (1st, Enchantment/Charm; PHB p.68). **V, S, M** (a pinch of fine sand, rose petals, or a live cricket). **Range 13". Casting time 1 segment. Duration 50 rounds. No saving throw at all.** All targets must lie within a **3" diameter circle**. **It does not affect undead.** Numbers affected, by the targets' Hit Dice: **up to 1, 4 to 16 (4d4); 1+1 to 2, 2 to 8 (2d4); 2+1 to 3, 1 to 4 (1d4); 3+1 to 4, 1 to 2 (½d4, round off); 4+1 to 4+4, 0 to 1 (d4, 3 or 4).** **Roll during play:** the number affected. **Slapping or wounding wakes a sleeper and noise does not; waking takes one full melee round; and a sleeping creature can be slain automatically at one per slayer per melee round.** *Encounter use:* **it cannot touch the module's own party.** **The printed table stops at 4+4 Hit Dice and has no row above it**, so characters of 5th to 7th level are outside the spell entirely. **The absence of a saving throw does not change that**, because the Hit Dice limit applies before any save question arises. **What it reaches is hirelings, henchmen, villagers, Ireena's household and any creature of 4+4 Hit Dice or fewer**, and against those it is lethal: no save, and a sleeper can be slain at one per slayer per round. **I6's own witches at K56 open with *sleep* and take “a member of the party, an NPC if possible”**, which is exactly what the Hit Dice limit predicts.

***Invisibility*** (2nd, Illusion/Phantasm; PHB p.70). **V, S, M** (an eyelash encased in gum arabic). **Range touch. Casting time 2 segments. Duration special. No saving throw.** The recipient cannot be seen by normal vision or infravision. **It lasts until magically broken or dispelled, until he or the recipient cancels it, or until the recipient attacks any creature**, at which point they become visible, **though the attack itself still gains the benefit**. **The guide's surprise ruling covers a vampire who cancels the spell and steps into view, and not this attack**: the benefit is gained while he is still unseen and the spell ends as the blow lands, so there is no coming into view to roll for. **Ordinary action does not end it**: the Handbook names opening doors, talking, eating and climbing stairs. **Highly intelligent creatures of 10 or more Hit Dice or levels have a chance to detect invisible objects automatically**, the provision being confined to the highly intelligent. **His own allies cannot see him either**, nor his gear, unless they can normally see invisible things or use magic to do so. **That restriction is visual only**: they still hear him and take his instructions, so it bears on where they believe he is, not on whether he can command them. **While the spell holds he offers no sight of his eyes, so the gaze does not operate (ruling), though his voice, and *suggestion*, still carry.** *Encounter use:* arriving, leaving, or watching.

***Locate object*** (2nd, Divination, reversible; PHB p.71 for the magic-user header, PHB p.47 for the effect). **V, S, M** (a piece of lodestone). **Range 20". Casting time 2 segments. Duration 10 rounds. No saving throw.** **The magic-user entry is the third level clerical spell with a different range**, so the effect is the clerical text: he casts, **turns slowly, and knows when he is facing in the direction of the object**, provided it is within range. **It locates a known or familiar object** such as apparel, jewellery, furniture, tools, weapons, or even a ladder or stairway. **Reversed (*obscure object*) it hides an object from location by spell, crystal ball or similar means.** **Neither application affects a living creature.** **I6 prints the spell as "locate objects"**; the spell is *locate object*. *Encounter use:* finding a treasure the Fortunes reading placed, and **the reverse is how he hides one from a party that can scry.**

***Mirror image*** (2nd, Illusion/Phantasm; PHB p.71). **V, S. Range 0. Casting time 2 segments. Duration 20 rounds. No saving throw.** Area a 6 foot radius about him. **Roll during play:** percentile dice **plus 10 for his level**. **25 or less, 1 image; 26 to 50, 2; 51 to 75, 3; 76 or more, 4.** **The printed table reads "51-75 = 3, 75 or more = 4" and so places 75 in two bands; this guide reads the fourth band as 76 or more, that being the only value which makes the four bands a partition** (ruling). **With his bonus he cannot roll below 11, so one image occurs only on a d100 of 15 or less.** The images do exactly as he does and **it is impossible to tell which is which**; a struck image vanishes, the rest remain, and **they shift from round to round, so a hit on the real Strahd in one round does not identify him in the next.** *Encounter use:* the standard answer to massed missile fire, and a drain on the party's attacks.

***ESP*** (2nd, Divination; PHB p.70). **V, S, M** (a copper piece). **Range 5", being half an inch per level to a 9" maximum. Casting time 2 segments. Duration 10 rounds. No saving throw.** One creature per probe. He reads surface thoughts, **but not those of creatures with no mind as we know it, all the undead among them**. **Blocked by two or more feet of rock, two or more inches of any metal but lead, or a thin sheet of lead foil.** He probes one creature per turn, and **a probe may continue on the same creature from round to round**. *Encounter use:* learning a plan through a door, which also gives the party a counter: **lead, stone or depth.** **The Handbook warns that *ESP* will not always reveal what sort of creature is there**, so it yields the thought and not reliably the thinker.

***Fireball*** (3rd, Evocation; PHB p.73). **V, S, M** (a tiny ball of bat guano and sulphur). **Range 10" + 1" per level, so 20" for him. Casting time 3 segments. Duration instantaneous. Saving throw for half damage. Area a 2" radius sphere.** **Damage 10d6 at his level. Roll during play:** the damage, and each target's save. **Those who fail take full damage; those who save take half.** The burst conforms to the space it occupies, filling roughly 33,000 cubic feet, **so in a corridor it travels rather than stopping**; it ignites combustibles and melts soft metals, and exposed items must be rolled for. *Encounter use:* his only area attack, and **the one spell he will not use where Ireena stands**, under the Jack or Queen goal.

***Suggestion*** (3rd, Enchantment/Charm; PHB p.75). **V, M** (a snake's tongue and either a bit of honeycomb or a drop of sweet oil). **Range 3". Casting time 3 segments. Duration 6 turns + 6 turns per level, so 66 turns at his level, which is eleven hours. Saving throw negates. One creature.** He influences the target's actions **by uttering a few words, a phrase, or a sentence or two, suggesting a course of action**. **The target must understand the language.** **The suggestion must be worded so the action sounds reasonable**: a request to stab itself, throw itself on a spear or do some other obviously harmful act **automatically negates the spell**. **A very reasonable suggestion causes the saving throw to be made at a penalty, such as -1 or -2, at the DM's discretion.** **The course of action can continue for a considerable duration.** **If the save succeeds the spell has no effect.** **Undead are not subject to *suggestion*.** *Encounter use:* **the quiet alternative to his gaze.** **Refusing eye contact is not a complete defence against the gaze and never was**: a character deliberately avoiding it still meets it **2 in 10**, and attacks at **-4** in melee while doing so (ruling). ***Suggestion*'s advantage is simpler and stronger: he can attempt to influence a target without securing eye contact at all.*** **He must still speak intelligibly to someone who can hear him, and the saving throw and the spell's restrictions remain operative.** **Against his own undead it does nothing.**

> **Two applications, which are not the same thing** (ruling).
>
> 1. **Getting the character to look at him.** **A suggestion that they meet his face while they speak**, if it sounds reasonable in context and the save fails. **That creates the opportunity for the gaze. It does not establish a charm**, and the gaze keeps its own saving throw against spells at -2.
> 2. **Obtaining an invitation.** **A suggestion framed as coming to negotiate, to offer information, or to help against a common danger.** **If the character complies, the invitation may satisfy I6's restriction on entering buildings**, and that building then stays open to him.

> **It is not an unconditional command, and the spell's own restrictions govern.** **A character who knows what he is may regard either request as obviously harmful, which automatically negates the spell.** **The wording and the circumstances decide it**, and a DM who treats a suggestion as a command has given him a power the Players Handbook does not.

> **He need not use it to set up the gaze at all.** **Sometimes the suggestion is the whole of what he wants**: an invitation, a character separated from the others, or something valuable handed over. **A charm would be a worse outcome than any of those if the charm is what gets noticed.**

> **The mechanical point a DM must keep.** ***Suggestion* requires no eye contact, but the spell's normal requirements for communication and comprehension still apply**: he must speak, the target must hear him and must understand the language. **It is not silent, it is not irresistible, and it is not a substitute for his gaze.**

***Gust of wind*** (3rd, Alteration; PHB p.73 for the header, p.74 for the effect). **V, S, M** (a legume seed). **Range 0, so the wind begins at him. Casting time 3 segments. Duration 1 segment. Saving throw none.** **A strong puff of air from him, moving in the direction he faces.** **It extinguishes candles, torches and similar unprotected flames.** **Protected flames such as lanterns dance wildly, with a 5% chance per level, so 50% at his level, of going out as well.** **It fans large fires outwards 1 to 6 feet in the direction of the wind.** **Small flying creatures are forced back 1" to 6"; man-sized ones are held motionless if they try to move into it; large flying creatures are slowed by 50% for 1 round.** **It blows over light objects.** **Its path is 1" wide by 1" of length per level, so 10" long at his level.** *Encounter use:* **the castle is lit almost entirely by the party's own torches.** One casting puts out every unprotected flame in a 10" path and gives lanterns an even chance. **Against a party in the dark with a vampire, that is worth more than damage.**

***Polymorph other*** (4th, Alteration; PHB p.78). **V, S, M** (a caterpillar cocoon). **Range half an inch per level, so 5" at his level. Casting time 4 segments. Duration permanent. Saving throw negates. One creature.** **It completely alters the form and ability, and possibly the personality and mentality, of the recipient.** **A creature of lower intelligence cannot be polymorphed into something with higher intelligence; the reverse is possible.** **The creature must make a system shock roll to survive the change**, and if it succeeds it **acquires all the form and abilities of the creature it has become**. **There is a base 100% chance the change also replaces personality and mentality**, reduced by **5% per point of the subject's intelligence** and adjusted by **5% per Hit Die of difference between the old form and the new**, above or below. **The chance is checked daily until the change takes place.** **The new form may be stronger than it looks.** **Only *dispel magic* restores the original form, and that requires another system shock roll.**

***Polymorph self*** (4th, Alteration; PHB p.78). **V. Range 0. Casting time 3 segments. Duration 2 turns per level, so 20 turns at his level. Saving throw none. Himself.** **He assumes the form of any creature from as small as a wren to as large as a hippopotamus, and its form of locomotion.** **The spell does not give the other abilities, attack, magic or otherwise, and does not risk changing personality and mentality.** **No system shock check is required.** **A magic-user changed to an owl could fly, but his vision would remain human.** **The strength of the new form must suffice for normal movement.** **He can change form as often as he likes within the duration, each change taking 5 segments.** **Damage to the polymorphed form is computed as if inflicted on him**, and **when he returns to his own form, 1 to 12 points of damage are restored**.

> **What the printed text does and does not give a disguise** (reading the spell, not a ruling). **Form and locomotion only.** **No abilities of the assumed shape, so he cannot borrow a person's skills.** **His own mentality is untouched, so he remains Strahd behind the face.** **The text addresses appearance and movement and says nothing about voice**, which is left to the DM. **It confers nothing against detection**: he is still a vampire and still evil to any spell that looks.

> **The duration is the hard limit on any infiltration.** **Twenty turns at his level, and I6 gives him one *polymorph self* among his fifteen.** **That is a visit, not a journey.** **A DM planning to have him travel with the party for days is not running this spell.**

***Animate dead*** (5th, Necromantic; PHB p.79, which prints the magic-user entry as the third level clerical spell with the header below). **V, S, M. Range 1". Casting time 5 rounds. Duration permanent. Saving throw none.** **The effect is the clerical *animate dead*.** *Encounter use:* **the printed route by which the castle's dead keep being replaced**, and the reason a party that leaves bodies behind may meet them again.

***Distance distortion*** (5th, Alteration; PHB p.80). **V, S, M** (a small lump of soft clay). **Range 1" per level. Casting time 6 segments. Duration 1 turn per level. Saving throw none. Area 100 square inches per level.** **The area's dimensions are distorted either to half or to double the distance for those travelling over it**, so a 10 by 100 foot corridor can seem 5 by 50 or 20 by 200. **The true nature of the area is absolutely undetectable to creatures travelling along it**, though **the area radiates a dim dweomer and *true seeing* reveals the earth elemental spread within it**.

> **A dependency that makes this spell uncastable by Strahd as printed.** **PHB p.80: "This spell can only be cast when the magic-user has an earth elemental conjured up."** **I6 gives Strahd fifteen spells and *conjure elemental* is not among them**, nor is any other means of producing an earth elemental. **On the printed list he cannot meet the spell's precondition.**

> **Nothing in the adventure supplies one.** **I6 contains no earth elemental, no means of conjuring one and none under his control, so he cannot cast *distance distortion* as printed.** **It stays in his spellbook and on his printed list: an unmet casting condition takes away the use of a spell, not the knowledge of it.** **Do not invent an elemental to rescue it.** **The guide's default fifth level memorisation puts *cloudkill* in its slot** (see "The fifth level pair, settled", below).

> **The witches' spellbook at K56 is the castle's one other source of magic.** **I6: it holds "all of the spells the witches know plus fear, curse and cloudkill".** **The witches' fourteen printed spells are all first level: *burning hands*, *charm person*, *dancing lights*, *detect magic*, *feather fall*, *friends*, *hold portal*, *dark*, *magic missile*, *protection from good*, *push*, *read magic*, *shield*, *shocking grasp*.** **The book also holds *sleep* and *invisibility*, which Strahd taught the witches** (guide interpretation); **he already knows both.** **Its spells above first level are therefore *invisibility* at second, *fear* and *curse* at fourth, and *cloudkill* at fifth.** **What he learned from it is in the spellbook table below.**

**Learning from books and scrolls (PHB p.10).** **"New spells can be gained from captured or otherwise acquired spell books or from scrolls of magic spells. In the latter event the scroll is destroyed in learning and knowing the new spell or spells."** **A new spell may not take him past his maximum for its level.**

**His Intelligence is 18 (guide ruling).** **I6 p.3 rates him "Genius" without a number and instructs: "Strahd is supposed to be a genius, play him as one."** **The score does not change during the adventure.**

**What Intelligence 18 means for his spells, from the table above.**

- **Chance to know, 85%.** **Roll d100 once for each new spell: 85 or less and he learns it.** **"Each spell may be checked only once."** **A spell he fails is closed to him for good** (PHB p.10).
- **Maximum, 18 per level.** **His largest level is first, at fifteen spells, so the ceiling never stops him.**
- **Minimum, 9 per level.** **It does not apply here.** **It is measured against the whole Players Handbook list for a level, not against the spells this adventure supplies.**

**Strahd's spellbook, as established.** **The witches and their book have been in his castle for as long as they have served him, so he made his rolls on it long before the party arrived.** **The results are fixed and are not rolled at the table; a DM who wants a different spellbook may reroll them before play.**

- ***Dancing lights* is in the book and beyond him.** **He failed it, and it can never be learned.**
- ***Read magic* needed no roll**: every magic-user knows it automatically (DMG p.39).
- ***Cloudkill* needed no roll either**: see the ruling below.
- **Only the printed column is I6's.** **The reconstructed column is this guide's, and should not be quoted as printed.** **It does not contradict the module, which says nothing about the size of his spellbook.**

**Spells he may still gain in play.** **From a party magic-user's spellbook or from a magic-user scroll, by the same roll, once per spell.** **A scroll is destroyed as he learns from it.**

#### *Cloudkill* is an automatic acquisition

**Ruled. It is not subject to the chance-to-know check, and the reason is the absence of any alternative.**

| Fifth level, everything the adventure supplies | Status |
|---|---|
| ***Animate dead*** | **Printed. His already.** |
| ***Distance distortion*** | **Printed. His already, and uncastable for want of a conjured earth elemental.** |
| ***Cloudkill*** | **The only other fifth level spell in I6, anywhere.** |

**A failed check at fifth level does not cost him one option among several. It leaves him with two slots and one usable spell, forced to memorise *animate dead* twice because the adventure offers nothing else.** **That is not a meaningful outcome; it is the adventure's spell inventory running out.** **The check is therefore not applied here.**

#### Having a spell and having it ready are different things

**This governs every encounter in all three guides.**

**Strahd's starting memorisation is the printed fifteen, unchanged.** **He begins the adventure with *distance distortion* prepared and *cloudkill* not prepared**, even though *cloudkill* is in his book. **A larger spellbook does not alter what he walks into the first encounter carrying.**

> **What this forbids.** **No encounter procedure may have Strahd cast a reconstructed spell as though it were already prepared.** **The twenty-nine spell repertoire is what he can draw on when he next memorises, not what he has ready now.** **A passage that has him answer with *cloudkill* on the first meeting is using availability as though it were preparation, which is the whole error the printed and reconstructed columns exist to prevent.**

> **What this permits, and the loosest form of it is still wrong.** **He memorises 4 / 4 / 3 / 2 / 2 from the full book each day like any magic-user.** **An elapsed day makes a reconstructed spell ELIGIBLE FOR SELECTION. It does not make it available to cast.** **Three further things must each have happened, and the passing of the day establishes none of them.**

| | What must be true | Status for the reconstructed fourteen |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **He has learned it** | **Settled above. All fourteen are in his book.** |
| **2** | **He has had the rest and memorisation time his level requires** | **Not implied by elapsed time. He may have been hunting, or driven off, or destroyed and re-forming. See the costs below.** |
| **3** | **He has allocated one of his 4 / 4 / 3 / 2 / 2 slots to it** | **A slot spent here is a slot not spent on something printed.** |

> **Changing a memorised spell is not free (ruling).** **He cannot change his memorised selection without the rest and study for the new spell's level.** **So a swap is not free even when he has the time to rest: the level of the spell coming IN sets the rest band, and the study is owed for it.** **Dropping a fifth level spell for another fifth level spell still costs the 8 hour band and 75 minutes.**

> **So the correct form of a guide passage is conditional, naming the slot.** **"If the DM has had him prepare *cloudkill* in place of *distance distortion*, he may open with it" is correct.** **"A day has passed, so he has *cloudkill*" is not, because it treats the repertoire as a reserve he draws from freely rather than a book he memorises from.**

#### The fifth level pair, settled

**The guide's default fifth level memorisation is *animate dead* and *cloudkill*.**

| Slot | Spell | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | ***animate dead*** | **Printed in I6** |
| **2** | ***cloudkill*** | **Guide adaptation, replacing *distance distortion*** |

**Why.** ***Distance distortion* is printed, and PHB p.80 requires an already conjured earth elemental for it to function.** **I6 supplies Strahd with no elemental and no means of conjuring one, so the printed spell has no established way of working.** ***Cloudkill* is in the reconstructed book and fills the slot without inventing an elemental.**

***Distance distortion* is not erased.** **It remains in the spellbook table above and in the source comparison, because I6 prints it and the guide does not conceal what it departs from.** **The substitution is an adaptation, and it is adopted here as the default rather than handed to the DM as a standing question.**

#### *Cloudkill*, from PHB p.79

**Printed statistics.** **Level 5, Evocation. Components V, S. Range 1". Casting time 5 segments. Duration 1 round per level, so ten rounds for Strahd. Saving throw none. Area of effect a cloud 4" wide, 2" high, 2" deep.**

**What it does to a party, which is the first thing a DM needs.**

| Target | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Fewer than 4+1 hit dice, which is 4 HD or fewer** | **Slain. No save.** |
| **4+1 to 5+1 hit dice** | **Save versus poison at -4, or slain** |
| **Up to 6 hit dice inclusive** | **Save versus poison normally, or slain** |
| **Above 6 hit dice** | **Nothing printed here. The DMG p.45 note adds 1 to 10 on the second and each succeeding round to anything staying in it.** |

> **Read that table against an I6 party.** **A fourth level character has four Hit Dice and dies with no roll, and so does every henchman and torchbearer below him.** **A fifth level character saves at -4 or dies. A sixth level character saves or dies.** **This is not a damage spell, it is a removal spell, and Strahd carrying it changes what a losing fight costs.** **A DM should decide before play whether they are willing to run that.**

> **The thresholds are Hit Dice, and for an ordinary single-class character Hit Dice and level correspond directly within the normal progression.** **A fifth level fighter has five Hit Dice, each a d10.** **The correspondence is not guaranteed for every creature or for an exceptional character, so read the printed bands rather than the level translation when the two could differ.**

#### What the cloud does: sight, movement, and his own undead

**1. What can see into or through it?**

**Nothing is printed.** **The entry describes "a billowing cloud of ghastly yellowish green vapors" and gives no rule for sight, concealment or cover.** **So the spell has no mechanical effect on vision, and the guide states neither that it blinds nor that it conceals.** **It does not blind him inside his own cloud and it does not hide him.**

**2. How does it move? This is the answer that matters.**

**It does not stay where it is put.** ***"The cloudkill moves away from the spell caster at 1" per round, rolling along the surface of the ground. A wind will cause it to alter course, but it will not move back towards its caster."*** **And: *"as the vapors are heavier than air, they will sink to the lowest level of the land, even pour down den or sinkhole openings."***

| | |
|---|---|
| **Movement** | **10 feet per round, away from Strahd, every round** |
| **Over its full duration** | **100 feet of travel at 10th level** |
| **Direction** | **Downhill and downward. It seeks the lowest level and pours down openings.** |
| **Guaranteed** | **It never moves back towards him** |

**3. Does the 1 to 10 reach undead? Yes for his zombies, no for Strahd, and it governs two things.**

**Neither passage states it.** **Every lethal effect in the PHB entry is a save versus poison, and the DMG's 1 to 10 names neither a save nor poison.** **The guide adopts poison immunity, ruled in full in the next section: it protects Strahd and does not protect his dead.**

| | What the ruling decides |
|---|---|
| **His own exposure** | **The cloud does not return to him, but that does not remove the round or rounds in which he is inside it, whether he centred it on himself or stepped into it.** **Under the adopted ruling that exposure is harmless, and the self-centring option costs him nothing.** |
| **His servants** | **A cloud rolling away from him rolls towards whatever is in front of him, and in this castle that is often his own undead.** **It destroys every Strahd zombie it reaches.** |

#### The ruling on undead and the 1 to 10

**Adopted as a labelled guide interpretation: poison immunity protects undead from the continuing damage.** **It is an interpretation and not an exemption printed in either passage.** **The PHB defines the cloud as poisonous vapour, and the DMG expands what prolonged exposure does to creatures that would otherwise survive it rather than converting the spell from poison into an independently damaging substance.**

**Immunity is not uniform, and the difference decides the tactic.**

| Entry | What it prints | Poison immunity? |
|---|---|---|
| **Vampire, MM p.99** | **"*Sleep*, *charm*, and *hold* spells do not affect vampires. Neither do poison or paralysis."** | **YES, in terms** |
| **Zombie, MM p.103** | **"*Sleep*, *charm*, *hold* and *cold*-based spells do not affect zombies."** | **NO. Poison is not in the list.** |

**So the ruling protects Strahd and does not protect his zombies, and the zombies are in the worst band.** **Strahd zombies are 4 Hit Dice.** ***Cloudkill* slays any creature with fewer than 4+1 hit dice with no saving throw, and four is fewer than four plus one.**

| Creature | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Strahd** | **Unaffected. Poison immunity printed at MM p.99.** |
| **Strahd zombies, 4 HD** | **SLAIN. No save. No printed poison immunity, and below the threshold.** |
| **Other undead** | **Decided by their own printed immunities, which must be read rather than assumed from the vampire entry** |
| **Living creatures above 6 hit dice** | **1 to 10 per round from the second round onward** |

> **What this means for how he uses it.** ***Cloudkill* and his own dead are mutually exclusive in the same space.** **A cloud rolling down a corridor destroys every Strahd zombie it reaches, with no roll, while leaving him untouched.** **He cannot advance behind the cloud with zombies ahead of it, or cast it into a room his dead are holding, without destroying them.** **A DM should treat the spell as something he uses INSTEAD of his servants, not alongside them.**

#### Contamination: there is none

**Ruled: once Strahd leaves the cloud he is not a mobile source of *cloudkill* damage.** **Nothing printed establishes that any of the substance remains on him, or that it could harm anyone he touches.** Either would need a duration, a range, a saving throw and a means of removal, and none exists.

> **What is permitted is atmosphere, not damage.** **He may walk out of the sickly yellow-green vapour with its smell clinging to him, untouched**, which is unsettling to witnesses precisely because it does nothing to him. **That is description. It carries no mechanical effect and no saving throw.**

#### What this does to the tactical claims

> **It cannot deny a corridor or seal a doorway.** **A cloud that advances 10 feet every round, rolls along the ground, seeks the lowest level and pours down openings cannot hold a position.** **It is a moving front, not a barrier.**

> **The route must be traced for the actual casting.** **The spell sinks into openings it encounters along its path, and this castle is full of them: I6 pp.10 to 33 carry 96 mentions of stairs, 45 of staircases, 34 shafts, 11 trap doors and a marble chute.** **Whether a given cloud meets one depends on the room, the direction and the route, so the DM traces the path for the casting in front of him.** **The guide supplies the rule and cannot supply the route.**

> **The use the printed text does support.** **Cast it at something, not across something.** **It is an advancing killing front he can send down a corridor at a party.**

> **How much of "walking behind it" is established.** **The direction is: it moves away from him and will not move back towards him, and that much is printed.** **Holding a chosen distance behind it through turns, junctions and changes of elevation is not printed and is a DM's adjudication.** **A cloud rolling along the ground and seeking the lowest level will not necessarily follow the corridor he is following.** **The direction is guaranteed and the pursuit is not.**

> **The self-centring option costs him nothing.** **He may form it around himself. The cloud is 2" deep and moves 1" per round, so he remains inside it into the second round unless he moves, and under the adopted ruling that exposure is harmless.** **Casting ahead of himself at the printed range of 1" avoids the exposure entirely, and with a 12" move he can step clear either way.** **Neither choice is wrong.**

> **One qualification on the reconstruction as a whole.** **I6 does not establish that the book at K56 is Strahd's.** **It is the witches' book, in his castle, and the guide's claim is only that an intelligent 10th level magic-user with uncounted years and unrestricted access to it would have learned what he could.** **This is a plausible, rules-governed repertoire assembled from material the module supplies. It is not a discovery of an unprinted canonical spellbook, and should never be described as one.**

#### What a change of spells actually costs him, from DMG p.40

**Rest first.** **The minimum rest is set by the highest spell level being recovered**, and it covers the whole batch rather than each spell: **4 hours for 1st to 2nd, 6 hours for 3rd to 4th, 8 hours for 5th to 6th.**

**Then study.** **A further quarter of an hour per spell level, for each spell.** **A first level spell costs 15 minutes, a fifth level spell 75.**

| What he is re-preparing | Rest | Study | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Everything, the full 4 / 4 / 3 / 2 / 2** | **8 h** | **9 h 45 m** | **17 h 45 m** |
| **Fourth and fifth level only** | **8 h** | **4 h 30 m** | **12 h 30 m** |
| **Both fifth level slots** | **8 h** | **2 h 30 m** | **10 h 30 m** |
| **All three third level** | **6 h** | **2 h 15 m** | **8 h 15 m** |
| **All four first level** | **4 h** | **1 h** | **5 h** |
| **A single first level spell** | **4 h** | **15 m** | **4 h 15 m** |

**That table assumes he was merely driven off.** **If he was reduced to 0 hit points the series' gaseous form ruling applies first, and the two periods are sequential rather than concurrent.**

#### Driven off, against destroyed to gaseous form

| | Driven off, still corporeal | Reduced to 0 hit points |
|---|---|---|
| **Travel to the coffin** | **none** | **actual time taken, 12 turns maximum or he is destroyed** |
| **Vampire recovery in the coffin** | **none** | **8 h** |
| **Then rest for spells** | **as the table above** | **as the table above** |
| **Then study** | **as the table above** | **as the table above** |
| **Full 4 / 4 / 3 / 2 / 2** | **17 h 45 m** | **25 h 45 m** |
| **Both fifth level slots only** | **10 h 30 m** | **18 h 30 m** |
| **All four first level only** | **5 h** | **13 h** |
| **A single first level spell** | **4 h 15 m** | **12 h 15 m** |

> **Why they do not run together.** **The eight hours in the coffin are spent as mist reforming, and a vampire in gaseous form is not a magic-user resting and revitalising his mental faculties.** **The spell rest begins when he is corporeal again.**

> **And there is a leg before the eight hours.** **MM p.99: a vampire brought to 0 hit points "must then return to its coffin within 12 turns, rest 8 hours, and reform a corporeal body."**

> **Read the twelve turns correctly, because it is easy to add it to the total and wrong to do so.** **It is a MAXIMUM ALLOWANCE, not an elapsed time.** **The right-hand column is: actual travel to the coffin, then 8 hours, then spell preparation.** **The travel may take almost no time if he is driven off beside his own tomb, or the better part of two hours from the far end of the castle.** **Nothing is automatically added.**

> **And the limit has teeth.** **Failing to reach the coffin inside twelve turns is destruction, not a longer recovery.** **So the party's opportunity is not only the hours he must spend but the route he must travel and the deadline he must meet to spend them.**

> **How much of an opportunity, which is far less than knowing where the tomb is.** **Knowledge of K86 establishes nothing on its own.** **Gaseous form is harmed by magical fire, by lightning at half damage, and by a whirlwind at double its source's damage, and by nothing else: no weapon, magical or not, and no holy water** (see the gaseous form section). **So interception requires all of the following at once, and a party that lacks any one of them is simply waiting.**

| | What the party needs |
|---|---|
| **1** | **An unexpended source of magical fire, lightning or a whirlwind** |
| **2** | **To be on his actual route, which they do not choose** |
| **3** | **To reach that point inside the twelve turns** |
| **4** | **Enough damage to exceed the hit points he has regenerated at 3 a round since reaching 0** |

> **Position, available routes and time remaining decide it in play.** **A DM should adjudicate the attempt rather than rule it impossible.**

#### Destroying the gas is only one of three tactics, and probably the worst

**The four conditions above govern DESTRUCTION BY DAMAGE while he is gaseous. They do not govern the other two routes, and a guide that offers only them understates what a party can do.**

| Tactic | What it needs | What it does not need |
|---|---|---|
| **Destroy the gas** | **all four conditions above** | |
| **Strike immediately on transformation** | **an area attack of the right kind, aimed at his last position** | **pursuit, or knowledge of his route** |
| **Deny the destination** | **to reach K86 first and render the coffin unusable** | **to touch the gas at all, or to time his arrival** |

> **The second is the strongest and the guide had buried it.** **Gaseous form is not invisibility, and the party knows where he was standing when he collapsed.** **Under the damage rule above, the sooner the blast lands the fewer regenerated hit points it must overcome: a *fireball* in the round after transformation faces almost none.** **Whether he is still in the area depends on initiative, movement and the geometry of the room, which a DM adjudicates. It is not to be waved away on the assumption that the gas has already gone.**

> **The third does not require catching him at all.** **If the coffin cannot receive him, the twelve-turn deadline destroys him whatever he does.**

#### What K86 settles about attacking the coffin, and what it does not

**Printed, I6 p.30.** **"Settled into the dirt on the floor, lies a shining black coffin of finely waxed wood. The coffin's fittings are of brilliant brass."** **And: "The smell of freshly turned earth is here."** **And, decisively for the DM: "If Strahd has been reduced to 0 hit points within the previous 8 game hours, his body is in this coffin."**

| The question | What the source settles |
|---|---|
| **Is the coffin vulnerable to magical fire?** | **It is finely waxed wood with brass fittings, so the item saving throw applies.** **Settled below from DMG pp.80 to 81: a *fireball* is attack form 6, the coffin is treated as thick wood, and it saves on 11 or better.** |
| **Can a blast destroy the grave earth?** | **No, not by blast.** **PHB p.73 prints the burst as flame without appreciable pressure, so no casting strips K86 of its soil.** **Scorched earth still qualifies. Ruled in full below.** |
| **Does destroying the coffin deny him a resting place?** | **NOT ESTABLISHED, and this is the weak link.** **MM p.99 says a vampire rests in "a coffin or similar receptacle", and K86 retains its earth whatever happens to the box.** **Whether a destroyed coffin leaves him a qualifying substitute is a ruling the guide has not made.** |

#### The room arithmetic: a *fireball* fills K86

**This guide establishes that the burst conforms to the space it occupies, filling roughly 33,000 cubic feet, and that exposed items must be rolled for.** **K86 is printed as 50 feet east to west by 30 feet across.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Floor area of K86** | **1,500 square feet** |
| **A 33,000 cubic foot burst over that floor** | **22 feet deep** |

**Unless the tomb's ceiling stands higher than twenty-two feet, a single *fireball* fills K86 entirely and overflows into its exits.** **Every square foot of the dirt floor is inside the blast, not merely the patch the coffin sits on.**

**And the earth is loose.** **I6 prints "the smell of freshly turned earth" and the coffin "settled into the dirt".** **Freshly turned earth is not compacted ground, and loose material is the easier case for fire, not the harder one.**

#### Whose *fireball*, and how big

**The damage above is Strahd's.** **A party magic-user attacking K86 is not casting at his level, and the guide should not quietly borrow the antagonist's dice for the party's spell.**

| Caster level | Damage | Average |
|---|---|---|
| **7th** | **7d6** | **24.5** |
| **8th** | **8d6** | **28** |
| **9th** | **9d6** | **31.5** |
| **10th** | **10d6** | **35** |
| **11th** | **11d6** | **38.5** |

**Any of these is lethal to an unprotected person. That is the right scale to hold in mind, and it is a reason not to wave the blast away as a scorch mark.**

#### What a *fireball* does to the earth, and the ruling

**Soil is not a creature.** **It has no hit points and takes no saving throw versus spells, and the damage dice above do not subtract from anything.** Giving it either would invent a mechanism to answer a question the rules did not ask.

**Three outcomes, which are different claims and should not be collapsed.**

| Outcome | What it would mean | Ruling |
|---|---|---|
| **Scorching** | Organic material destroyed, the surface altered. It remains recognisably earth from his grave. | **It still qualifies.** |
| **Physical disruption** | Loose earth scattered or displaced, enough that the resting place no longer works. | **EXCLUDED by the printed text.** |
| **Fundamental transformation** | Heated enough to fuse or vitrify and cease to be soil. | **Nothing establishes it.** |

> **THE RULING.** **Ordinary scorching leaves the earth qualifying.** **Disruption by blast is excluded by the spell's own text** (PHB p.73): *"The burst of the fireball does not expend a considerable amount of pressure, and the burst will generally conform to the shape of the area in which it occurs, thus covering an area equal to its normal spherical volume."* **It is flame without appreciable force, whatever the word explosive suggests**, and no guide passage may have one casting strip K86 of its soil by blast. **Fundamental transformation would not leave the earth qualifying, but nothing establishes it**: the spell gives no temperature and is instantaneous. **So the earth very probably survives in a usable state, and the coffin very probably does not.** **Damaging soil and destroying its identity as the soil from his grave are two different things, and the second does not follow from the first.**

**The same entry supplies three more things, and two of them cut the other way.**

| Printed | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **"Ignites all combustible materials within its burst radius"** | **The coffin is finely waxed wood. This is as direct a statement as the guide is going to get that it burns.** |
| **"Items exposed to the spell's effects must be rolled for to determine if they are affected. Items with a creature which makes its saving throw are considered as unaffected."** | **The coffin is rolled for. It has no creature to shelter behind, since Strahd has not yet arrived.** |
| **"Range: 10" + 1"/level"** | **Range is the caster's, not a fixed figure. 17" at 7th level, 20" at 10th.** |

> **The attack on K86 bears on the receptacle rather than the soil.** **How well it works is settled below, from the item saving throw table.**

#### Why the earth may matter less than it seems

**If the coffin is destroyed, he has lost the receptacle the retreat rule requires, whatever happens to every grain of soil.** **The earth question decides a second-order case: whether a surviving or replaced receptacle would still work.** **A DM short of time should resolve the coffin first.**

#### The coffin's saving throw, settled from DMG pp.80 to 81

**Table III, Saving Throw Matrix for Magical and Non-Magical Items.** ***Fireball* is attack form 6 and has its own column, separate from magical fire at form 7.**

| Material | vs *fireball* (6) | vs magical fire (7) | vs normal fire (8) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wood or rope, thin** | **15+, saves 30%** | **11+, saves 50%** | **9+, saves 60%** |
| **Wood or rope, thick** | **11+, saves 50%** | **7+, saves 70%** | **5+, saves 80%** |

**The coffin is treated as thick wood, being a substantial receptacle rather than a thin object.** **That classification is the guide's; I6 does not specify.** **So it saves on 11 or better: an even chance.**

**What success and failure mean, which the DMG answers directly.** **"These saving throws are self-explanatory in general. It is a case of either saving or failing."**

> **So no half measures, and no borrowing the creature rule.** **A character who saves against a *fireball* takes half damage. An item does not: it either withstands the attack or it does not.** **The coffin has no hit points, takes no fraction of 7d6, and the party's damage roll is irrelevant to it.**

> **One printed condition worth holding onto.** **The table assumes "the item in question is actually exposed to the form of attack ... the fire actually contacts the item".** **A *fireball* detonated elsewhere does not roll against the coffin merely because a radius overlaps the tomb on a map.**

#### Saving is not surviving: the second fire

**The PHB says the *fireball* "ignites all combustible materials within its burst radius".** **The item saving throw governs the burst. It does not make wood noncombustible, and it does not put out a fire that has started.**

**So a coffin that saves is a coffin that is alight**, and the continuing burn is a different attack form with its own line in the same table.

| | |
|---|---|
| **The burst** | **attack form 6, *fireball*. Thick wood saves on 11+.** |
| **Afterwards** | **attack form 8, normal fire. Thick wood saves on 5+.** |
| **The printed condition on form 8** | **"The item in question would have to be exposed to the fire for an amount of time sufficient to have an effect", with thresholds given by material.** |

> **And the wax is fuel.** **I6 prints the coffin as finely waxed. Wax is combustible and melts as it heats, feeding a fire rather than resisting it.** **That bears on how long the burning lasts, not on whether it starts, which the PHB has already settled.**

> **What a DM should not do.** **Fix a number of rounds, or assume every ignition consumes the whole object.** **Sufficient exposure is the printed test, and whether the fire sustains depends on air and on how much of the coffin is exposed.** **The DM adjudicates duration; the table supplies the thresholds.**

> **Strahd cannot deal with it himself.** **He arrives as gas, under compulsion, inside twelve turns, and this guide's retreat rule gives him no other form before the coffin.** **He cannot beat out a fire he reaches as vapour.**

#### The conclusion, stated at the right strength

> **Not "the attack on K86 works".** **A *fireball* that actually reaches the coffin can render Strahd's resting place unusable, subject to the item saving throw and to how the subsequent burning is adjudicated.** **Roughly: an even chance of immediate failure, and a surviving coffin still alight with its own wax feeding the flame.**

> **A strong tactic and not a guaranteed one, which is the correct description of it.**

#### The procedure, in four steps

| | |
|---|---|
| **1** | **A *fireball* that actually reaches the coffin triggers ONE item saving throw, attack form 6, thick wood, 11+.** |
| **2** | **The DM determines whether the coffin or its waxed finish goes on burning after the burst.** |
| **3** | **If it does, apply the form 8 exposure requirement, then its saving throw at 5+ once sufficient exposure has occurred.** |
| **4** | **Judge whether what remains is still a functional coffin. The grave earth is a separate matter throughout.** |

> **One save for the assembled object.** **A coffin is a lid, sides, a base, joints and fittings, and all of them meet the same burst.** **Rolling for each panel would multiply saves with no printed basis for how finely to divide a construction, and would give the perverse result that the more pieces an object has the more chances it has to fail.** **Components are rolled for separately only when characters deliberately attack one.**

> **The acceptance test is function, not ashes.** **Can the remains still serve as a coffin?** **A destroyed lid probably leaves one, since an open box still receives a body. A broken base or separated sides probably does not.** **So a party that attacks the base achieves more than one that hammers the thickest piece.**

> **Two things this procedure does not pretend.** **A successful save is survival of the attack, not an undamaged coffin.** **And extending the form 8 exposure series to thick wood is a DM ruling: the printed examples stop at bone and ivory and say "etc."** **That is a much narrower ruling than inventing hit points, and it is still a ruling.**

#### If the party smashes it instead of burning it

**This was the case the exposure procedure could not reach, and the same table answers it. Repeated blows are repeated saving throws, one per blow.**

| Attack form | What the DMG says it is | Thick wood | Fails per blow | Expected blows |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **2, crushing blow** | **"struck by a weighty falling object or a blow from an ogre's or giant's weapon"** | **saves 10+** | **45%** | **about 2** |
| **3, normal blow** | **"an attack by a normal-strength opponent or only fairly heavy object"** | **saves 3+** | **10%** | **about 10** |

> **Averages, not timetables.** **Ten and two are expectations, not destruction times.** **A coffin may split on the first swing or shrug off twenty.**

> **And the repetition itself is the guide's application, not a printed mechanic.** **The DMG prints one saving throw per attack. Rolling one per blow is the obvious reading, but the table nowhere says that blows accumulate, and the guide should not imply it does.**

#### GUIDE RULING: progressive damage under repeated blows

**LABELLED AS THE GUIDE'S, NOT THE DMG'S.** **Nothing in the pages checked prescribes a cumulative penalty for successive blows.** **This is adopted because a flat 10% per swing treats the twentieth hammer blow as though it struck an undamaged coffin, which is the one thing everyone at the table knows is false.**

**The rule.** **Each successive damaging blow against the same object imposes a cumulative -1 on its saving throw.**

| Blow | Save needed | Fails | Destroyed by this blow or earlier |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **3+** | **10%** | **10%** |
| **2** | **4+** | **15%** | **24%** |
| **3** | **5+** | **20%** | **39%** |
| **4** | **6+** | **25%** | **54%** |
| **5** | **7+** | **30%** | **68%** |
| **6** | **8+** | **35%** | **79%** |
| **7** | **9+** | **40%** | **88%** |
| **8** | **10+** | **45%** | **93%** |
| **9** | **11+** | **50%** | **97%** |
| **10** | **12+** | **55%** | **98%** |

**Expected blows falls from ten to between four and five, and an even chance is reached on the fourth.** **That is a party demolishing a coffin over most of a minute, not most of a turn.**

> **The qualification on what counts.** **The penalty accrues for every blow that actually strikes and is capable of damaging the object, whether or not the save succeeded.** **A successful save means it remains functional, not that it is undamaged.**

> **Scope, so the coffin is not uniquely fragile.** **This applies to repeated physical blows against any breakable object, a wooden door as much as a coffin.** **It does not modify item saving throws generally, and fire and the other attack forms keep their own procedures: a second *fireball* is a fresh save at 11+, not 10+.**

> **The optional floor from DMG p.81.** **The page offers a rule a DM "may wish to consider", allowing a save on a natural 20 regardless of penalties.** **A DM using it keeps a 5% floor, so no single blow is ever certain however long the demolition runs.**

#### GUIDE RULING: hitting an unattended object

**ALSO THE GUIDE'S.** **The pages checked contain no rule for attacking an unattended object; what they cover is helpless creatures under pummel, grapple and overbear, which is a different thing.**

**The rule.** **A blow against a stationary, accessible, unattended object hits automatically. No attack roll.** **The question worth rolling is whether the object survives, not whether a character standing beside a coffin can hit it.**

> **Why this is not the prone or helpless rule borrowed.** **A fallen creature can still move, flinch and defend itself, and the printed bonuses reflect that.** **An unattended coffin does none of those things, so the right answer is no roll rather than a generous one.**

> **The three steps, kept separate.** **Hitting is automatic. Surviving is the item saving throw on the right attack form. Progressive damage is the cumulative penalty above.**

#### What destroying the coffin does and does not cost him

**Tested against the settled rulings rather than assumed. The tactic is narrower than it looks, and sharper for it.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **It does not stop his daylight rest** | **MM p.99 requires rest in a coffin "unless far beneath the surface of the ground". K86 is far beneath. He can rest in the catacombs with no coffin at all.** |
| **It carries no slow penalty** | **The MM specifies no additional penalty for a vampire prevented from resting. Nothing wastes away.** |
| **It is lethal at exactly one moment** | **The forced retreat. Compulsory recovery requires his own coffin (ruling), so a party that destroys it has armed a trap that springs only when he is next reduced to 0.** |

> **So a party that burns the coffin and then avoids him has gained nothing, and a party that burns it and then fights him has gained everything.** **The order matters and the guide should say so.** **Destroying it after he has already been forced to gas is better still, if they can reach K86 inside his twelve turns.**

#### He can replace it, and not quickly

**The qualification procedure (ruling) is the answer to the Tuesday-to-Friday case.**

**A replacement qualifies for the forced retreat only when both are true:** **it is a genuine resting receptacle holding soil from his grave at K86, AND he has completed one ordinary eight-hour coffin rest in it.** **Before that it is a box with earth in it.**

> **The compulsory eight hours after a defeat do not count towards qualifying it.** **That recovery is not ordinary rest, and a Strahd who reaches an unqualified box in gaseous form has reached nothing.**

| Does it qualify? | |
|---|---|
| **An empty crypt coffin he has never used** | **No** |
| **One holding another person's burial earth** | **No** |
| **One supplied with his grave soil, not yet rested in** | **No** |
| **One with his grave soil, after eight hours' ordinary rest in it** | **Yes** |
| **A previously established spare, intact and reachable** | **Yes** |
| **Anything improvised inside the twelve turns** | **No. Grave soil in another container is not a coffin.** |

**No network of spare coffins is invented.** **I6 and the Monster Manual give him one, at K86, and the catacombs supply crypt coffins he could press into service given time and soil from his own grave.**

#### The state of the coffin, and what a defeat costs him in each

**This is the table a DM needs at the moment the party reduce him to 0, and it is the only question that matters then.**

| State of his resting place | A forced retreat now |
|---|---|
| **Original intact, soil intact** | **Succeeds. Twelve turns to K86, eight hours, he reforms.** |
| **Original intact, soil removed or destroyed** | **Succeeds as a destination: the coffin remains valid (I6 p.32).** **What the loss of grave soil costs his restoration of power is NOT RULED and is open: do not resolve it silently either way.** |
| **Original destroyed, nothing prepared** | **Fails. No destination. He is destroyed at the twelfth turn.** |
| **Original destroyed, a replacement prepared but not yet slept in** | **Fails, exactly as if nothing were prepared. A box with his earth in it is not a coffin.** |
| **Original destroyed, replacement qualified by eight hours' ordinary rest** | **Succeeds, to the replacement.** |
| **Original destroyed, and he is already in gaseous retreat** | **Fails unless he reaches another valid coffin inside the remaining turns.** |

> **The DM's test for the fourth row against the fifth is one question: has he slept a full ordinary rest in it since preparing it?** **Not whether it looks like a coffin, and not whether the earth is in it.**

#### A seventh case: destruction AFTER he arrives

**The table above ends at arrival. What if the party reach K86 while he lies in the coffin recovering?**

**Most of it is already settled (ruling).**

| | |
|---|---|
| **What he is** | **A body, not gas, for the eight hours, counted from arrival** |
| **What he can do** | **Nothing. He cannot leave or fight, and disturbing him does not change that.** |
| **What disturbance costs** | **Nothing. The eight hours neither restart nor cost him hit points.** |
| **Whether he is safe** | **No. Resting is no immunity to attack or destruction (ruling).** |

> **So a party standing over him does not need the coffin at all.** **He is helpless, he cannot fight back, and the ordinary means of destroying a vampire are open to them.** **That last point depends on there being a body to stake, which is a guide interpretation rather than printed text, since MM p.99 places the reformation of a corporeal body after the eight hours rather than during them.** **A DM who declines the interpretation has a party that must destroy the coffin instead, which the ruling below already covers, so the outcome does not change.** **Burning the box under a helpless body is the hard way to do an easy thing.**

**Guide ruling. If the coffin is rendered unusable before the eight hours are complete, his recovery fails and he is destroyed.**

**Arrival ends the deadline. It does not complete the recovery.** **He has reached the place that can restore him and remains dependent on it until the eight hours elapse.**

| Three consequences, stated rather than left to be worked out | |
|---|---|
| **Destroying the coffin is not the same act as destroying Strahd** | **It is the ruling that makes his destruction follow from losing the receptacle during recovery, not an identity between the two.** |
| **The twelve-turn clock does not restart** | **He has arrived, and he can neither leave nor fight (ruling).** |
| **A backup coffin cannot rescue him here** | **Even a qualified spare is no use: he cannot move to it while helpless, and no printed mechanism transfers an interrupted recovery.** |

> **The line that matters at the table is between disturbance and destruction.** **Shaking the coffin, opening it, striking it without rendering it unusable: the recovery continues and the eight hours neither restart nor cost him hit points (ruling).** **Destroying its function as a coffin ends him.** **A coffin is destroyed when it can no longer hold a body enclosed.**

> **The three levels of certainty, kept apart.** **PRINTED, MM p.99: he must "return to its coffin within 12 turns, rest 8 hours, and reform a corporeal body", and the page states no consequence for those conditions going unmet.** **INTERPRETATION: that destroying the receptacle partway prevents the recovery from being completed, since the page supplies no alternative route.** **GUIDE RULING: that the consequence of that incompletion is permanent destruction.** **It is weaker than the missed twelve-turn deadline, where the page sets a compulsory limit and names the one destination, and a DM should not treat the two as equivalent.**

> **Do not read the regeneration rate into the eight hours.** **Strahd regenerates 3 hit points a round, and that rule governs ordinary activity.** **The eight hours govern what follows combat reducing him to 0 and forcing him into gas.** **Nothing on the page says the rate continues through the recovery, or that the body assembles itself a few points at a time.** **Treating the recovery as regeneration in slow motion would produce two things the rules do not supply: a partial hit-point total, and a corporeal target carrying it.**

> **What the DM describes, and what the DM must not build.** **An interrupted reformation can be described: an incomplete body collapsing or dissolving as the coffin is broken up.** **That description carries no mechanics.** **It is not a creature with hit points, an armour class, a to-hit roll or a saving throw, and the DM should not make an encounter out of it.** **The consequence is settled by the ruling above, so a partly formed Strahd has nothing left to resolve.**

> **And the practical note stands: a party who can do this can almost always destroy him directly instead, since resting gives him no immunity (ruling).** **The ruling matters for the case where they attack the box rather than the body, which is likelier than it sounds when the body is a corpse in a closed coffin and the party are carrying torches.**

#### The discovery safeguard on all of this

> **Every row above is DM-facing. None of it is knowledge the characters have.**

**The party can see that a coffin is burnt. They cannot see whether a second one has been slept in.** **Qualification leaves no mark: a qualified coffin and an unqualified one are the same object with the same earth in it, and no printed means of telling them apart exists.**

> **So a party may exploit the window without being told there is one.** **They burn the coffin, they fight him that night, and he is destroyed. What they learn from that is that burning coffins is worth doing, not that an eight hour rule governs it.** **A DM who explains the qualification period has handed them a ruling rather than an experience.**

> **What they CAN establish by playing: that he was destroyed when they expected him to escape, that a second coffin has appeared in the catacombs, that he has not been seen for a day.** **Inference from those is the game. The rule behind them is not theirs.**

> **And the tactical shape, stated for the DM alone.** **He is not destroyed by losing the coffin and he is not safe.** **Until he has slept a full ordinary rest in a replacement he has no retreat destination, so any defeat in that window kills him.** **A party attacking the same night is using the window whether or not they know it exists; one that waits a week has handed it back without ever knowing they held it.**

> **Helga is not in the same position.** **Her coffin at K32 is above ground, so losing it costs her the daylight rest as well: she needs another receptacle or somewhere far underground before dawn.** **Strahd, underground, loses only the destination.**

> **Two things are open and should be decided explicitly rather than left to whichever was assumed.** **Soil destroyed or removed with the coffin intact: the coffin remains a valid destination, and what it costs him that his power is no longer restored by contact with his grave soil has never been ruled.** **And what, if anything, qualifies as a substitute receptacle: the guide makes no ruling.**

> **The number that matters, read correctly.** **Reduce Strahd to 0 hit points at midnight and he owes 8 hours in the coffin, then 8 hours' rest, then 9 h 45 m of study: 25 h 45 m of rest and recovery.** **He is NOT ready 25 h 45 m later.** **A DM who prefers to run the periods concurrently is making a ruling, and should know it shortens his absence by about a third.**

> **The study is confined to K37 by night (ruling), and K37 is above ground.** **The coffin recovery and the rest run through the following day, and the study waits for the next dark.** **So a Strahd destroyed to gas at midnight is out of the fight for that night and the whole of the next day, and is working on his spells through the night after.**

> **Nightfall is not left vague: the guide sets daylight at 06:00 to 18:00, or the campaign's own sunrise to sunset.** **Only the journey up from K86 is the DM's, and it is short beside the wait.**

#### The worked clock, and the thing it exposes

**Driven off at midnight, still corporeal.** **Rest 00:00 to 08:00. Then nothing he can do about it: K37 is above ground, he does not act above ground by day (ruling), and a windowless study is no exemption, exactly as shutters and curtains are none.** **Study runs 18:00 to 03:45.**

**Reduced to 0 at midnight.** **Gaseous recovery 00:00 to 08:00, spell rest 08:00 to 16:00, then the same wait.** **Study runs 18:00 to 03:45.**

**Both columns are Strahd**, in two outcomes of the same fight. **Nothing here describes vampires generally: Helga and the rest have no spells to regain, and none of this applies to them.**

> **Both land at a quarter to four on the second night.** **The nightfall gate collapses the difference between being driven off and being destroyed to gas, because the study waits on the same sunset either way and there is slack in front of it.** **An eight hour penalty that costs nothing is worth knowing and is invisible from the totals.**

> **An illustration, not a rule. The equality is conditional** and holds only because both rest periods finish before the same sunset. **A defeat later in the night, an interrupted rest, or a different spell load breaks it.** **And it assumes study begins at 18:00: the journey up from K86 pushes both completions later by however long it takes, and is not silently zero.**

#### Available to act is not the same as fully rearmed

**Two clocks, and a DM should not run them as one.**

| At 08:00, recovery done | At 03:45, study done |
|---|---|
| **He is corporeal again. Above ground he is still barred until 18:00, but underground he can move, give orders and use everything he did not expend.** | **The spells he chose to regain are back.** |

**He does not wait until a quarter to four to act.** **He waits until then to be fully armed, and those are different things.**

#### The rest overlaps his daylight; the study spends his night

**The rest is NOT free.** **It is a separate compulsory requirement and it does not go away.** **What the worked example shows is that it can be served during hours he must spend inactive anyway, because it needs only his coffin or anywhere far underground (ruling).** **Two requirements overlapping, not one requirement removed.**

**The study is different. It needs K37, which is above ground, so it is night work (ruling).** **That is the part which competes with everything else he might do.**

**Study is charged per spell, a quarter hour per spell level, so he can be finer than a level group.**

| Spell level | Each spell costs | Two | Three | Four |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1st** | **15 m** | **30 m** | **45 m** | **1 h** |
| **2nd** | **30 m** | **1 h** | **1 h 30 m** | **2 h** |
| **3rd** | **45 m** | **1 h 30 m** | **2 h 15 m** | |
| **4th** | **1 h** | **2 h** | | |
| **5th** | **1 h 15 m** | **2 h 30 m** | | |

**A full reload is 4(15) + 4(30) + 3(45) + 2(60) + 2(75) = 585 minutes, or 9 h 45 m.**

> **The rest band is still set by the highest level he is regaining, so one fifth level spell costs the 8 hour band and 1 h 15 m of study.** **The band is the overlap; the study is the spend.**

> **So partial preparation is a real decision, and finer than by level.** **Spells not cast are kept (ruling) and study can be divided between spells (ruling), so he can take back one *fireball* for 45 minutes rather than the whole third level for two and a quarter hours.**

> **The tactical shape: he chooses between being well armed and being available.** **A DM running him should make that choice deliberately rather than default to a full reload.**

> **On the twelve hour night.** **It comes from the guide's daylight of 06:00 to 18:00 and is an illustration, not a fixed property of Barovia: a campaign setting its own sunrise and sunset changes every figure above.** **Travel between K86 and K37 is additional to all of it.**

> **That is a real window, it is the party's reward for a hard fight that did not finish him, and it is the strongest argument in this guide for tracking what he has expended.**

> **Driven off while still corporeal is the cheaper case and the likelier one.** **At midnight he rests until eight, and then owes 9 h 45 m of study, which he cannot begin in daylight.** **He waits for dark, travels up from K86 to K37, and works through that night.** **Either way he cannot be beaten at dusk and come back at midnight with a different book in his head.**

> **So the tables above give rest and study, which is what they are for, and NOT elapsed time.** **Elapsed time adds the wait for nightfall and the journey up from K86.** **No figure is invented here for the journey.**

> **Checked, since it is the obvious objection.** **I6 p.20 describes K37 lit by "a blazing hearth fire" and prints no window, where the adjoining K36 explicitly has "a window in the south wall ... draped with heavy curtains".** **The module notes windows where there are windows.** **It makes no difference.** **Clouds, fog, shutters and curtains give no exemption, and the rest requirement stands whether or not destructive sunlight reaches him (ruling).** **A windowless room above ground does not let him work by day.**

> **The asymmetry a DM should exploit.** **Swapping one first level spell costs him four and a quarter hours. Swapping both fifth level slots costs ten and a half, because the 8 hour rest falls due the moment a fifth level spell is involved however few he is replacing.** **He adjusts at the bottom of his list freely and at the top rarely.**

> **So the conditional form of a guide passage now has a number behind it.** **"If the DM has had him prepare *cloudkill*" means a DM who decided that at least ten and a half hours of game time before the encounter, or seventeen and three quarters if he was changing his whole list, or eighteen and a half if he had been reduced to 0 hit points first.**

**The ruling: the two Crypt 37 scrolls stay in Crypt 37 and do not extend Strahd's spellbook.** I6 places them there and the party finds them there, and the guide does not move published treasure into an antagonist's possession.

**The adaptation, for a DM who wants the tactically maximal Strahd.** Rule that he has taken the two scrolls, and remove them from Crypt 37, because he cannot hold them and leave them there. **Possession is not acquisition.** **DMG p.39**: a magic-user who finds a scroll makes his chance-to-know for each spell, and on success records it in his book, "thus destroying that portion of the scroll". **So the scrolls are destroyed spell by spell as they are learned, not kept.**

**What he could learn.** **Five spells on the scrolls are new to him: *hold person*, *haste*, *lightning bolt* and *slow* at third level, and *charm monster* at fourth.** **Roll d100 once for each, 85 or less to learn it; a failed spell is closed to him for good.**

| Level | Scroll spells he lacks | Checks |
|---|---|---|
| **3rd** | *hold person*, *haste*, *lightning bolt*, *slow* | **four at 85%** |
| **4th** | *charm monster* | **one at 85%** |

**The adaptation yields up to thirty-four spells.** **At 85% a check, expect him to fail about one of the five.**

> **It gives him no extra slots.** He cannot cast more spells in a day and his ceiling in any single round is unchanged. **What changes is selection**, counting a spell as able to fill more than one slot (ruling).

| Level | Slots | Spells before | Spells after | Selections before | Selections after |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **3rd** | **3** | **3** | **7** | **10** | **84** |
| **4th** | **2** | **4** | **5** | **10** | **15** |

> **Eighty-four third level selections instead of ten means he can bring a far better-suited set to a particular encounter, and a DM cannot predict him from the printed list.** The adaptation buys flexibility, which is an increase in effectiveness as well as in unpredictability.

> **For the party, Crypt 37 is the richest magical find in the catacombs**: two scrolls, eight spells between them, and a *wish*. **Expect a magic-user who reaches it to transcribe what they can.**

### 9j. Gaze and charm

**Applies:** whenever Strahd, Helga or another vampire turns its gaze on a character, and while a character is charmed.

- **Hit Dice equate to level,** with additional hit points moving the creature up one Hit Die for each four points: 1+1 to 1+4 becomes 2, 1+5 to 1+8 becomes 3, 2+1 to 2+4 becomes 3, and so on.
- **Most monsters save as fighters,** except that a creature with another class's abilities takes the most favourable score, and a creature with no real offensive fighting capability saves according to its area of ability.
- **Non-intelligent creatures save at a level equal to half their Hit Dice, rounded up,** except against poison or death magic.

**Worked for this adventure:** Strahd, 10 Hit Dice with a magic-user's abilities, takes the more favourable of the fighter 9 to 10 and magic-user 6 to 10 rows in each category: 8 / 9 / 9 / 9 / 10. Helga, 8+3, becomes 9 for saving throws and saves as a fighter of 9 to 10. A wight at 4+3 becomes 5 and saves as a fighter of 5 to 6. A wraith at 5+3 becomes 6 and saves as a fighter of 5 to 6. A worg at 4+4 becomes 5. A ghoul or an ordinary zombie at 2 saves as a fighter of 1 to 2. **A Strahd zombie is non-intelligent at 4 Hit Dice, so it saves as a fighter of 1 to 2, except against poison or death magic, where it saves as a fighter of 3 to 4.**

**Adjustments to saving throws (PHB pp.15 to 17, 22).** **Use the saving throw for the character's class and level, then apply only the bonuses or penalties established for that character and that attack.** **Dwarves, gnomes and halflings gain +1 for every 3½ points of Constitution against rods, staves, wands and spells: Constitution 4 to 6 +1, 7 to 10 +2, 11 to 13 +3, 14 to 17 +4, 18 +5.** **Dwarves and halflings gain the same against poison.** **Elves and half-elves have no general bonus; they have their stated resistance to sleep and charm spells (9j).** **Paladins gain +2 on all saving throws.** Other modifiers come from specific spells, abilities, magic items and circumstances, where their own rules allow them.

**Magic armour (DMG p.81)** does **not** aid saving throws against gas, poison, or spells which cause no physical damage, that is petrification, polymorph, *magic jar* and charm. It **does** aid saves against acid except when immersion occurs, disintegration, falling damage, fire magical or otherwise, and spells which cause physical damage. Metallic armour does not add against electrical attacks; nonmetallic armour does.

**Which blow applies (interpretation, DMG pp.64, 80).** A crushing blow is the item struck by a weighty falling object or a giant's weapon; a normal blow is an attack by a normal-strength opponent. **For a thrown vial of holy water, use the crushing blow column, as the grenade-like missile procedure on DMG p.64 instructs: a vial, on the crystal or vial row, survives on 19 or 20 and breaks on 1 to 18.** **DMG p.80 also describes the normal blow column for a vial hurled against a tough but slightly yielding surface, where it would survive on 14 or better; this guide gives p.64's specific procedure precedence.** **A container specially weakened is an exception p.64 names, with no number printed, so the DM adjudicates it.** Magical items gain +2 on all rolls plus +1 for each plus above +1, and gain +5 against attack forms in their own mode.

#### Zero hit points and recovery (DMG p.82)

**Zero hit points.** A creature brought to 0 is **unconscious**, optionally as low as -3 if the same blow took it there. In each succeeding round it loses 1 further negative point until -10, at which it dies. The loss stops immediately on any round a friendly creature administers aid: binding wounds, starting respiration, administering a draught, or whatever is needed to restore life.

**After revival.** Any character brought to 0 or fewer and then revived remains in a coma for 1 to 6 turns. Thereafter they must rest a full week, minimum, incapable of any activity beyond moving slowly to a place of rest, eating and sleeping. They cannot attack, defend, cast spells, use magic devices, carry burdens, run, study or research. **This holds even if cure spells or healing potions are given**, although a *heal* spell lifts the prohibition.

**Scarring.** A creature that reaches -6 or worse before revival may, at the DM's choice, be scarred or lose a member.

**Recovery of hit points.** Rest is necessary: combat, spell use or similar activity is not rest, and no hit points are regained on such a day. **1 hit point is regained for each day of rest, up to and including 7 days.** A character with a constitution penalty deducts that weekly. After the first week of continuous rest, a character with a constitution bonus adds it to the weekly recovery. **Four weeks of continuous rest restores any character to full strength**, whatever their hit point total.
