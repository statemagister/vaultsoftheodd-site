## 1. Arrival in the village

Part II ended with the party walking into the village of Barovia, tired and short on certainty. The church and the castle were the two sightlines visible above the rooftops. Part III opens at the first cobblestone.

### 1a. The village's geography and the road through it

The village is a small grid of cobbled streets, shuttered houses, and a single active square. The main road runs east to west into that square, and leaves it again heading west and southwest, crossing the river by a bridge on the road to the junction at F (Map 1). A short spur runs north from the square to the church and the cemetery. The party enters along the main road. The square is a few minutes' walk from the first shuttered houses. The church stands on the rise at the north end, against the pillar-stone. The Burgomaster's house lies south of the square, on the street that runs down towards the river. Mad Mary's townhouse is off a side street within earshot of the square.

### 1b. What the square delivers, and what it does not

The sounds of the village are specific and few. The cobblestones underfoot. The fog that smothers carriage noise. The creak of shutters against weather. And from the direction of Mad Mary's house "a single mournful sobbing that echoes through the streets from a distance" (p.8). The tavern's light is visible in the fog from most of the square. The mercantile's sparser light, from behind heavy curtains, is visible from the square itself. Everything else the village contains is either shuttered or absent. With the exception of the tavern and the mercantile, every shop is closed, looted and clawed (p.8).

### 1c. The rules that hold in the village

The valley's standing procedures run in the village as everywhere in Barovia outside the castle. This is their operative form; Part II Section 3 sets out the reasoning. The guide's rulings are marked R, and its inferences from the published rules I.

**Where the people and creatures are.** This Part gives each person's capability where the decision about them is made. **Their complete entries, with statistics, abilities, turning numbers and provenance, are in the Ravenloft Encounter Guide**, which is the series' single reference for every creature and named character. The division is this: where a number is needed to make a choice, it is in the passage that needs it, and the two creatures whose capabilities govern the whole Part, Strahd and Helga, are set out here in full; everything else is one lookup away.

**Barovia's creatures (R).** Fixed and random encounters with the same creature type draw on one finite population, established once before play. Placed creatures are counted first; each random-table entry either draws on that population or, where the source shows it to be a separate group, establishes one, rolled once, before play or when first needed. No population is added twice, and separate groups are not merged. Encounters roll their printed dice, capped at the survivors; confirmed deaths are permanent; survivors reappear with their wounds; a creature type whose population reaches zero gives no further encounter. A tracked creature already engaged in one encounter cannot appear in another until that encounter ends. Summoning calls existing creatures and creates none; undead made by a spell or another stated mechanism are added when, and as, the rule that makes them says. Where a new undead is under its slayer's control, as wights, wraiths and vampires are, the slayer's destruction, before or after the rise, ends that control but does not destroy the new undead, which remains hostile, independent and counted (R); for vampires, MM p.99 itself makes them "free-willed monsters" when the slayer is killed. The ghoul row states no control.

***Raise dead* and the pending dead.** It is a 5th-level clerical spell (PHB p.50), beyond Donavich (C-2, p.9) and any cleric not yet able to cast 5th-level spells; without a cleric able to cast it, it comes only from a scroll or an item (I). Madam Eva, a 10th-level cleric, can cast it ("Fifth Level: true seeing, raise dead", p.11), but "She never gives aid and never needs any" (p.11): she is no source of it (I). The spell restores life to "a dwarf, gnome, half-elf, halfling, or human", not an elf; only up to 1 day dead for each level of the caster; missing parts stay missing; the raised person must survive the resurrection survival roll (PHB p.50, referring to Constitution) and needs a full day of bed rest for each day dead (PHB p.50); under the guide's ruling that is at least one day, and one for each day or part of a day dead (R). A pending ghoul, wight, wraith or vampire victim whom it successfully restores before the rise returns alive and does not rise; if it fails, or cannot affect the victim, the rise stands (R). A ghoul's victim must first be blessed (R, from MM p.44's "blessed and then resurrected"). Once the undead has risen, the spell works on it as the PHB says: newly made undead, other than skeletons, within the caster's days-dead limit "become resurrected dead, providing the constitution permits survival; otherwise, they are simply dead" (PHB p.50); the MM has it destroy a wight and slay a wraith (MM pp.100 and 102). The guide reads the two together: the spell ends the undead state, and the resurrection survival roll decides whether the person returns alive or stays dead (R). Anyone the spell restores returns with 1 hit point, then recovers by the normal rules, and is "weak and helpless" during the bed rest (PHB p.50; the hit points R): under the guide's ruling, conscious, but unable to fight, cast spells, travel or do strenuous work (R). An attack on the shelter does not of itself interrupt the recovery; having to flee or take part in strenuous activity does (R). A drained victim restored before or after the rise returns at the level held immediately before the fatal draining attack; earlier losses stay lost (R). The remains must be accessible to the caster: a buried body is exhumed first (R). An elf risen as a wraith is slain by the spell (MM p.102) but cannot be restored (PHB p.50): the creature is destroyed and the elf stays dead (R).

**Vampires: daylight, 0 hit points and gaseous form.** Vampires "must rest in a coffin or similar receptacle during hours of sunlight unless far beneath the surface of the ground" (MM p.99). Barovia's clouds, fog, shutters and curtains give no exemption (I): above ground, Strahd, Helga and any other vampire do not act by day. Outdoors, daylight counts as exposure to sunlight for a vampire's destruction, even beneath Barovia's "perpetual rolling blackness of thunderclouds" (p.6) (R): "exposure to direct sunlight kills the creature in 1 turn, and it becomes powerless immediately" (MM p.99), in body or gas (I). In a sheltered interior, daylight diffused through curtains is not destructive exposure, nor is daylight filtered through stained glass such as K88's; direct sunlight through clear glass or an open window is, and so is a distinct beam through a small gap where that beam actually reaches the vampire (R). The daytime rest requirement stands whether or not destructive sunlight reaches the vampire (MM p.99), and the MM specifies no additional penalty for a vampire prevented from resting. Daylight runs from 06:00 to 18:00 by the guide's default (R), or from the campaign's own sunrise to sunset. Three clocks stay separate: daylight rest; the 12 turns of a forced retreat; and the 8 hours of recovery from reaching the coffin, which run regardless of daylight. A vampire whose recovery ends in daylight stays at rest unless far underground (MM p.99). Far underground a vampire may remain active, though "they must occasionally return to such rest, for their power is restored by contact with soil from their grave" (MM p.99). Strahd's tomb, K86, lies below the catacombs, which are "buried deep beneath the keep" (p.27): Map 12 shows a stair down to it, and the text gives the teleports between crypt 32 and K86 (pp.27, 29 and 30). The Tome has him "far below Ravenloft" (p.31): there he is far beneath the surface and can conduct his affairs by day (I). A party that seeks a vampire by day can still reach its resting place or underground refuge; daylight does not make it invulnerable there (R). Any vampire brought to 0 hit points "by combat" "is not killed, but it is forced into gaseous form. It must then return to its coffin within 12 turns, rest 8 hours, and reform a corporeal body" (MM p.99). The MM states no consequence for missing the deadline; under the guide's ruling the vampire is then destroyed, and a coffin destroyed before it arrives cannot receive it (R). Before that point a vampire can change form at will, and Strahd, "if he is losing a battle", "becomes gaseous, polymorphs into a wolf or bat, and/or summons other creatures to guard his retreat" (p.3).

*Forced gaseous form (R).* Once forced into gaseous form, a vampire keeps regenerating, but regaining hit points does not release it: it cannot take bat or any other form before it reaches its coffin. Under the guide's rulings, a deliberately restrictive set that the MM does not state, it retreats and does not fight: no physical attack, gaze, spells or summoning. The summoning "to guard his retreat" on p.3 belongs to a retreat he chooses while still able to fight. A cleric can attempt to turn it as a vampire; its form gives no immunity (Turning, below). It is visible as moving vapour where light and visibility allow, so it can be followed, though ordinary fog can hide it.

*Voluntary gaseous form.* A vampire that chooses gaseous form is not bound to the coffin retreat and can resume another form at will (R). A vampire in voluntary gaseous form suffers the same applicable damage as one forced into gas (I; *Magical fire, lightning and whirlwinds*, below). If combat damage reduces it to 0 hit points or below, the triggering attack brings it to 0, and it enters the forced retreat and must reach its coffin within the prescribed 12 turns (R). The excess damage of that attack does not destroy it (R, extending MM p.99's protection at 0 to a vampire already in gas). A subsequent qualifying attack exceeding what it has regenerated since then destroys it under the guide's established gas-damage ruling (R). As a cloud it has no mouth, voice or eyes: it makes no blow, and so no energy drain, uses no gaze, and casts no spell with a verbal component (I). *Polymorph self* is a spell, distinct from the innate gaseous and bat forms (I). Whether it can command a character it has already charmed, or summon, is not established, and is not assumed (held, below).

*Movement and passage (R).* The MM gives a vampire's gaseous form no rate. The guide takes the DMG's *potion of gaseous form* as its model (DMG p.126): the gas flows "at a base speed of 3"/round", and base speed is "tens of feet in the dungeon or similar setting indoors, tens of yards outdoors" (DMG p.66), building interiors counting as indoors (DMG p.68): 30 feet a round inside, 30 yards in the open. "A *gust of wind* spell, or even normal strong air currents, will blow the gaseous form backwards at air speed"; it can "enter any space which is not airtight"; and it "is transparent and insubstantial. It wavers and shifts" (DMG p.126). Unless a *gust of wind* or a strong air current is established, the gas moves under its own power at that rate: ordinary weather neither carries nor hinders it, and no calm is declared to rescue a vampire from a strong wind already established (R). A strong current against its route blows it back at air speed for as long as it lasts (DMG p.126). With no mechanically significant wind established, Strahd and Helga can reach their coffins from anywhere in the village by the face of the pillarstone, and the road is too long to cover in 12 turns (R); an established strong wind, the route and the openings on the night can still defeat them (R). I6's "Wind blows up the shaft" of K18a (p.16) is treated as a strong current (R), so a gas cannot descend it while that holds (R); the other winds I6 describes in the castle are for Part IV. An ordinary closed door, shutter, window or coffin lid is not an airtight seal unless its construction or an established magical effect makes it so: the gas passes through its gaps without opening it, and only a genuinely airtight barrier stops it (R). It does not pass through solid stone, intact glass or other solid material without an opening (R). Passage is not invitation: gaseous form gives no way into a building the vampire has not been invited into (p.6; R). Holy symbols, garlic and protective circles are not general barriers: their own effects apply where their conditions are met, and a circle does not seal a passage. Holy water does not harm a vampire in gaseous form (DMG p.65).

*Magical fire, lightning and whirlwinds.* Any combat damage, a blow or a spell, that takes a vampire in its body to 0 hit points or below brings it to 0 (I): it "is not killed, but it is forced into gaseous form" (MM p.99). Its gas is then harmed as the *potion of gaseous form* is, which "cannot be harmed except by magical fires or lightnings, in which case damage is normal", and "A whirlwind will inflict double damage upon any creature in gaseous form" (DMG p.126; their use for a vampire is the guide's, R). A *fireball* or other magical fire does its normal damage, with its normal saving throw (PHB p.73); a lightning spell does half, as the vampire takes "one-half damage from spells based on cold or electricity" (MM p.99), and other magical lightning the same (R), after the attack's normal saving throw; a whirlwind does its source's damage, doubled (DMG p.126). No other attack damages the gas: a weapon, magical or not, does not (R, on DMG p.126's "cannot be harmed except"), nor does holy water (DMG p.65). MM p.99's protection is against being brought to 0, which has already happened: damage to the gas within the hit points it has regenerated since then only reduces them, and its retreat and 12-turn deadline continue; damage beyond them takes it below 0, and it is destroyed (I). So a gas at 0 that has regenerated 6 hit points and takes 36 from a *fireball* stands at -30 and is destroyed. Whatever brought it to 0, a blow or a spell, magical fire, lightning or a whirlwind that then reaches the gas, in the same round or a later one, can destroy it; the order within a round follows ordinary combat sequencing (I; DMG pp.62, 65 and 66). A sword that strikes the gas after a spell has forced it does nothing. This applies to Strahd, Helga and any other vampire. Halved damage, here as anywhere in the guide, rounds down, with a minimum of 1 when an attack inflicts damage (R; no rule held sets it). The gas keeps the vampire's other vulnerabilities. Direct sunlight destroys it as it destroys the body; that is not hit-point damage and is not counted this way (I; MM p.99). Running water is hit-point damage: "A vampire immersed in running water for 3 melee rounds is killed. The vampire loses one-third of its hit points per round of immersion" (MM p.99); as gas, it applies only once the gas actually enters the water, not when it passes above (I). How that loss is calculated, whether immersion counts as combat, and what follows if the hit points reach 0 before the third round are held (below).

*Recovery.* MM p.99 has a vampire "rest 8 hours, and reform a corporeal body" once it reaches its coffin; the 8 hours run from its arrival (R, on MM p.99). I6 p.30's "If Strahd has been reduced to 0 hit points within the previous 8 game hours, his body is in this coffin" tells the DM where he is found; the guide does not treat it as a different clock (R), though read literally it would have him gone from the coffin up to two hours sooner. Once a forced retreat has reached the coffin, the vampire is a body during its 8 hours' recovery (I: MM p.99 has it "rest 8 hours, and reform a corporeal body", and p.30 puts "his body" in the coffin; neither fixes the moment it re-forms). It cannot leave or resume combat before its recovery ends; disturbed, it still cannot fight, and the 8 hours neither restart nor cost hit points (R). Turning does not force it out of the coffin during those 8 hours (R); that is no immunity to attack or to any other means of destruction. The Fortunes placement that has him "within his coffin, ready to attack at the first sign of someone opening the lid" (p.5) is a starting condition, not recovery after defeat (R).

*Spells after defeat (R).* Recovering from 0 hit points and recovering spells are separate: the 8 hours of recovery restore none of his expended spells and do not count as rest for them (R). Spells he has not cast are kept (I; "Once cast, a spell is totally forgotten", PHB p.40). To regain spent ones he needs the DMG's "complete rest (usually sleep)" for the highest level regained, 4 hours for 1st and 2nd level, 6 for 3rd and 4th, 8 for 5th, and then "one-quarter hour per level of spell" in study for each spell (DMG p.40): *fireball* takes 45 minutes of study after the rest. His ordinary rest, in his coffin or elsewhere far underground, counts as that rest, and a meaningful interruption restarts it (R). Study can be divided between spells: a spell interrupted before it is learned is restarted, and spells already prepared are kept (R). He studies in his study, K37 (I; p.18); K37 is above ground, so he studies there by night (MM p.99; I). Once his physical recovery ends, he can resume activity before regaining his spells; those not regained stay unavailable, and he cannot change his memorised selection without the rest and study for the new spell's level. In the guide's reading, the p.3 list is his memorised repertoire (Part I 4b); I6 names no spellbook for him and no list of alternatives, and spells beyond the p.3 list are the DM's call (R).

Under the guide's rulings, one day means 24 hours from the completion of burial; exhumation stops that clock and cancels the pending rise while the body stays unburied, and reburial starts a fresh 24 hours; an unburied victim has no burial deadline, and a failed *raise dead* on an exhumed body does not make it a vampire; each vampire so made needs its own invitation; and *raise dead* on a pending victim follows the common rule above (R). Victims are recorded as pending, with whether and when each was buried. [Held: what "appropriately strengthed" (MM p.99) gives a new vampire.] |

These are the guide's reconstruction from I6's placements and tables, not a census I6 gives. *Half-strength* is the MM's own term for a new wight or wraith, and the MM does not define it. Under the guide's convention a half-strength creature has half the standard creature's Hit Dice, any fraction and any bonus rounded up, and its hit points are rolled on those reduced Hit Dice; its other abilities are the standard creature's (R).

**Strahd's worgs and bats (R).** The worgs are a persistent population, established once before play: the 25 that guard the woods at C, in packs of 9, 8 and 8, and 4d4 more, rolled once, in packs of no more than 12 (a roll of 13 to 16 makes two packs as near equal as possible). That reads I6's two printed worg quantities together, the force at C (p.8) and the nightly harassment (p.6). A random worg result meets one surviving pack, chosen at random among those not engaged elsewhere, at no more than its survivors, and never several packs assembled to match the roll; Strahd's deliberate deployments may assemble several, each worg's pack recorded before combat so that its death comes off that pack (R). A pack already engaged elsewhere cannot appear in another encounter at the same time (R). The nightly harassment, the worg strike and the force at C roll their printed numbers and draw on the survivors of all the packs, which Strahd can redeploy; no encounter deploys more worgs than remain. The 3,000 bats of K84 (p.27) supply the nightly bats, the 10d10 drawn from the colony's survivors. Killed animals are removed permanently, from whichever group they came. Survivors keep their wounds and heal at the DMG's rate for natural recovery: 1 hit point for each day of rest in the first week, then 7 a week; a day of fighting is not a day of rest (DMG p.82; applied to monsters by the guide, R). A pack can return to an encounter still wounded. The ordinary wolves he summons (MM p.99) are not worgs: summoning calls existing wolves, not replacements for the dead, and I6 gives no count of them. A party that destroys the worgs before it tries to leave meets fewer at C, or none.

**Strahd's reports and strikes**

From the party's arrival in Barovia (R; p.3 gives no starting point), his spies and servants report at dawn, noon, dusk and midnight; each time there is a 60% chance that he knows where the party is, and if he does he attacks wherever it is within two hours, choosing the time and method (p.3). Other than the strikes, such an attack may involve his gaze, spells, summoning, servants or personal combat (I). Under the guide's rulings a report is an opportunity, not an obligation, since I6's own principle is that he "is allowed to make an attack how and where he wants" (p.3); and the reports reach him underground at their printed times; while he rests, his servants hold routine reports until he is available, may wake him for urgent ones, such as the party entering the castle or gaining a dangerous artefact, and raise the alarm at an immediate threat (R). The two-hour window runs from the scheduled report time, even when the report is held; once that window expires, the opportunity lapses (R). Above ground he does not act by day (MM p.99): his servants carry out his orders by day (R), but a personal attack above ground cannot come by day. The DM keeps the report clock privately. Characters concealed where his servants cannot find them, as in a *rope trick*, are not revealed by a report, and characters who conceal themselves after one can be sought at their last known place but not found there while concealed (R).

His three strikes are separate from the reports and the harassment (I), each usable once in the adventure (p.3): he attacks a single PC for five melee rounds, then leaves; he calls 10 to 15 Strahd zombies, but deploys no more than his reserve and any garrisons he withdraws can supply (R); or he calls 3 to 12 worgs, drawn from the survivors. The zombies or the worgs attack together in one place. A strike needs him to know where the party is, from a report or from what he or his servants have learned in play (I; p.3, "Whenever he is aware of the PCs' positions, he is allowed to make an attack how and where he wants"). Outside the first strike, the guide imposes no fixed duration on his combat: he presses an attack or withdraws as his goals and the circumstances dictate (R). Between interventions he is in his Fortunes place, unless forced to his tomb, and the 90% lair chance is not rolled (R). The place sets where he is encountered during the hours he can act; by day he is somewhere far beneath the surface, such as his tomb, and an above-ground place finds him absent (I). Of the reading's places, the Crypt of Sergei (K85) lies with the deep catacombs, and he can be found there by day (I). The Crypt of Ravenovia (K88) has "Great stained windows filling the eastern walls" that "filter dim light into this room" (p.30): it admits daylight, so by day he is absent from it (I), though filtered daylight is not thereby the direct sunlight that destroys him (R; 1c). The K88 reading qualifies p.3's "can always be found" for the hours of daylight.

**His gaze.** He charms a character who meets his eyes; the victim saves against spells at -2 (p.3; MM p.99). Under the guide's rulings he turns his gaze on one character a round, in addition to his blow, and the target first rolls to see whether the gaze is met (MM2 p.55, for a man-sized gaze-user): completely surprised 10 in 10; surprised 8 in 10; viewing him 6 in 10; attacking normally 4 in 10; avoiding his gaze 2 in 10, attacking at -4 while doing so in melee. The gaze needs sight of the eyes: shuttered or planked windows deny it until a breach or an opened door, though his voice, and *suggestion*, still carry (R). An invitation given under a valid *suggestion* counts; a character who understands that admitting him exposes them to a vampire treats the request as obviously harmful, and the *suggestion* fails (R; Part I 4b). *Protection from evil* or a paladin's aura gives +2 on the save against the *suggestion* (R). Net against the *suggestion*: unprotected 0, protected +2, the paladin +4 (the aura's +2 and the paladin's own +2 on all saves, PHB p.22), a UA paladin rolling its 90% first (below). Under *Unearthed Arcana*, magical or magic-like effects on the mind, explicitly including charm and *suggestion*, are 90% unlikely to affect a cavalier, in addition to any saving throw (UA pp.15 and 16); a paladin, as a cavalier sub-class, gains the cavalier's abilities (UA p.16). The 90% is rolled first, then the save (R). A paladin under the Players Handbook alone has no such resistance.

A charmed victim obeys his commands, including handing over possessions and betraying companions, unless an instruction is obviously self-destructive; commands reach the victim within sight, or within 60 feet out of sight, and without a shared language only basic emotions and simple commands pass (DMG p.65). A magic circle of protection suspends his control while the victim is inside it, without ending the charm (DMG p.65). Under the guide's rulings *protection from evil* counts as such a circle while it lasts, and gives +2 on the save against his gaze; a paladin's 10-foot aura does the same for everyone within it, and the paladin's own +2 on all saves applies in addition (PHB pp.22 and 44). Net against the gaze: unprotected -2, protected 0, the paladin +2. **Spellcasting and the gaze.** Casting requires the caster to be relatively motionless and concentrating throughout, and violent movement, such as dodging a blow, prevents it (DMG p.65). Under the guide's ruling he therefore makes no physical attack in a round in which he casts a spell (R; DMG p.65). The DMG names missile discharge, magical device attacks and turning undead as able to occur simultaneously with casting; a gaze is not among them, and under the guide's ruling he does not use his gaze in a round in which he casts (R; DMG p.65). His gaze may accompany his blow, not his spellcasting. A successful hit on him while he casts spoils the spell (DMG p.65). A spell spoiled early in the round still takes the round: he gains no gaze later in it (R). His passive abilities, such as his regeneration (p.3), continue (R).

**Turning.** No cleric below 6th level can turn him; a 6th-level cleric needs 20, a 7th-level cleric 16; paladins turn as a cleric two levels lower (DMG p.75). Each cleric present may try (DMG p.65). A turned Strahd flees for 3 to 12 rounds and can return, to be turned again (DMG p.76). The duration is rolled, not chosen: the DMG gives only the range, and 3d4 is the guide's dice convention (R). Against a vampire in gaseous retreat, a turned gas must "move directly away from his or her person, and stay as far away as possible" for that duration, "moving at full speed for the duration if at all possible" (DMG p.76). The cleric's position fixes its direction; under the guide's ruling it chooses its path within that, around obstacles and through the openings available, and where the direct way is blocked it keeps as far from the cleric as the openings allow (R). The 12-turn clock runs on. When it comes back, the cleric may turn it again (DMG p.76, "subject to further turning by the cleric"; p.65's "only once by each cleric" read as once for each approach, I); a failure ends that cleric's attempts (DMG pp.65 and 76). Once it has reached its coffin, turning no longer moves it (1c, *Recovery*). If turning keeps it from its coffin until the deadline passes, it is destroyed (R, as in 1c). The DMG pages held give no range for turning, and the guide sets none. After a failed attempt "No further attempt by the cleric can be made with respect to the particular undead" (DMG p.76; also p.65). The DMG states no duration for that bar; under the guide's ruling it lasts for the rest of the encounter, and a brief withdrawal followed at once by the same confrontation is not a new encounter (R). Against lesser undead, a successful turn affects 1 to 12 of them (DMG p.76). When he is present with lesser undead that serve him, turning the group may be disallowed if the cleric cannot affect him, never for mindless undead (DMG p.65). Strahd zombies are non-intelligent (p.32) and turned as mummies (p.32): 16 at 5th level, 13 at 6th, 10 at 7th (DMG p.75).

## 2. Walking the village

The order below is the order a party walking in and exploring outward will find the locations. Ismark's offer at E2 can short-circuit that order, and in the module's designed sequence usually does: a party that accepts his offer goes directly from E2 to E4 with him, visiting E1 later or not at all. Both orderings work. The walk pays more. The funnel gets to Ireena faster.

### 2a. E2, the Blood of the Vine Tavern

The tavern is the brighter of the two lit buildings on the square: "A single shaft of light thrusts into the main square" (p.8). For a party new to the valley this is the natural first door. The sign above the gaping doorway hangs askew and proclaims the "Blood on the Vine Tavern"; close inspection shows the original "Blood of the Vine", with the F scratched over with an N (p.8).

Inside is thin. Arik, the barkeep, cleans glasses in a dull rhythm, takes orders in a hollow voice, and ignores questions. Three Rhenari lurk near the door, owners of the tavern who see that customers pay; they are lawful neutral, unlike the clan as a whole, and "otherwise disinterested in the PCs" (p.8). Ismark the Lesser sits in the shadows to the right, sipping wine.

The tavern is a meeting-point, not a place the party learns much by sitting in. Arik gives nothing, the Rhenari give nothing, the patrons cower. What the tavern delivers is Ismark. He is the Burgomaster's son, and to aid his adopted sister, Ireena Kolyana, he greets the party and takes them to the Burgomaster's house (p.8). He is as tight-lipped as any villager unless the subject is Ireena or Strahd; he knows everything the other villagers know, and he knows that "for some unknown reason, Strahd is attracted to Ismark's sister more than anything else" (p.8).

> **Behind the horror.** One possible reading of "Ismark the Lesser" is "his mark": the lesser mark, beside Ireena. I6 carries a good deal of wordplay like this beneath its straight Gothic presentation.

Shown the letter the party received at the start, Ismark confirms that his father did try to send a letter of warning, and that this one is not in his father's hand (p.9). Under the guide's chronology (Part II 2b), the warning went with Markov Gaardovich, the household's servant, who left with it after the Burgomaster died; Ismark and Ireena knew him well. A party that mentions the dead man on the road, or brings his body, finds them recognising him, and learns that the letter never left the woods. ***Speak with dead* on Markov (R):** found several days dead (p.7), he is within the same bands as the Burgomaster (2d). He answers only from what he knew: who sent him, that his master died before he left, that he delayed out of fear, and why he finally went. He did not read the sealed letter and knows its purpose, not its words. He cannot say what became of the household after he left. In the camouflaged opening there was no forgery: the letter from the body is the only letter, and Ismark recognises his father's hand and seal.

**The camouflage and the tavern Rhenari (R).** A party robbed by the Rhenari band on the road arrives with a grievance and finds three Rhenari in the first lit room. They are not the thieves and know nothing of the theft. They defend themselves and their tavern if attacked: three 4th-level fighters, AC 4, with 32, 30 and 24 hit points, hitting for 2 to 7 (p.8). The band itself passed through the village without stopping to trade: Bildrath and the villagers saw wagons going west, not who was in them or where they went beyond that, and their account is no proof that the tavern's Rhenari were involved.

### 2b. E1, Bildrath's Mercantile

Bildrath's is the other lit building on the square, across from the tavern, its light sparse behind drawn curtains (p.8). He sells anything on the AD&D equipment list except "Religious Items", "Livestock" and "Transport", at ten times the normal price (p.8). He trades with the Rhenari when they pass through. Anyone else is an opportunity he prices accordingly.

Every shop but his and the tavern is closed (p.8), so his is the only place there to buy equipment. No holy water or holy symbols are for sale: the PHB lists both under "Religious Items" (PHB pp.35 and 36). Garlic is listed under herbs and mirrors under miscellaneous equipment, so he sells them at ten times the list price: a bud of garlic for 5 silver pieces, a large metal mirror for 100 gold, a small silver one for 200 (PHB pp.35 and 36). Strong garlic makes Strahd hesitate, and a mirror presented boldly turns him aside; neither harms him (1c).

Bildrath "never bargains a price" (p.8): haggling meets a refusal, not a fight. A party that gives him trouble gets Parriwimple, his nephew and stock-boy, a 9th-level fighter with 50 hit points who hits for 5 to 12 (p.8), dedicated to his uncle and unwilling to follow the party anywhere. Bildrath does not offer sanctuary and does not engage with the village's crisis. He serves himself.

### 2c. E3, Mad Mary's Townhouse

The sobbing the party heard from the square leads here. The house is boarded up and barricaded from the inside. Mad Mary sits on the floor of an upstairs bedroom, lost in her sorrow, barely registering visitors. Her daughter Gertruda was hidden in the house her whole life, "broke out of the house and has not been seen since" (p.9). Her mother fears the worst.

Mary fears correctly. Gertruda is in Strahd's Bedchamber at K42, unbitten, because "He is intent on his current plot and is saving her for later" (p.19). A party that notes the mother's name and the missing daughter's will recognise Gertruda when they find her in the castle. The thread does not pay out in the village. It pays out in K42, and the payoff is proportional to whether the party has met the mother.

Her grief gives the party no lead in the village: no one has seen Gertruda since she broke out (p.9).

### 2d. E4 and E5, the Burgomaster's home

The house sits behind a rusting iron fence south of the square. The module's description is specific: gates twisted and torn, one cast aside and one swinging crazily, weeds choking the grounds, claw marks on the walls, great black blottings of old fire, every window barred with heavy planking. A path has been worn in the weeds around the house, as if something has been walking its perimeter (p.9). This is the state of the place Ismark is taking them to.

Ireena will not open the heavily barred door unless convinced that those outside are of no allegiance to the Count, or Ismark is with them (p.9). Inside is well furnished but wearing: holy symbols in every room, windows boarded, and in a side drawing room the Burgomaster, Kolyan Indirovich, lies dead among candles. The stench is the stench of a body kept indoors too long. The guide follows I6's "dead for nearly a week" (p.9), which fits his letter's date (R); it is also the figure the module itself reasons from, deducing in the same paragraph that the letter presented in the tavern "is certainly forged" (p.9). The module's ten-day figures ("over 10 days", p.8; "nearly 10 days", p.9) are printed and not used (R).

Ireena is what the adventure has been pointing to since the Burgomaster's letter first named her. She has been bitten twice. She remembers nothing of her early past and does not know how she came to Barovia (p.9). The great holy symbol that was the household's strongest protection was stolen (p.9). Her father's heart gave out under the constant assault, and since his death the wolves have not attacked the house (p.9). That respite does not extend to the party: Strahd's nightly harassment comes wherever the party spends the night (1c). She is strong-willed, and "will aid the party as best she can in saving herself" (p.9).

**E4 and the invitation (R).** Nothing in I6 says where Ireena was bitten, or that Strahd was ever admitted to the house. It begins closed to him. Its weakness is the loss of the great symbol and his other means: the wolves' break-in roll, his servants, and a voice at the door. Its planked windows deny his gaze a line of sight until a breach or an opened door (1c).

**Ireena's wounds (R).** The bites have no continuing mechanical effect; I6 gives none, and her father's letter that she "languishes and dies" (p.7) is his fear, not a countdown. There is nothing for *cure disease*, *remove curse* or *neutralize poison* to remove. Her printed 6 hit points are her current total, and healing raises her to no unprinted maximum.

**What the household lends (R).** Ismark and Ireena will lend the house's remaining holy symbols for a specific protective purpose, not give the house's protection away. A villager may be persuaded to lend one; none surrenders their own protection casually. The villagers' are lawful good (p.8), which is what turns Strahd aside when presented boldly.

***Speak with dead* on the Burgomaster (R).** Dead nearly a week, he is within reach of a cleric up to 7th level, for 1 round and 2 questions, and of a 7th- or 8th-level cleric for a month, for 3 rounds and 3 questions (PHB p.48; a 7th-level cleric, in both of the PHB's bands, uses the better, R); a delay of a day or two puts him beyond the lower band. He spoke common, the language of his letters (I). He answers from what he knew at death: the attacks on the house, Ireena's bites, the stolen symbol but not who took it, Strahd's interest in her, the warning letter and that Markov carried it, and where he found Ireena as a girl. He knows nothing of Strahd's plans.

***Raise dead* and the Burgomaster, in one place (R).** A party that thinks of raising him needs three things together, and they are set out here so the choice can be made at the house rather than assembled from three sections.
- **How long he has been dead.** This guide follows I6's "dead for nearly a week" (p.9), about six or seven days when the party arrives.
- **What the spell reaches.** *Raise dead* restores a person dead **no longer than one day for each level of the caster** (PHB p.50). **So, on the guide's week reading, he is within the reach of a cleric of about 7th level or better**, and further out of reach with every day the party spends in the village.
- **Who could cast it.** **Nobody in the valley will.** Donavich is a 2nd-level cleric. **Madam Eva can cast it and refuses**, "she never gives aid and never needs any" (p.11). What the party brings with them, or finds, is the DM's to know.

That possibility depends on which of I6's conflicting dates the DM adopts (above), and on the party's available magic. A suitable scroll, magical item, wish or other means of restoring the dead may provide a route beyond the clerics described here. The DM adjudicates the particular effect under the applicable AD&D rules (R).

**Satisfying the time limit is not the whole of it.** The spell's own requirements still apply: the body must be whole, the raised person must survive the resurrection survival roll on constitution, and they are weak and helpless, needing a full day of bed rest for each day dead (PHB p.50; 1c).

***Speak with dead* is a separate spell with separate timing**, and his eligibility for one says nothing about the other. Its bands are below.

**The burial.** Ismark wants his father buried; no one in the village has been brave enough to help (p.9). I6 gives no ceremony and no duration; the guide sets clerical funeral rites at one turn (1c). The cemetery lies behind the church (p.9). The cost is time: a party still at E7 at midnight meets the procession (2f), and the harassment finds it wherever it is that night (1c).

Ireena will accompany the PCs: she has been bitten twice, can no longer be protected in the house, the townspeople avoid her, and the party is her only hope (p.9). **If the party tries to leave her (R),** she argues to come, but she is not a prisoner of the plot: if they insist, she stays, with Ismark, at the church or in the house. Donavich shelters her (R), and she remains free to decide whether to stay. Strahd and his wolves still never attack her directly; nothing else is bound by that, and Strahd pursues his goal through the party and other permissible means (Part I Table 3). The optional ending requires her with the party (p.30).

The guest house (E5) is "not fortified against undead as are most of the other buildings in town" (p.9). The wolves' break-in roll is the same there as anywhere (1c). The difference that matters is sight: E5's windows are not described as planked, so his gaze may reach those inside (I).

### 2e. E6, the Church

The church stands at the far north end of the village, on a rise at the base of the castle's pillar-stone. The module describes it as sagging and worn, its bell tower hanging to one side, light shining through holes burned in the roof (p.9). Inside is a shambles, with overturned benches. At the altar is Donavich, hoarse and weak from a night of prayer.

Donavich is exhausted because "Only nightly prayers have kept the church a place of cleanly sacredness in Barovia" (p.9). The church is not a fortress, but a place whose sanctity holds while it is actively maintained. He has spent "this night, as every night, warding off evil from his church" (p.9). His spells today are *protection from evil*, *sanctuary*, *detect evil* and *remove fear* (p.9); as a cleric he prays for his spells each day, so on later days he may choose other 1st-level spells (I); a *protection from evil* cast on a charmed character suspends Strahd's control for its 6 rounds, 3 rounds a level (PHB p.44; 1c). Holy water is a material component of *bless* (PHB p.43) and one of two ways of tracing the circle of *protection from evil*, the other being burning incense in the air (PHB p.44); of his spells today, only *protection from evil* can use it. Under the guide's ruling he has a modest working supply of holy water, its quantity not fixed, and enough incense for his own *protection from evil* (R). He cannot make more: the DMG's procedure needs a cleric of at least 5th level, a specially made and consecrated font, and *create water*, *purify food & drink*, *bless*, *chant* and *prayer*, the last a 3rd-level spell (DMG pp.114 and 115), and he is a 2nd-level cleric (p.9). He gives holy water to those defending the church or burying the Burgomaster, but not the church's whole store (R). [Held: how many vials he holds.]

He has three pieces of knowledge the party can get nowhere else (p.9). First, that only his nightly prayers keep the church a place of good. Second, a piece of information that reshapes the adventure: there is a book in the library of Ravenloft that might help destroy Strahd, because Strahd "kept meticulous notes on all that he did or said", and some weakness may be written there. This is the village-phase lead towards Strahd's own writings; the party learns of a book, not its name. Third, the origin of Ireena: Kolyan Indirovich found her as a girl at the edge of the Svalich woods near the pillar-stone, and adopted her. Ireena does not know this. Donavich does.

I6 does not say that he offers the party shelter. Under the guide's reading a LG priest who has spent his life holding the church against Strahd does not turn away those trying to save Ireena, which is what makes the hold-out in Section 5 possible; his responsibility for the church also gives him reasons to refuse what would endanger it.

### 2f. E7, the Cemetery

The cemetery is quiet by day. At midnight a procession of one hundred spirits rises from the graves and marches up the road to the castle: to the chapel (K15), up the high tower stair (K18) to the top of the tower (K59), where they throw themselves down the shaft towards the crypts and disappear (pp.9 and 11). The spirits cannot be hit, damaged or turned, have no interest in the living, and will not communicate (p.9).

For the DM: "These are the spirits of previous adventurers who died trying to destroy Strahd. Every night they attempt to complete their quest and each night they fail" (p.11). The party sees only a nightly march of the dead towards the castle. What it means is theirs to work out, and it is the adventure's most direct statement of what failure here costs.

## 3. What the village gives the party

The walk through E1 to E7 hands the party three durable threads.

### 3a. Ismark's proposal

Ismark wants his sister moved somewhere safe and his father buried. It gives the party a named NPC to help and a reason to take the road out of the village. The module gives him no role beyond the village (p.8). A DM who wants a familiar face on the road can have him come along until Ireena is safe, but that is the DM's addition, not the module's. He is a 2nd-level fighter with a longsword and 18 hit points (p.8), not a second party member.

### 3b. Donavich's knowledge

The church is the only place in Part III where the party is told that a specific object in the castle, a book in its library, may hold Strahd's weakness (2e). A party that skips the church does not learn it exists, and has less reason to search the castle rather than run straight at him. His account of Ireena's origin pays out later, when Tatyana returns to the story.

### 3c. Ireena, and the pressure of delay

Ireena has been bitten twice. I6 prints no countdown to a third bite and no deterioration from the passage of nights (2d). What delay gives is time: more nights for Strahd to pursue his goal, which under the Ireena goal is the staged rescue, not a bite (Part I 5b, Table 3, Jack or Queen). Travelling with the party, she changes how Strahd and his wolves can attack it: they never attack her (p.6), so the worgs go around her, and a *fireball* that would catch her is one he does not cast (R). Other creatures are under no such restraint. She is a piece of terrain the party carries, not a shield.

## 4. The cost of staying too long in the village

Most of the village is not a fortress. The church is a separate case (Section 5).

### 4a. Attrition and the purse

The harassment comes every night the party stays anywhere but the castle (1c), whatever the wolves have spared E4 since the Burgomaster's death. It costs more than hit points: the cleric's spells spent after breaches, arrows lost in the dark, sleep lost to the bats and the watches. Replacing what is spent means Bildrath, at ten times the price, with no other supplier and no way to recover the gold. A single trip to outfit the expedition is a cost the party absorbs; a week of trips bites. The party that leaves on day two is in materially better shape than the party that leaves on day five.

### 4b. What Strahd learns

By day three in the village he has had about a dozen reports (1c), on average about seven of them locating the party unless it has concealed itself, three nights of harassment in which to watch which spells come out and in what order, and time to rest and change his spells to answer them (Part I 4b and 4e). The Strahd the party meets in the castle may lead with *protection from good* and *mirror image* because he watched the wizard spend four *magic missiles* on a worg-pack on night two.

## 5. The church as a temporary hold-out

The church is the one position in the village a careful party may reasonably decide to hold: a building Strahd cannot enter uninvited, a cleric on the premises who can accept them (2e), and a roof over Ireena's head that is not her father's house. It has held against the valley longer than any other building the party has seen, sagging and holed though it is.

**What attrition does.** Killed worgs and bats stay dead, and the harassment can field only the survivors (1c), so the packs thin as the party kills what comes at the church. Strahd's three strikes remain separately limited, one use each (1c). "He does not fight the whole party in the open" (Part I 4e), and a church the party holds is that engagement on worse terms for him. The threat to the church is not the frontal assault, but the pressure he brings to bear on the conditions of the sanctuary.

### 5a. What the protection covers, and what it does not

The protection is Donavich's. The church is a place of active sanctity only as long as he prays. He is a 2nd-level cleric with 10 hit points, hoarse and weak the first time the party meets him (p.9). The prayers are not a standing ward. They are active maintenance, nightly, by one exhausted man.

The module does not spell out what the church's sanctity does in game terms. Under the guide's ruling it adds no quantified protection of its own: it matters to the fiction and to the church's sacred character, and the invitation rule applies there as at any building. It establishes that the sanctity depends on the prayers (p.9), and that Strahd cannot enter any building uninvited (p.6). Strahd has strong reasons not to test the church directly while Donavich's prayers hold, and the invitation gives him better indirect tools than a frontal breach. The exterior follows the village's rules (1c): the wolves make their break-in attempt at the doors and windows, and the harassment runs at the church like anywhere else. **The roof (R):** the burned holes are not a breach. They do not by themselves establish that the bats have reached a caster: the casters' check applies indoors only once the bats come in through a breach with the wolves (1c; p.6). The holes do not admit creatures that cannot fly, worgs, zombies and ghouls among them. They give a gaze a line of sight only where Strahd or Helga can actually see a character's eyes through one: position, angle, obstruction and light decide it, and a hole that admits bats does not by that alone; neither crosses the threshold through a hole uninvited. What the invitation rule keeps out is Strahd himself, and under the guide's ruling Helga, the maiden vampire (1c), not the pressure that keeps the party from leaving.

### 5b. How Strahd takes the sanctuary without storming it

Each of these is within his character and capabilities. None requires him to enter the church.

*Attriting Donavich.* Every night of harassment at the church is another night's sleep he does not get. Wolves, bats, fear and the village's suffering work on him as on the party. His hoarser prayers are atmosphere, not a mechanical decline: I6 gives no exhaustion track and no point at which the prayers fail. A lapse, if the DM wants one, is the DM's addition, and works only if its trigger is settled before the party settles in; one invented on the night the party is winning ends the hold-out by fiat.

*Targeting the invitation.* When he comes to the door, he tries to charm characters into inviting him in (p.6). He need not come to the door every night ("Strahd may knock", p.6); each attempt needs his gaze to be met, one target a round, the contact roll and then the save, with a UA paladin's 90% rolled before the save (1c); and through a shut door or boarded window he has only his voice and *suggestion*. A party that keeps him from its eyes and does not open the door denies him the gaze for as long as it holds to that, though not his voice. Many nights in one place give him repeated chances, not a certainty. A character he does charm stays charmed until a further save succeeds (1c): for middling Intelligence, three weeks. Once given, the invitation keeps the church open to him.

*Using the time.* The church changes where Ireena is, not what he wants with her. Under the Ireena goal he needs the party charmed into attacking her so that he can stage her rescue (Part I Table 3); under his other goals she is leverage. Waiting spends his opportunity, not her health.

*Attriting the village selectively.* "He does not destroy the village. He needs it; he terrorises but does not exterminate" (Part I 4e). That leaves the removal of individuals whose absence pressures the party: Mad Mary, Arik, the lone men in the occupied houses, Ismark if he has been left behind. **Two of those are not contests.** **Mad Mary has 1 hit point** and **Arik has 3** (p.8; p.9). Anything that reaches them kills them, so the question a DM runs is never whether they survive an attack but **whether the party learns what is intended in time to prevent it**, and what the loss costs the village's willingness to speak to them at all.

*Starving them out.* Bildrath is the only supplier. Food, oil, torches and arrows run down, and resupply means someone leaving the sanctuary to pay ten times the price.

### 5c. Rest, and what the hold-out costs

Sanctuary and rest are not the same thing. The harassment comes every night (p.6), so overnight recovery is unreliable. Under the AD&D rules, though, recovery is not tied to night: a caster needs "complete rest (usually sleep)" set by the highest spell level regained, four hours for 1st and 2nd level and six for 3rd and 4th, then a quarter hour of study or prayer per spell level (DMG p.40); under the guide's ruling that rest is uninterrupted (R). A party that secures the church, keeps its watches and lets its casters sleep through the daylight recovers before the next night; this is a consequence of the rules, not something I6 states. The encounter checks still run every three turns, and a rolled encounter does not automatically come inside (1c): a passing encounter does not interrupt rest; one that reaches and disturbs the occupants does, and any hostile creatures attempting entry to the shelter, worgs, zombies or ghouls, interrupt it whether or not they break through, a failed strike still being spent (R). At a 1 in 6 every three turns, a 6-hour rest meets about nine chances in ten of at least one encounter result; that is not the chance of an interrupted rest. Strahd receives a daytime report underground, but cannot intervene in person above ground by day; his servants can, on his orders (R). A report is not an automatic interruption, and a worg or zombie strike spent against the church makes its single break-in attempt (1c).

A party that holds the church for several days has learned the valley's rhythm, watched the procession, seen the harassment fail to break in, and taken any strike spent on it off his board. What it has spent is time Strahd can use against Ireena, supplies on Bildrath's terms, and nights in which he studies its watches, saves and spells (Part I 4e). The hold-out is a legitimate strategic choice, not a safe one, and nothing in I6 guarantees that he wins.

## 6. Where Part III stops

Part III ends when the party leaves the village on the west road, with Ireena unless they have persuaded her to stay, and with Ismark if he has chosen to go with them (3a), whether after a single day or after a week holding the church. Part IV takes the party through the road junction (F) to Tser Pool (G and G1), Tser Falls (H) and the carriage at I: the stretch where the Rhenari reveal themselves in full and Madam Eva, at Tser Pool, may give the party a reading (Part I 5c).
