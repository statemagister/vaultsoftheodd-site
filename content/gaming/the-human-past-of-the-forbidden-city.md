*Part 3 of four. Parts 1 and 2 established what I1 owes to the lost-city tradition and what it placed into Hepmonaland. This part asks who the ruined city belonged to before its present factions occupied it.*

*Evidence: I1, both 1981 printings, with the 1980 World of Greyhawk folio only where regional placement bears on the question. Nothing published after 1981 is used as evidence about the module's text. Cook's own later statements are admitted as testimony about 1979 to 1981, dated and marked as retrospective wherever they appear.*

---

## Nobody can be securely named

I1 gives several kinds of evidence about who the people of the Forbidden City were, and they do not agree.

In the notes for the DM, the mongrelmen are descendants of slaves once kept in the city. In area J, the ruins they occupy are described as all that remains of the original human inhabitants, now reduced to cunning and ruthless mongrelmen. In one of the suggested adventures, the ancient sewers beneath the city contain jungle-ghouls and the last human descendants of the ancestors of the yuan-ti.

Those are not three versions of the same genealogy.

Slaves are not founders. A founding population reduced to a scavenging remnant is not the same thing as a servile population surviving its masters. And the last human descendants of the ancestors of the yuan-ti introduce another surviving line again.

Only the first two actually conflict. Both are said of the mongrelmen, and they cannot both be true. The sewer line is separate: compatible with the account of slaves, and at odds with area J only if the ancestors of the yuan-ti were the city's original inhabitants, which the module never says.

The text contains a mechanism that could connect the account of slaves to the yuan-ti line. The yuan-ti capture mongrelmen as slaves and use them in breeding programmes. That means the module contains machinery capable of joining servitude to yuan-ti descent. It never performs that join itself.

The physical evidence does not decide between them. At C9 there are strange frescoes, half-covered with fungus, painted on broken slabs; their content is never described. The tombs of ancient yuan-ti are not keyed remains but one suggestion for what a referee might place beyond a collapsed passage, offered alongside an underworld of giant mushrooms and a race older than man. The sewer population likewise appears only in a suggested adventure. Area J contains rubble walls, pits, lean-tos, tents and thatched huts, but nothing there identifies the builders of the ruins.

No genealogy gains a material tiebreaker.

One source inside the city speaks to the question directly, and it is not material. Captives brought to the mongrelmen's leader at J5 are told the history of the mongrelmen by a chanter: a long and solemn epic, performed in a sing-song mixture of broken Common and animal cries, which the module calls ridiculously heroic in parts while other parts are confused nonsense. Cook never gives its content.

What he gives is its existence and its character, and both matter. The mongrelmen keep a history of themselves, performed ceremonially enough to need a chanter and heard solemnly enough that mockery is punished. That is collective memory, transmitted. And the module marks it as a source whose claims need judgement rather than dismissal: an epic can preserve real events while exaggerating them, confusing them, or making them grander than they were. It does not settle the genealogy either. It establishes something the conflicting descriptions cannot, which is that the people living in the ruins hold an account of their own past.

The contradiction is not a problem awaiting reconstruction. It is the state of the published text.

## Nobody is simply gone

What I1 cannot tell us securely is whose descendants survive. What it says about those survivors is much more consistent.

The yuan-ti are explicitly creatures who were once human. Their own dark and unknown practices fouled their blood and produced a degenerate and corrupt race. The result is graded. Purebloods remain human enough to pass as such most of the time. Halfbreeds combine human and serpentine forms in varying proportions. Abominations retain least of the human shape.

The mongrelmen are described differently but on the same axis. In-breeding and association with the other creatures of the city have left them with only a trace of their original humanity. Some can still look human. Others are diseased, jungle-mad or bestial.

The suggested sewer population adds a third route. Humans living beneath the city wage a constant war of attacks, sallies, counter-attacks and sieges against jungle-ghouls, which attempt to transform them into twisted slime-beasts with their cancerous touch. Those who have not transformed are nearly blind after centuries underground and hate anyone who brings light into their world.

Three mechanisms. Three agents. One repeated result.

Human stock remains in the city by degrees.

That is the stable fact beneath the incompatible genealogies. I1 repeatedly refuses simple disappearance. Whatever ancestry is chosen, the human past survives as transformation.

## Transformation belongs to the humans

The pattern becomes clearer when the non-human factions are placed beside them.

The tasloi are native to the jungle of the area. They ally themselves with the inhabitants of the city for food and protection.

The bullywugs are immigrants. They came to the ruins many years ago after being driven from other lands, bringing with them a small "god-egg", in the module's own quotation marks: their word for it, not a stated relationship to anything else in the city.

The bugbears are simply there. The module gives the band no origin at all.

None of those populations is described as degenerating. None is graded according to how much of an earlier condition remains. None is becoming another kind of creature. They are native, immigrant or unexplained, but they are stable.

The human-descended populations are not.

The yuan-ti have transformed themselves through their own practices. The mongrelmen have changed through in-breeding and association with the other inhabitants of the city. The sewer humans are changed by infection, darkness and prolonged isolation.

Every human population I1 describes is somewhere on a path away from ordinary humanity, and each path is different.

That makes degeneration more than a peculiarity of the yuan-ti entry or an explanation for why mongrelmen look as they do. It is one of the organising patterns of the module's human past.

There is a secondary alignment pattern beside it. I1 assigns lawful neutral to the creature type it elsewhere uses for the city's degraded human remnant, while assigning chaotic evil to the once-human yuan-ti, who in the city assume the position of lords, attempting to direct activities and maintain their power. Those alignments first appear with these creatures in I1 rather than being inherited from an earlier monster book.

That does not mean the builders of the city were lawful and their successors chaotic. The lawful neutral alignment belongs to the mongrelman species entry, not specifically to the inhabitants of area J, and alignment in I1 is representative rather than binding. Mongrelmen may assist a party under some circumstances, Shruzgrap can betray his own allies, and the referee is explicitly given latitude over how the pan lung behaves.

The alignment contrast is a design choice visible in the module, not a genealogy and not an explanation. The transformation pattern needs no such qualification. It is stated directly of all three human-descended populations.

## It is still happening

The most important consequence is that this is not merely the history of a ruined city. The process has not ended.

The yuan-ti capture mongrelmen for slaves and for their breeding programmes. The bugbears hunt them for food. The bullywugs use them for food and sacrifice when other supplies are scarce.

One degraded human population is being acted upon by another in the module's present tense.

The phrase "breeding programmes" matters because it turns the city's history into an active mechanism. The yuan-ti are not simply the product of some forgotten corruption, occupying a ruin beside another degraded people. They are deliberately doing something to mongrelman stock while the player characters are there.

The module's past is still producing its present.

That sits beside another pattern. Two projects look towards restored power. Horan, who is responsible for the increased raiding, is attempting to unite the yuan-ti's disorganized bands and plans a new empire of his own, leading the yuan-ti meanwhile with promises of regained evil power and glory. The bullywugs are trying to rebuild their race and hope one day to drive every other creature from the city, co-existing for now. And the module notes that Horan's promises to the bullywugs are much the same as those he makes the yuan-ti.

The mongrelmen have no comparable project. They are described as vindictive and full of hate because of the way the other inhabitants treat them, and they survive partly by hiding and stealing. But not only by that. They gamble with visitors, trade information and service for coin, and will barter with parties that are not hostile. And they keep the epic.

So the ruins contain two projects looking towards restored power, and one population that seeks no restoration but hides, trades and remembers.

The yuan-ti's part in that is particularly strange. The power Horan promises them is regained power, which implies they once held it, and they are a people the module simultaneously describes as the result of human degeneration, while the same faction continues a breeding programme involving another degraded human population.

Restoration and transformation are happening together.

## The human past that remains

The Forbidden City's human past cannot be reconstructed into a single genealogy from I1. The text says two incompatible things about the mongrelmen, describes a separate human line in the sewers, and gives the mongrelmen an epic of their own that it marks as partly heroic and partly nonsense. Neither the keyed ruins nor the optional material resolves them. Any attempt to name one population as the city's definitive founders has to choose evidence the module itself does not choose.

But the contradictions conceal a much more consistent pattern.

The human populations do not disappear. They persist in altered forms. The yuan-ti retain humanity by degree. The mongrelmen retain a trace of it. The sewer population is either becoming something else or living on the edge of transformation. Non-human populations arrive, migrate or simply inhabit the ruins without undergoing the same process.

And in the module's present, that process is still being imposed.

The 1980 folio offers one further possibility but not a solution. It places Suloise refugees in Hepmonaland and names no earlier population there. I1 supplies humans but never identifies them as Suel, and it never dates the construction of the Forbidden City against the migration. Connecting one or more of its surviving human lines to the Suel is therefore possible, and probably the simplest extension available to a referee who wants one, but it remains an extension.

The published module stops earlier.

It cannot securely tell us who the humans of the Forbidden City were. It is much clearer about what happens to humans who remain there, and about what they keep: a war, an exchange, and a history of their own.

**Later testimony.** In a 2012 Star Frontiersman interview, Cook described creature design as beginning with a goal or theme rather than with the creature itself, and discussed the creatures made for Dwellers in connection with its Conan and Burroughs lost-city conception. That supports treating the module's distinctive populations as thematic design rather than an accidental collection of monsters. It does not establish that the particular pattern identified here, human degeneration and transformation, was itself the intended theme.

## Questions the text leaves open

*Some could still be answered by David Cook or other contemporary testimony. Others belong to the referee, because the published module does not settle them.*

**For David Cook, or contemporary testimony**

> Did you have a particular answer in mind for who the original human inhabitants of the Forbidden City were?

> The module variously describes the mongrelmen as descendants of slaves, the area J mongrelmen as the remains of the original inhabitants, and the sewer humans as descendants of the yuan-ti's ancestors. Were those intended to fit together, or are they remnants of different stages of development?

**For the referee**

> Which account of the mongrelmen's origin, if either, is true in this campaign?

> If more than one survives, how are the mongrelmen, the yuan-ti ancestors and the sewer humans related?

> Horan plans a new empire and promises the yuan-ti regained power. Did they once hold power in this city, and if so, over whom?

> The mongrelmen's own epic is heroic in parts and confused in others. What in it is true, and what has it forgotten, inflated or invented?

> Horan makes the bullywugs promises much the same as those he makes the yuan-ti. What happens when either discovers how alike they are?

> If the folio's Suel population is connected to the city, is that ancestry known to anyone in the present, or wholly lost?

*Source note. I1 passages were collated across both 1981 printings of TSR 9046, identified by back cover: the first printing carries the ISBN alone, the second adds the product number 394-51424-6TSR0550. All were found textually identical. The mongrelman, tasloi and yuan-ti are first published in I1; their alignments originate there rather than being inherited, Monster Manual II being two years later. Folio passages were checked against the 1980 folio rather than the 1983 Guide; it names Hepmonaland twice, in the migration account and the Lordship of the Isles entry, and supplies no population for the region other than the Suel. Cook's 2012 remarks are from his interview in Star Frontiersman 18, August 2012, from p.3; retrospective testimony, dated as such. One qualification on the degenerate-human motif: the Fiend Folio's bullywug entry already rumours viable bullywug-human crossbreeds and degenerate humans dwelling in mixed communities, and I1 makes bullywug ancestry one possible component of a mongrelman. The association of these creatures with degraded human stock is therefore inherited. The three transformation mechanisms discussed above are I1's own.*
