Walk towards the horizon and it moves.

Not because it is running away from you, and not because you are walking badly. It moves because it was never a place. It is a line defined by where you happen to be standing, so it recalculates itself with every step, and it will still be exactly as far away when you stop.

Enough behaves like that, and it is worth understanding why before deciding it says something unflattering about you.

## The question that cannot be answered

Ask yourself how much money would be enough and notice what the question actually requires.

Enough for how long. Growing at what rate. With prices rising by how much. Assuming what happens to your health, your work, your rent, the people who depend on you. Every one of those is unknowable, so the sum has no answer, and a question with no answer does not sit quietly. It comes back.

What arrives instead of an answer is a feeling, and the feeling always does the same thing. It sits slightly above wherever you are.

That is what makes it so demoralising. Save more and it moves. Earn more and it moves. The target that was going to let you stop worrying turns out to be attached to you, at a fixed distance, like the horizon.

And then a perfectly reasonable conclusion follows: that saving does not work, because you have done it and nothing has changed.

## Change the unit

Here is the thing that made the difference for me, and it was not discipline.

I stopped asking how big the pot needed to be and started asking what income it would produce.

That sounds like a small change and it is not, because one of those questions can be answered and the other cannot. A pot has to be measured against a lifetime you cannot see. An income can be measured against a month you already know very well, because you have been paying for it for years.

Notice what has happened to the difficult question. It has gone.

You are no longer asking whether a lifetime of savings will be enough. You are asking whether one month's income covers one month's life. That is a comparison, not a prediction, and it uses two numbers you already possess.

It is also available to somebody who is certain they are bad with money, because there is nothing to work out. It is bigger or smaller.

## What I actually did

My own version was that the dividends from what I had put aside should pay something like an ordinary salary. Not a comfortable one. An ordinary one.

It took a long time and I got there.

And I still am not certain it is enough.

I want to be honest about that rather than tidy it away, because tidying it away would make this article a lie. Writing the number down did the thing it promised: the target stopped moving, and I could tell whether I was above or below it. What it did not do was remove the uncertainty. It gave me a fixed point to be uncertain about, which is a real improvement and is not the same as peace of mind.

If somebody tells you that a written number will make you feel safe, be careful with the rest of what they are selling.

## Where the analogy breaks

The horizon never moves closer. A number can.

Your first figure will be wrong. Mine was. That is not a failure of the method. It is the method. You write down a stake in the ground so that you have something to revise, rather than a feeling that revises itself without telling you.

An income target can also move on its own, because the income is not a promise. What pays you this year may pay less next year. Prices rise while the figure stays still. Neither of those means the exercise was pointless. They mean the number needs looking at occasionally rather than never.

And having a number is not the same as having the money. This article cannot help with the second thing. What it can do is stop the first thing from being impossible. A target you can see is the difference between saving towards something and simply not spending.

## One thing to do

Do not start with the whole of your life. That is the version of this question that has no answer.

Pick one thing you are quietly worried about. A month without work. Christmas. The thing that needs replacing and has not broken yet. Whatever it is that sits at the back of your head and gets vaguer every time you look at it.

Then give it a figure. Not the right figure, because there is no way to know that yet. Just a number, written down, that you can be above or below.

You will have converted a worry into a question, and a question is something you can actually answer.
