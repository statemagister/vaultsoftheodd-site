We were away when the airline went bust.

Not a delay, not a cancellation. The company stopped existing, and the flights we had booked months earlier stopped existing with it. Somewhere at the back of my head was the assumption that this was now a problem between me and a company that no longer had a phone number, an office or any money.

One thing first. This is not an argument for borrowing on a credit card.

This article is about how you pay for something you can already afford. Putting a holiday on a card and clearing it that month, and putting a holiday on a card and paying it off over two years, are different acts with the same piece of plastic. Everything below assumes the first.

## What actually happened

I had paid for the flights with a credit card.

So I rang the card company, more in hope than expectation, and discovered something I had not known. They refunded the cost of the flights. They also offered to arrange replacement flights home, which we turned down, because we thought we could find something cheaper ourselves, and we did.

The airline had gone. The obligation had not.

## There was a third party all along

Here is the thing I had never understood about paying by credit card.

When you buy something with a card, there are not two parties to the purchase. There are three. You, the company you bought from, and the company that provided the credit. And in law, for purchases of a certain size, the card provider carries the same responsibility as the seller if the thing goes wrong or the seller disappears.

That is why the card company could refund flights it had never operated. Not out of goodwill, and not because they are nice. Because they were equally liable for the purchase from the moment they paid for it on my behalf.

## What this is called

The protection is called Section 75, from the Consumer Credit Act.

It applies where the thing you are buying costs more than £100 and no more than £30,000, and where at least part of it was paid for with a credit card. The card provider is then equally responsible with the seller if there is a breach of contract or a misrepresentation, or if the company fails.

Two details make it more useful than it first appears.

You do not have to put the whole cost on the card. Paying the deposit by credit card can bring the whole qualifying purchase within it, which matters for holidays, kitchens, cars and anything else where the deposit is the small part.

And there is no equivalent for a debit card. What you get there is called chargeback, which is a different animal. It can apply to purchases of any size, which Section 75 cannot, but it is a set of rules operated by the card schemes rather than a right in law, so it is not guaranteed in the same way, and there is a time limit, usually around 120 days.

That difference is the whole reason this article exists. Same holiday, same money, two ways of paying, and one of them puts a legally responsible second party behind the purchase.

## Where the analogy breaks

The card company is not standing behind everything you buy.

The amount is per item, not per bill. More than £100 has to be the cost of the thing itself or of a set sold together. A weekly shop of £180 is not covered because it adds up past the line. And on the specific case of flights, a family ticket bought as one thing can count as one item, while four separate tickets each under the limit may not. That is a nasty edge for exactly the sort of booking this article is about.

More than £100 also means more than. Exactly £100 does not qualify.

The relationship between you, the card and the seller has to be direct. Buy through a marketplace, a booking agent or certain payment intermediaries and that chain can break, because the company you paid may not be the company supplying the thing. It is worth knowing before booking rather than afterwards, since the same holiday bought two ways can be covered or not.

It is not insurance. It covers a purchase that goes wrong, not a decision you regret, and not a company that delivers exactly what it promised while you wish it had not.

And it does not make a credit card free or safe. Everything true about credit card debt is still true. This is a reason to pay by card, not a reason to borrow on one.

## One thing to do before the next article

Think about the next thing you buy that costs more than a hundred pounds and is not delivered immediately. A holiday, a sofa, a kitchen, a car, a course, tickets for something months away.

Ask one question before you pay. If the company supplying this disappeared before I received it, who else would be responsible?

If you would be on your own, and you can clear the balance, paying at least the deposit by credit card changes the answer. That is not a trick and it costs nothing when the card is cleared. It is simply a different way of paying for the same thing.
