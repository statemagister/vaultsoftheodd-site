*This hostile read was conducted against the frozen 10,182-word version of the paper on 30 August 2026. The paper was not altered while the read was in progress. Each attack began from the assumption that the central reconstruction was wrong, and was given the strongest case that could be constructed before a verdict was reached. The record below preserves both successful and unsuccessful attacks, including one apparent counterexample subsequently defeated by the primary text.*

---

## Attack 1. Explanatory compression: is Truth doing work, or is it an elegant name for two unrelated habits?

**The deflationary hypothesis, at its strongest.** There is no unified phenomenon here. There are two independent authorial habits.

D1: Math's magic is materially efficacious rather than illusory. This is a common enough way to characterise a powerful magician, and it needs no theological explanation. It also explains Aranrhod, since her false claim is exposed by the rod, which is an instance of D1 and not of any separate rule about speech.

D2: nobody successfully lies to Math. But this may be an artefact of the plot rather than a fact about the figure. Math is stationary and almost the entire action happens away from him. The cast that addresses him at all is tiny: a nephew who is being granted everything he asks for and therefore has no need to lie, a victim reporting truthfully, two culprits submitting, messengers relaying terms, and Aranrhod. Absence of successful falsehood may simply be absence of occasion.

On that account the directional symmetry is real but idle: two habits that happen to point in opposite directions, unified by our choice of a word.

**This is the strongest attack in the set and it cannot be dismissed cheaply.** The move that gives it teeth is the claim that Aranrhod collapses into D1, which would leave the speech-strand with no positive instance at all, only a bounded absence. Two distributions would become one distribution and one silence.

**Where it fails.** Three things the deflationary account does not explain and cannot cheaply acquire.

It does not explain Gwydion. D1 and D2 are claims about Math; neither says anything about his nephew's conduct. Yet the same man, in the same story, lies fluently to Pryderi and repeatedly to Aranrhod and never once to Math, and in the one place where a lie would have served him he tells the truth and withholds instead. On the deflationary reading this is coincidence. Coincidence is a permitted explanation, but it is a cost, and it is being paid to avoid a rule that predicts the behaviour.

It does not explain the sequence at the rod-test. If Math simply needed a virgin and possessed a reliable instrument, the natural narrative is to test. What the branch gives is question, answer, then test. The claim is elicited before it is adjudicated, which is the shape of a falsehood being met rather than a condition being checked.

And the durability contrast is not merely present but stated, in the text, by the illusionist, at line 96. The story is conscious of the difference between his magic and Math's. That is harder to write off as two unrelated conventions when one of the characters articulates the distinction himself.

**Verdict: the attack fails against the core and succeeds against one strand.** See Attack 2.

## Attack 2. The tragic limit is not evidence, and the paper treats it as though it were

**The attack.** Section 4 presents Math's inability to undo the rape as intrinsic to his nature: he can change what is and not what was, because to make a thing that happened appear never to have happened would be the one lie Truth cannot tell. The convergence paragraph then counts this limit as one of its strands.

But nothing anywhere in the Four Branches undoes a past event. Rhiannon's penance is served, not erased. Pryderi and Rhiannon are recovered from Llwyd's enchantment, which lifts a state rather than reverses an act. Lleu is brought back from the edge of death, which is a restoration and not a reversal. Branwen's war cannot be unfought. Time-reversal is simply not an operation the corpus performs, for anyone.

So Math's inability is not distinctive of Math. It is a property of the genre. Attributing it to his nature is unfalsifiable in the worst way: no possible narrative would have shown otherwise, because no narrative in this literature shows otherwise for anybody.

**Attempted defence, and it does not hold.** One might say that the branch dwells on the failure, that it marries Goewin and gives her authority and then loses her from the story, and that the dwelling is the evidence. But that shows the author registering a moral cost, which is a fact about the tale's ethics, not about the ontology of its magician.

**Verdict: the attack succeeds.** The limit cannot be counted as evidence for Truth. It remains a coherent implication of the reading, but it is a consequence drawn from the thesis rather than a fact supporting it.

**Required repair.** The convergence count drops from four strands to three: the speech-distribution, the instrument-distribution with the durability contrast as its second evidential form, and the restorations. Section 4 must be reframed so that the limit is presented as what the reading implies and not as something the branch establishes, and the sentence in Section 5 listing Truth limited among the anchored claims must be corrected, since it currently says only the last is substantially interpretive while treating it as part of the same evidential series.

## Attack 3. Find a successful false assertion to Math

**One candidate was missed by the initial audit.**

At lines 46 to 47, Math warns Gwydion that Pryderi may refuse him, and Gwydion answers *Nit drwc uyn trawscwyd i, Arglwyd*, my errand is not bad, lord. The sweep classified this true, reading *trawscwyd* as the mission's prospects: my errand is not a poor one, I have a fair chance.

It will also carry the moral sense. My errand is not a bad one, said by a man who intends to defraud his host of Otherworld swine under an oath, provoke a war, and vacate his uncle's court so his brother can commit rape. On that reading it is a false assertion made to Math's face which is never exposed, and it is precisely the falsifier the paper states in advance.

**Verdict at the time of the read: the attack lands a hit, though not a kill.** The line looked genuinely ambiguous and the ambiguity was lexical rather than manufactured. The count would have had to carry a marked doubt.

**Resolution, and the attack is withdrawn.** The repair was to begin by establishing what *trawscwyd* can bear. It can bear only one thing here, and the branch settles it internally. The word occurs twice, thirty lines apart, in the same episode. At line 75, after Gwydion tells his companions that the swine will not be got by asking, they reply *pa drawscwyd y keir wynteu*, by what *trawscwyd* shall they be got. A device, a stratagem, a means. It cannot mean harm there, and both manuscripts agree at both places.

So *nit drwc uyn trawscwyd i* is a claim about the quality of a plan and not about its morality: my stratagem is not a bad one. No interpretive rescue is therefore required. The moral reading is dead on the text's own evidence, and not by any narrowing of *drwc*, appeal to Gwydion's sincerity, or carved exception. All three were available. All three would have been special pleading. The hypothesis survived without any alteration to the rule used to test it.

The finding is stronger after the attack than before it. Gwydion does not deceive Math with an innocuous true sentence. He tells Math, in a word that means device, that he has a device, and Math permits him to go without asking what it is. The distinction between false assertion and withheld content is sharper for it.

**No repair required.** The speech distribution stands exceptionless: of sixteen assertions to Math, fifteen true, one false and undone in the act.

## Attack 4. Find a wand occurrence the inventory missed, or a false seeming produced by it

**Nothing found.** The count of ten was reached twice by different methods, once by eye through Hughes and once by regular expression through the Gruffydd text, and no other rod-word occurs. No operation produces an appearance the narrative marks as not so.

**But a precision fault.** The paper speaks of "his characteristic instrument" and of "Math's wand." At line 597 the instrument is in Gwydion's hands, restoring Lleu from the eagle. If the rule concerns Math's use of the wand, that occurrence lies outside it and the operative count is seven, not eight. If it concerns the wand as an object, the possessive language is loose. The finding survives either way, since both instances produce actuality, but the claim therefore needs to distinguish ownership from operation.

**Verdict: no kill. One imprecision to fix.**

## Attack 5. Find the scholar who got there first

**Nothing found.** Searched by component rather than by thesis. The field reads Math as a magician-king, reads the branch as a study in deception, and reads his magic as materially effective; nobody has proposed that the pattern of falsehood around him is a nature. The nearest approaches are Bromwich and Hughes on the contest in magic, and McKenna on the durability contrast, both of which the paper already cites and builds past.

**Caveat, and it should be stated rather than glossed.** This was a search of accessible English-language scholarship and bibliographical resources. It is not a specialist sweep of CMCS, BBCS, Studia Celtica and THSC back-runs, nor of Welsh-language scholarship, which is a real gap given that the paper is about a Welsh text and its author does not read Welsh. The claim that the reading is novel should therefore be made in the form the evidence supports: I have not found a precedent, rather than there is none.

**Verdict: no kill. The novelty claim must be worded to the strength of the search actually run.**

## Attack 6. Find the place where compatible-with-diminution becomes therefore-a-god

**Checked the Mathonwy chain in Section 3 and the closing moves in Section 5.** The Mathonwy argument is properly stepped: Davies establishes the doublet, the doublet is compatible with genealogisation, genealogisation is what the diminishment frame would predict, and the paper says explicitly that a doubled name may arise by literary genealogy, scribal interpretation or narrative convention without preserving the demotion of a god. No link impersonates the next.

Section 5 separates the levels and the strong claim is marked as conviction.

**One soft spot.** Section 3's opening move from "kingship is the most transmitted-through element" to the reconstruction leans on the availability premise, which is now well cited from McKenna, but the paper nowhere states what would count against the diminishment frame itself. Every other major claim in the essay names its falsifier. This one does not, and after note 5 has been strengthened with McCone the asymmetry is more visible than it was.

**Verdict: no kill, but a gap in the paper's own discipline.**

## Attack 7. Does the Irish comparison mislead?

Dillon is now bounded in note 10 to what four pages can establish, and the *fír flathemon* material was demoted when the reconstruction stopped admitting kingship. The remaining risk is the Act of Truth being felt to support more than compatibility, and the prose is careful.

**Verdict: no kill.**

## Summary

One attack landed. One was raised, pressed, and defeated by the primary text. Nothing found kills the argument.

**The limit is not evidence, and has been withdrawn.** The convergence is three strands, not four. Nothing anywhere in the Four Branches undoes a past event, for any figure, so Math's inability to do so does not distinguish him. Section 4 is reframed as a consequence of the reading rather than support for it. This is the significant result of the read, and it withdrew one of the paper's strongest interpretive strands.

**The proposed counterexample at line 46 was withdrawn on the evidence**, after the branch's second use of *trawscwyd* thirty lines later settled the sense. The speech distribution stands exceptionless.

**Three lesser faults, all repaired.** The wand claim now separates ownership from operation, since the eighth operation is Gwydion's; the novelty claim is worded to the search actually run and states that no precedent was found rather than that none exists; and the diminishment premise is given a defeater rather than a textual falsifier, on the ground that a premise cannot be tested by the evidence whose interpretation it conditions.

The core survives the compression attack because the deflationary alternative must treat Gwydion's differential conduct as coincidence, and because the branch states the durability rule in its own voice. The paper that emerges claims less than the one that went in.

---

*Outcome: one evidential strand withdrawn. One apparent falsifier raised and defeated by internal textual evidence. Three lesser claims narrowed or corrected. Central reconstruction survives.*
